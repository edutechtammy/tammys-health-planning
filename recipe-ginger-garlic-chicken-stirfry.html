Ginger-Garlic Chicken Stir-Fry with Cabbage & Mushrooms

The full built-out version of the stir-fry listed in your Tuesday meal plan. Thinly sliced chicken seared at high heat, then stir-fried with ginger first (more heat-stable than garlic), followed by mushrooms and cabbage. A splash of ginger bug liquid deglazes and carries the compound profile through the dish. Garlic finishes off-heat, as always. Cabbage is a prebiotic glucosinolate source with direct DP gut-GLP-1 relevance; mushrooms contribute beta-glucan (also GLP-1 pathway), ergothioneine (APOE4 antioxidant), and Vitamin D precursors. Whole ingredients only — no sauce packs, no cornstarch, no soy sauce (GF tamari or coconut aminos instead). Twenty minutes, entirely from your existing pantry.

⏱ Prep: 8 min 🔥 Cook: 12 min ⏳ Total: 20 min 🍽 Yield: 2 servings

🔍 Why cabbage and mushrooms — not just any vegetables

🥬 Cabbage — glucosinolates + prebiotic Cabbage contains glucosinolates (converted to indole-3-carbinol and DIM by gut bacteria), which upregulate phase II liver detoxification enzymes. Also a significant prebiotic fibre source — the fermentable fibre feeds Bifidobacterium and supports overnight GLP-1 for DP. Low oxalate. Zero glucose impact.
🍄 Mushrooms — beta-glucan + ergothioneine Mushrooms are the richest dietary source of ergothioneine — an antioxidant compound that accumulates selectively in mitochondria and is being studied for APOE4-related neurodegeneration. Beta-glucan from mushrooms also stimulates intestinal L-cells for GLP-1 secretion (same DP pathway as asparagus inulin and kefir). UV-exposed mushrooms also provide Vitamin D precursors.
🫚 Ginger before garlic in the wok Gingerol and shogaol (fresh ginger's active compounds) are released into oil under heat. Garlic's allicin is destroyed under heat. The solution is sequential: ginger goes in with hot oil (30–45 seconds), then the wok is pulled off heat and garlic goes in last. This is the same principle used in every recipe in this library, applied to the stir-fry format.
🫧 Ginger bug liquid as the deglaze Your ginger bug fermentation liquid contains dissolved ginger compounds, trace B vitamins, and mild organic acids from the fermentation. Using it as the stir-fry deglaze rather than water adds those compounds to the sauce while lifting the caramelised residue from the wok. Water works as a substitute — but ginger bug liquid is already in your kitchen and costs nothing extra.

Chicken + vegetables alone are negligible GI. If serving over millet or quinoa, food-test that combination — run T0/T30/T60/T120 on your first full bowl. The stir-fry itself does not require testing.

🧾 Ingredients — 2 servings
  • Protein
  • 2Chicken breasts, thinly sliced⬜ zero
  • Aromatics
  • 2 tspFresh ginger, grated (wok with oil)🟢 low ox
  • 3 clovesGarlic, crushed (5-min rest, off-heat finish)🟢 low ox
  • Vegetables
  • 200gGreen cabbage, shredded🟢 low ox
  • 150gMushrooms (button / cremini / shiitake)⬜ zero
  • Sauce + fat
  • 2 tbspEVOO — divided⬜ zero
  • 2 tbspGinger bug liquid (or water)⬜ zero
  • 1 tbspGF tamari or coconut aminos⬜ zero
  • 1 tspLemon juice (at finish)⬜ zero
  • 1 tspToasted sesame oil (optional, finish)⬜ zero
  • 2Spring onions, sliced (to serve)🟢 low ox
Oxalate: Low. Cabbage is low oxalate. Mushrooms are zero. Tamari: use GF-certified brand (San-J or Kikkoman GF). Coconut aminos is naturally GF and lower-sodium. No soy sauce — regular soy sauce contains wheat.
👩‍🍳 Instructions
  1. 1
    Prep everything before the wok gets hot
    Crush garlic; rest 5 minutes. Slice chicken thinly across the grain. Grate ginger. Shred cabbage, slice mushrooms. Measure out ginger bug liquid, tamari, and sesame oil. Have a plate ready next to the stove. Stir-fry cannot be paused — once the wok is hot, each step takes 60–90 seconds.
  2. 2
    Sear the chicken
    Heat 1 tbsp EVOO over high heat until just smoking. Add chicken in a single layer. Sear 90 seconds without touching. Stir-fry 60 more seconds until just cooked through. Remove to a plate — it will finish when returned to the wok.
  3. 3
    Ginger first, into hot oil
    Add remaining EVOO to the hot wok. Add grated ginger immediately — stir-fry 30–45 seconds until fragrant and starting to colour. Gingerol needs hot oil contact to activate. Do not add garlic here.
  4. 4
    Mushrooms then cabbage
    Add mushrooms — stir-fry 2 minutes until beginning to colour and releasing liquid. Add cabbage — toss over high heat 2–3 minutes until wilted but still with bite. Do not cover with a lid or it will steam.
  5. 5
    Deglaze and return chicken
    Pour ginger bug liquid into the centre — scrape the wok base. Add tamari. Return chicken and toss everything 60 seconds over high heat to coat. Sauce reduces almost immediately.
  6. 6
    Finish off-heat
    Remove from heat. Add activated garlic, lemon juice, sesame oil. Toss to combine. Taste for salt. Serve over millet, cauliflower rice, or on its own, topped with spring onions and black pepper.
Mechanism summary: Mushroom beta-glucan → L-cell GLP-1 secretion (DP gut axis) + ergothioneine → mitochondrial antioxidant (APOE4 neuroprotection) · Cabbage glucosinolates → indole-3-carbinol → phase II liver CYP1A2 + glutathione (Fatty Liver / MTHFR) · Cabbage prebiotic fibre → Bifidobacterium → overnight GLP-1 (DP) · Gingerol (hot oil, early) → COX-2, AMPK, nausea/motility · Garlic allicin (off-heat, full preservation) → AMPK, glutathione · EVOO MUFA → APOE4 LDL oxidation reduction · GF tamari → no wheat, no gluten (Celiac) · Low oxalate throughout

🍽 Meal plan pairings

🥣 Over millet (Tuesday dinner) The direct build-out of Tuesday's meal plan entry. Serve over millet cooked in bone broth — the broth provides glycine and the millet provides resistant starch. Food-test this combination (T0/T30/T60/T120) on first use.
🥦 With cauliflower rice (low-GI option) Pulse cauliflower in a food processor to rice-sized pieces and dry-fry in EVOO for 3–4 minutes. Zero GI. Serves as the base for this stir-fry on days when you want to avoid the millet GI load entirely or are watching glucose more carefully.
🥚 With a fried egg on top A fried egg in EVOO on top of the stir-fry adds egg phosphatidylcholine (liver fat export) to the cabbage glucosinolate (liver phase II) + mushroom ergothioneine combination. Three separate liver pathways addressed in one bowl.
🫙 With sauerkraut on the side The stir-fry cabbage is cooked; sauerkraut is raw fermented. Combining both provides prebiotic fibre (cooked cabbage) + probiotic live cultures (sauerkraut) at the same meal — a dual gut-support plate that stacks DP GLP-1 mechanisms.
🍗 Protein swap — use leftover golden paste chicken Skip the fresh chicken and fold in cold sliced golden paste chicken thighs from the night before. The curcumin paste flavour works with the ginger-garlic base. This is the leftover use case for batch-cooked chicken without needing a separate recipe.
🌿 Add kale instead of (or alongside) cabbage Kale works as a direct swap for cabbage and adds folate (MTHFR) to the same glucosinolate profile. Add it for the last 90 seconds of cooking only — kale wilts fast and turns bitter if overcooked. Shred off the stems first.

🔬 Why "whole ingredients only" matters for Celiac

  • Regular soy sauce contains wheat: Traditional soy sauce (Kikkoman standard, most restaurant soy sauce) is brewed with wheat as a fermentation substrate. Even small quantities — a splash as a condiment — can trigger a Celiac immune response. Gluten-free tamari (San-J GF, Kikkoman GF) is brewed without wheat and is certified to ≤20ppm. Check the label every time — the GF and regular versions of the same brand are packaged identically except for a small "GF" marking.
  • Coconut aminos as an alternative: Coconut aminos (e.g. Coconut Secret brand) is made from fermented coconut sap and contains no soy, no wheat, and no gluten. It is naturally lower in sodium than tamari and slightly sweeter. It is an acceptable substitute in this recipe and is the better choice if you are trying to reduce sodium. Flavour profile is slightly different — less sharp, more rounded.
  • Why no cornstarch: Cornstarch is used in most restaurant stir-fry recipes to thicken the sauce and coat the chicken. It is gluten-free, but it contributes to glucose spike — a cornstarch-coated chicken stir-fry has a measurably higher GI than the same dish without it. This recipe achieves sauce body from reduction (boiling off liquid over high heat) rather than starch thickening. The result is a less viscous sauce but a lower GI dish.
  • Why EVOO in a stir-fry (not sesame oil for cooking): Sesame oil has a lower smoke point than EVOO (~177°C vs ~200°C) and its characteristic flavour is damaged at high stir-fry temperatures. Toasted sesame oil is best used as a finishing oil added off-heat — which is how this recipe uses it. EVOO handles the high-heat searing. Using EVOO for cooking also ensures the MUFA fat is present as the primary cooking medium for APOE4 LDL protection.
