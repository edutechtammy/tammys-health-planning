📅 Week 5 Tasting Set

Creamy EVOO Dressings

Two creamy dressings that replace seed-oil-based commercial creamy dressings (ranch, Caesar, avocado ranch) with whole-food bases: your own Sugar Shift yogurt thinned to pouring consistency, and fresh avocado blended with EVOO and lime. Both are short shelf life. Both deliver far more nutritional value per serving than their commercial equivalents — the yogurt version provides live probiotics, and the avocado version delivers monounsaturated fat, potassium, and folate in every bottle.

⚠️ Both are short shelf life — orange/yellow labels for both. Yogurt Dill (#15): 12 oz batch, 3–5 days. Avocado-Lime (#16): 6 oz batch, 2–3 days only (avocado oxidizes faster than anything else in the set even with lime juice). Recipe 16 does not scale to 12 oz — two avocados going brown in 2 days is wasteful. Make small and make it fresh.
Bottle label guide — this set
🟡 Orange label — both recipes · Recipe 15: 3–5 days · Recipe 16: 2–3 days · write batch date on label

Both dressings are low GI at a 1-oz serving. The yogurt base in #15 and the avocado fat in #16 both slow glucose absorption from the salad — genuinely beneficial for blood sugar management, not just neutral.

15 Creamy Dill Yogurt Dressing
🟡 3–5 days · orange label · 12 oz batch
Ingredients — 12 oz batch
    Creamy base
  • ¾ cupSugar Shift yogurt strained overnight — or labneh straight from jar
    • Oil & acid
  • 3 tbspExtra-virgin olive oil
  • 2 tbspFresh-squeezed lemon juice
    • Seasoning
  • 1 tspDried dill weed
  • ¼ tspGarlic powder
  • ¼ tspFine sea salt
  • pinchBlack pepper
    • Thinning liquid
  • 3–4 tbspWater (add 1 tbsp at a time — see method)
¾ cup yogurt (6 fl oz) + 3 tbsp EVOO + 2 tbsp lemon + up to 4 tbsp water = ~12 fl oz ✓ · Exact water amount depends on yogurt thickness
Method
  1. 1
    Strain if using yogurt
    If using Sugar Shift yogurt (not labneh): strain overnight through a cheesecloth-lined sieve in the fridge. The yogurt needs to be thick enough that it coats greens without sliding off — labneh is ready without straining. If the yogurt was already quite thick, 4–6 hours of straining is sufficient.
  2. 2
    Whisk base
    In a bowl, whisk together the strained yogurt, EVOO, lemon juice, dill, garlic powder, salt, and pepper until smooth and uniform. EVOO should be whisked in (not stirred) to emulsify it into the yogurt — this creates a creamier texture than just stirring.
  3. 3
    Thin to pourable
    Add water 1 tbsp at a time, whisking after each addition, until the dressing pours easily from a spoon. Target: thicker than a vinaigrette, thinner than tzatziki. It should flow freely from a squeezed silicone bottle without significant pressure.
  4. 4
    Fill & label
    Use a small funnel to fill 12 × 1-oz silicone bottles. Orange label with today's date. Refrigerate. Squeeze each bottle before use — the dressing may thicken slightly when cold. A brief palm-warm (10–15 seconds) restores pourability.
What's working: Sugar Shift yogurt or labneh as the base delivers live probiotic strains (gut-liver axis support for NAFLD, B12, calcium), high protein (which blunts post-meal glucose response from any carbohydrate alongside it), and phosphatidylcholine (APOE4 relevant). EVOO stirred in — oleocanthal preserved raw. Dried dill provides ferulic acid and flavonoids. Lemon juice adds urinary citrate (kidney stone protection). This is a pourable tzatziki — same components, thinner consistency, designed to go on greens not be used as a dip. Best on: any green salad, cucumber-tomato, roasted potato salad (if doing a food test on potatoes), alongside Greek meatballs or grilled chicken. Oxalate: very low. Celiac: verify your yogurt brand if not using homemade Sugar Shift — most plain yogurts are GF but check for cross-contamination notes.
16 Creamy Avocado-Lime Dressing
🟡 2–3 days only · orange label · 6 oz batch · write date on label
Ingredients — 6 oz batch
Does not scale to 12 oz — avocado oxidizes to grey-brown within 2–3 days even with lime. One avocado = one batch. Make it when you'll use it within 2 days.
    Creamy base
  • 1 ripeAvocado (medium, ~4–5 oz flesh)
    • Oil & acid
  • 2 tbspExtra-virgin olive oil
  • 2 tbspFresh lime juice (about 1 lime)
    • Seasoning
  • ¼ tspGarlic powder
  • ¼ tspGround cumin
  • ¼ tspFine sea salt
  • pinchBlack pepper
    • Thinning
  • 2–3 tbspWater (add to reach pourable consistency)
Avocado flesh (~4–5 oz) + 2 tbsp oil + 2 tbsp lime + 2–3 tbsp water ≈ 6 oz ✓ · Orange label · Write date · Discard after 2–3 days
Method
  1. 1
    Choose a ripe avocado
    The avocado must be fully ripe — it should yield gently to thumb pressure. An underripe avocado will produce a grainy dressing that won't blend smooth. If it's not ready, wait a day.
  2. 2
    Blend smooth
    Scoop avocado flesh into a small blender or use an immersion blender in a tall cup. Add EVOO, lime juice, garlic powder, cumin, salt, pepper, and 2 tbsp water. Blend until completely smooth — 30–45 seconds. Add remaining water 1 tbsp at a time until the dressing flows freely from a spoon. A jar and immersion blender produces less cleanup than a countertop blender for this small volume.
  3. 3
    Taste & adjust
    Taste: more lime for brightness, more salt if flat, a pinch of cumin if it needs depth. The lime is the primary oxidation inhibitor — don't reduce it below 2 tbsp or the dressing browns faster.
  4. 4
    Fill & label with date
    Use a small funnel to fill 6 × 1-oz silicone bottles. Orange label — write today's date clearly. Refrigerate. Even in the fridge, the surface inside the bottle will start to oxidize (darken) by day 2 — this is aesthetic, not a safety issue if within the date window. Shake well before squeezing. Discard on day 3 regardless.
What's working: Avocado provides monounsaturated fat (same oleic acid as EVOO — additive dose), potassium, folate (MTHFR relevant — avocado is one of the highest whole-food sources of dietary folate per serving), and beta-sitosterol (plant sterol with cholesterol-modulating effects relevant to APOE4 lipid management). Lime juice contributes limonene and urinary citrate alongside significant vitamin C. Ground cumin adds thymoquinone and cuminaldehyde — both with anti-inflammatory and hepatoprotective activity. EVOO folate and oleocanthal preserved raw. Best on: any green salad, black bean + corn salads (if testing beans), as a dip for raw vegetables, alongside the cassava pita when that test is complete. The flavour is distinctly Southwestern/Mexican rather than Mediterranean — a complete change of flavour zone from the other 15. Oxalate: avocado is low oxalate. Note: this is the most expensive dressing in the set (avocado cost) — relevant if scaling decisions are made after the tasting week.
