Rosemary EVOO Cassava Crackers

A direct replacement for almond crackers — genuinely crisp, holds toppings well, and made from cassava flour which is trace-level oxalate compared to almonds at 469mg/100g. The technique is straightforward: cassava flour worked with EVOO, fresh rosemary, and lemon zest, rolled as thin as possible between parchment sheets and baked low-and-slow at 160°C until completely dry. They crisp further as they cool. A seed cracker variation using pumpkin, sunflower, and flaxseed with psyllium as a binder is included below as a second format — different texture (denser, nuttier) but the same low-oxalate and GF foundation.

⚠️ Why almond crackers had to go
📊 Oxalate comparison — cracker bases

Almonds are one of the highest-oxalate foods in common use. A typical almond cracker recipe uses 200–250g almond flour per batch of ~20 crackers. A 5-cracker serving (~50–60g almond flour equivalent) can contain 200–280mg oxalate — close to the entire recommended daily limit for kidney stone prevention in a single snack, before the rest of the day's food is counted. After one to two years as a dietary staple, this is a significant ongoing contribution to oxalate load.

🫙 Recipe A — Rosemary EVOO Cassava Crackers
🧾 Ingredients — ~30 crackers
  • Base dough
  • 1 cup (120g)Cassava flour — sifted🟢 trace ox
  • 2 tbspTapioca starch — extra crispness⬜ zero
  • ¼ tspFine non-iodised sea salt⬜ zero
  • 3 tbspEVOO⬜ zero
  • 4–6 tbspCold water — added gradually⬜ zero
  • Flavour
  • 1 tbspFresh rosemary, very finely chopped (or 1 tsp dried)🟢 low
  • 1 tspLemon zest (unwaxed)⬜ zero
  • Topping
  • to tasteFlaky sea salt⬜ zero
  • optional2 tbsp pumpkin or sunflower seeds, pressed into surface🟢 low
📍 Cassava flour ≠ tapioca starch: They come from the same root but are different products. Cassava flour is the whole dried root (fibre intact). Tapioca is the extracted starch only. Both are needed here — cassava provides structure; tapioca provides crispness. Swapping one for the other entirely will change the texture significantly.
🌿 Flavour variations: Swap rosemary for dried thyme + garlic powder. Or use 1 tsp za'atar (low oxalate blend of thyme, oregano, sumac, sesame — check label). Or black pepper + lemon zest only for a clean neutral cracker to pair with any topping.
👩‍🍳 Method
  1. 1
    Preheat and sift
    Preheat oven to 160°C (320°F) — not hotter. Cassava crackers need a gentle, even bake to dry out without browning the surface before the inside crisps. Sift cassava flour and tapioca starch into a bowl. Add fine salt, rosemary, and lemon zest. Stir thoroughly.
  2. 2
    Work in the EVOO
    Add 3 tbsp EVOO. Use your fingertips to rub the oil into the flour mixture for 1–2 minutes until the texture looks like coarse, slightly damp sand and holds together when pinched between your fingers. This step is what makes the crackers flaky rather than dense.
  3. 3
    Add water and form the dough
    Add cold water 1 tbsp at a time, stirring after each addition. Stop when the dough just comes together into a smooth ball that doesn't crack when pressed and doesn't stick to your hands. This is usually 4–5 tbsp. Cassava flour varies by brand and humidity — go slowly. Over-watered dough produces chewy, soft crackers that won't fully crisp.
  4. 4
    Roll between parchment — as thin as possible
    Divide dough in two. Place one half between two sheets of baking parchment. Roll to 2mm or thinner — the thinner the cracker, the crispier it will be. Use even pressure from the centre outwards. If edges crack, press them back and continue. Aim for even thickness throughout — thick patches will still be soft when thin edges are done.
  5. 5
    Score, top, and transfer to tray
    Peel off the top parchment sheet. Use a knife or pizza wheel to score the sheet into rectangles (~5×8cm) or squares — don't press all the way through, just score for easy snapping after baking. Press any seeds into the surface. Scatter flaky sea salt over the top. Slide the bottom parchment sheet with the dough onto a baking tray.
  6. 6
    Bake 160°C for 18–22 minutes
    Bake until the crackers are pale golden and completely dry to the touch — when you lift a piece it should not flex. Edges will colour before the centre. At 18 minutes, check: if centre pieces still feel slightly soft, turn the oven off and leave the tray inside for 5 more minutes as it cools. Do not raise the temperature to speed this up — the crackers will burn on the edges while the centre stays chewy.
  7. 7
    Cool fully before breaking
    Leave on the tray for at least 10 minutes before touching — they are still firming up as they cool. Snap along the scored lines. Store airtight at room temperature for up to 5 days. If they soften slightly after a day or two (happens in humid conditions), spread on a tray and return to a 140°C oven for 8–10 minutes to re-crisp.
🌱 Recipe B — Seed Crackers (pumpkin, sunflower, flax + psyllium)
🌻

Seed Crackers — no flour, psyllium-bound

GF · Low Oxalate · High Zinc

A completely flour-free cracker — seeds and psyllium husk only. Psyllium absorbs water and forms a gel that binds the seeds into a solid, sliceable sheet. The finished cracker is denser and nuttier than the cassava version, with a satisfying snap and a much higher mineral content (pumpkin seeds are one of the best dietary zinc sources — relevant for APOE4 for cognitive protection and for MTHFR for methylation cofactor support). Two different cracker formats means you're not eating the same thing daily.

Ingredients — ~20 crackers
  • ½ cup (70g)Pumpkin seeds (pepitas)🟢 low
  • ½ cup (70g)Sunflower seeds🟢 low
  • ¼ cup (40g)Flaxseed / linseed (whole)🟢 mod at this dose
  • 3 tbspPsyllium husk (not powder) — binder🟢 low
  • ¾ tspFine non-iodised sea salt⬜ zero
  • 2 tbspEVOO⬜ zero
  • ¾ cupCold water⬜ zero
  • optional1 tsp dried thyme or rosemary🟢 low
Method
  1. 1
    Mix and soak — 15 minutes
    Combine seeds, psyllium husk, salt, herbs, EVOO, and water in a bowl. Stir well. Rest 15 minutes — the psyllium will absorb the water and the mixture will thicken into a sticky, spreadable mass. It should hold together when you press it with a spoon. If it's still wet and loose after 15 minutes, add 1 more tbsp psyllium and rest 5 more minutes.
  2. 2
    Spread on parchment — 3mm
    Preheat oven to 150°C (300°F). Transfer the mixture to a parchment-lined baking tray. Use a damp spatula or damp hands to spread evenly to ~3mm thickness. Score into rectangles or squares with a knife — press all the way through for these, as the seed crackers are harder to snap cleanly after baking.
  3. 3
    Bake 150°C — 40 minutes, flip halfway
    Bake 20 minutes. Flip the entire sheet by placing a second parchment sheet on top, inverting, and peeling off the original parchment from what is now the top. Return to oven for another 20 minutes. The crackers are done when they are completely hard, dry, and golden — they should sound hollow when tapped. Cool fully on a rack — they are still softening as they cool.
Psyllium husk vs powder: Husk (coarser) binds slightly less strongly than powder. If using psyllium powder, reduce to 2 tbsp — it is more absorbent and a full 3 tbsp will make the mixture too thick to spread.
🧀 What to put on them
🍽️ Topping pairings from this recipe library
Labneh + lemon zest + EVOO
The simplest and best. Protein + fat slows cassava starch digestion and buffers the GI of the cracker. Labneh's live cultures and the cracker's prebiotic inulin (cassava fibre) stack well. Black pepper on top.
Homemade mozzarella + lemon zest
Low-sodium, low sat fat. Tear fresh mozzarella cold onto the cracker — do not melt onto it (the fat will soak in and soften the cracker). Eat immediately. Pairs with a spoonful of purple sauerkraut alongside (Recipe 20 — anthocyanin + labneh culture stack).
Avocado + lemon + black pepper
Avocado is very low oxalate (8–10mg/100g), high in oleic acid (APOE4 appropriate), and high in potassium (kidney stone protective — increases urinary citrate). Mash cold with lemon juice and salt. The cassava cracker is sturdy enough to hold it without breaking.
Bone broth cheese sauce (Recipe 19)
Use the seed cracker (denser format) as a dipper. The psyllium seed cracker holds up better to a wet sauce than the cassava cracker. Warm the sauce gently — don't reboil.
Smoked salmon (if tolerated) + labneh + capers
Wild-caught smoked salmon is very low oxalate, high omega-3 (APOE4 / inflammation), and high B12 (MTHFR). Capers are low oxalate. Watch sodium if blood pressure is a concern — smoked salmon is high sodium; the cracker's salt content is minimal so net sodium is manageable.
Olive tapenade (no vinegar version)
Blend Kalamata olives, EVOO, garlic (cold-activated), and lemon juice only — no red wine vinegar (oxalate) and no anchovies if sodium is a concern. Olives are very high in oleocanthal (APOE4 anti-inflammatory). A small amount (1 tbsp) per cracker keeps sodium in check.

🔬 Why these choices

  • Cassava flour — not almond, not oat, not rice: Oat flour (even GF-labelled) carries cross-contamination risk and contains avenin which some Celiac patients react to. Rice flour is very high GI and has its own arsenic concerns at daily use. Almond is off the list entirely for the oxalate reasons above. Cassava is the Celiac-safest, lowest-oxalate flour alternative that produces a genuinely crisp cracker rather than a soft or chalky result.
  • EVOO as the fat — not butter, not coconut oil: Butter adds sat fat (APOE4 contraindicated at daily use). Coconut oil is predominantly medium-chain saturated fat — fine in small doses but not the daily fat of choice at scale. EVOO's oleic acid is consistently the preferred fat for APOE4 from a lipid metabolism standpoint, and it contributes to the cracker's flavour and crispness in the same way butter would in a conventional recipe.
  • Baking at 160°C not 180–200°C: Cassava starches gelatinise and then crisp as moisture escapes. At high heat, the outside firms and browns before the moisture inside can escape — the result is a cracker that looks done but flexes and softens within an hour. Low-and-slow allows the entire thickness to dry out evenly. The same principle applies to the seed crackers at 150°C.
  • Psyllium as the seed cracker binder — not egg: An egg would work as a binder but introduces a daily egg if these are eaten regularly alongside the kefir chicken and other egg-containing items in the library. Psyllium is a soluble fibre that forms a firm gel; it is the binder of choice in GF baking for this reason. It also adds meaningful prebiotic fibre (fermentable by Lactobacillus — synergistic with the purple sauerkraut) and modest cholesterol-lowering effect at the amounts used here (relevant for APOE4 lipid management).
  • Flaxseed at seed cracker quantities — kidney stone note: Whole flaxseed at ¼ cup across 20 crackers (approximately 2g per cracker) is a low-to-moderate oxalate dose per serving. Ground flaxseed has slightly higher bioavailable oxalate. The whole seed format used here reduces oxalate absorption slightly because the seed coat is partially impermeable. At a 2–3 cracker serving, the oxalate from flaxseed is well within daily limits.
