Lemon-Garlic Pan Chicken with Bone Broth Pan Sauce

Chicken breast seared golden in extra virgin olive oil, rested, then a two-minute bone broth pan sauce is built in the same pan — scraping up every bit of the fond (caramelised protein and collagen from the sear) and finishing with 5-minute activated garlic and lemon. This is the default elevated chicken recipe for your plan. It works alongside roasted asparagus, sautéed kale, or a millet or quinoa base. The technique produces consistently juicy breast every time: flatten to even thickness so the whole piece finishes simultaneously, sear without moving, and rest before slicing. The pan sauce turns a plain protein into a complete therapeutic meal — bone broth glycine, EVOO MUFA, allicin, and urinary citrate in a 90-second sauce.

🔪 Four technique points that make or break pan chicken

1. Flatten to even thickness Chicken breast is shaped like a teardrop. The thin end cooks 2–3 minutes faster than the thick end. Without flattening, one end is dry before the other is safe. Bash the thick end down to ~2cm. One minute of effort; consistently better result.
2. Dry the surface completely Moisture on the surface creates steam in the pan. Steam = grey boiled chicken, not golden sear. Pat with paper towel until the surface feels slightly tacky, not damp. Leave on the board uncovered for 5–10 min if time allows.
3. Scrape the fond — all of it The brown layer stuck to the pan after searing is not burnt food. It is the Maillard reaction product of proteins, collagen, and sugars — concentrated flavour and nutrition. The bone broth deglaze dissolves it instantly. Scrape every bit into the sauce.
4. Garlic goes in off-heat Allicin forms during the 5-minute pre-rest and survives brief gentle heat. A hot pan above ~160°C destroys it quickly and burns the garlic to bitterness. Add the activated garlic to the sauce after removing the pan from the heat — residual warmth is enough.

Chicken + pan sauce is negligible GI. Food-test the full meal (chicken + side dish combination) if the side is millet, quinoa, or GF bread — the chicken itself does not require testing.

🧾 Ingredients — 2 servings
  • Chicken
  • 2Chicken breasts (~180–200g each)⬜ zero ox
  • 1½ tbspExtra virgin olive oil (EVOO)⬜ zero
  • ¼ tspFlaky sea salt⬜ zero
  • to tasteFreshly ground black pepper⬜ trace
  • Pan Sauce
  • 3 clovesGarlic, crushed (rest 5 min, add off-heat)🟢 low ox
  • 180mlHomemade bone broth⬜ zero
  • ½ lemonLemon juice (at finish)⬜ zero
  • 3–4 sprigsFresh thyme (or ½ tsp dried)🟢 low ox
  • Optional
  • 1 tspEVOO or butter (sauce finish, for gloss)⬜ zero
  • handfulFresh parsley, chopped (MTHFR folate)⬜ zero
Oxalate: Near-zero. Chicken, EVOO, bone broth, and lemon are all zero oxalate. This is one of the safest main dishes in the library. Use your homemade bone broth — store-bought is lower in collagen and minerals.
👩‍🍳 Instructions
  1. 1
    Activate garlic and flatten chicken
    Crush 3 garlic cloves and rest on the board for 5 minutes. Meanwhile, place each breast between two sheets of baking paper and bash the thick end with a rolling pin or heavy pan until the whole breast is approximately 2cm thick throughout. Pat both sides completely dry with paper towel. Season generously with salt and pepper.
  2. 2
    Sear the first side — do not touch it
    Heat EVOO in a stainless steel or cast iron pan over medium-high heat until shimmering. Add chicken and press lightly with a spatula to ensure full surface contact. Do not move it. Cook for 5–6 minutes. It is ready to flip when it releases cleanly — if you have to force it, it needs another 30–60 seconds.
  3. 3
    Flip and finish
    Flip and cook the second side 4–5 minutes until golden. Internal temperature at the thickest point should reach 74°C (165°F). Transfer to a plate, tent loosely with foil, and rest for 5 minutes. Do not slice yet — resting allows the juices to redistribute. The chicken will continue cooking slightly off-heat.
  4. 4
    Build the pan sauce
    Reduce heat to medium. Pour in the bone broth — it will bubble hard. Immediately scrape the base of the pan with a wooden spoon to dissolve all the fond. Add the thyme. Simmer 3–4 minutes until reduced by roughly half and the sauce coats the back of a spoon lightly.
  5. 5
    Finish off-heat and serve
    Remove pan from heat. Stir in the activated garlic. Add lemon juice. Stir in the finishing EVOO or butter if using. Taste and adjust salt. Discard thyme sprigs. Slice the chicken across the grain, pour the pan sauce over, and scatter fresh parsley. Serve immediately with your chosen side.

🔬 Why the technique choices are therapeutic, not just culinary

  • Why EVOO for searing (not butter, not coconut oil): EVOO's smoke point (~200°C) is sufficient for medium-high searing and the oleic acid content makes it the correct fat for APOE4 carriers. Butter adds saturated fat (SFA) and coconut oil is almost entirely SFA — both increase LDL oxidation risk in APOE4 carriers. Using EVOO here is a deliberate APOE4 fat strategy, not a flavour choice. The small amount used for searing is also absorbed by the chicken surface and becomes part of the meal's fat profile.
  • Why bone broth (not water, not store-bought stock): The pan sauce needs a liquid with body — plain water produces a thin, flavourless deglaze. Homemade bone broth contains dissolved collagen (gelatin), glycine, proline, minerals, and trace glucosamine. Glycine is the rate-limiting amino acid for hepatic phase II detoxification (glycine conjugation of metabolic waste and drugs) — directly relevant for MTHFR and fatty liver. Store-bought stock is largely water with added sodium and flavouring; the collagen and mineral content is negligible compared to a properly made 12-hour homemade batch.
  • Why garlic goes in off-heat: The 5-minute pre-crush activates allicin. Adding it to a hot pan above ~160°C degrades allicin within 30–60 seconds and produces bitter compounds. Adding it to the sauce immediately after pulling the pan from the heat exposes it to ~85–90°C residual warmth — enough to mellow the raw edge and allow it to infuse the sauce, but not enough to destroy the allicin. This is the same principle used in every recipe in this library.
  • Why lemon at the finish (not as a marinade): Acid (citric acid, lemon juice) denatures surface proteins if used as a marinade — it "cooks" the outer layer of the chicken, creating a mealy texture and poor browning. As a finish, it provides flavour brightness, activates salivary amylase (improves digestion), and delivers urinary citrate for kidney stone management without any negative effect on the cooked chicken's texture.
  • Why rest the chicken before slicing: Cutting immediately after cooking causes all the accumulated juices to run out of the muscle fibres onto the plate, leaving the chicken dry. Resting for 5 minutes under foil allows the muscle fibres to relax and reabsorb the juices. This is not optional technique — it is the difference between a dry and a moist breast, and it continues during the 3–4 minutes you spend making the pan sauce, so it costs zero additional time.
