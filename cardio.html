Week 1–2 — lower volume version Zone 2: 15–20 min only.
Resistance: 2 days only (Mon + Fri).
After-dinner walk: every day.
Priority: establishing the habit and finding your Zone 2 speed. Do not push duration until form and breathing feel natural.
When the weight system is not yet available Run the full bodyweight protocol on the 3 resistance days. Add the resistance band row as a priority — it covers the back muscles that bodyweight cannot reach. Once the weight system arrives, slot in the dumbbell exercises using the same day structure.

Core idea Zone 2 cardio creates the metabolic signal that helps muscle absorb and burn excess glucose and fat. It is not “cardio for fitness” in the usual sense — it is a recovery and metabolic reset tool that supports the liver and insulin-resistant tissues.
Why this matters
  • Raises AMPK in muscle, which supports GLUT4 movement without needing insulin.
  • Increases mitochondrial density and fat oxidation capacity.
  • Improves fasting glucose and reduces post-meal glucose spikes over time.
  • Supports the liver by lowering triglyceride load and reducing fatty liver pressure.
Session structure
  • 4–5 days per week at a sustainable Zone 2 effort.
  • 25–40 minutes per session depending on recovery and goals.
  • Use a pace where conversation is possible but you are breathing harder than at rest.
  • Short post-dinner walks can be layered in without replacing the main session.
Treadmill ready checklist
  • Water bottle filled and within reach.
  • Phone or secondary device ready.
  • Work tasks queued up or audio prepared.
  • Bathroom break taken before starting.

Mechanism summary

Exercise activates a separate pathway for GLUT4 translocation than insulin. This means glucose can be pulled into muscle even when insulin signaling is impaired.

It also lowers the liver's glucose release and improves triglyceride processing through repeated muscle contraction and mitochondrial activation.

For fatty liver, the benefit is not just “calories burned.” It is the cumulative effect of better muscle uptake, improved beta-oxidation, and lower hepatic triglyceride packaging.
