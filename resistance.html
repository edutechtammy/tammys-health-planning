                Option A — Bodyweight protocol
                Chair Squat

                Chair Squat

                Bodyweight
                Quads · Glutes · Hamstrings

                3 × 12–15

                Stand in front of a chair, sit down slowly, stand back up without using your hands.

                Pause squat → full squat → single-leg squat.

                Glute Bridge

                Glute Bridge

                Bodyweight
                Glutes · Hamstrings · Core

                3 × 15–20

                Lie on back, knees bent, feet flat. Drive hips up and lower slowly.

                Hold at top → single-leg bridge → elevate feet.

                Step-Up

                Step-Up

                Bodyweight
                Quads · Glutes · Balance

                3 × 10 each side

                Step up onto a low step and alternate leading leg.

                Slow lowering phase → higher step → light backpack.

                Dead Bug

                Dead Bug

                Bodyweight
                Core · Hip flexors · Spinal erectors

                3 × 6–8 each side

                Lie on your back, knees bent at 90° floating above your hips, arms extended toward the ceiling. Slowly lower your right arm overhead while straightening your left leg just above the surface — only as low as your lower back stays pressed down. Return to center and switch sides.

                Safely teaches your core to brace while your limbs move, with no neck strain. The back-to-mattress cue keeps the movement within a safe range.

                Knee Marches

                Knee Marches

                Bodyweight
                Hip flexors · Lower abs · Quads

                3 × 10 each side

                Sit near the edge of the mattress and use the scaffold end-frame rungs for stability if needed. Slowly lift one knee toward your chest in a controlled march and hold for a second before lowering. For extra quad and lower-ab work, straighten the leg out after the lift before returning.

                Uses the scaffold frame for balance so you can focus entirely on the lift. Targets hip flexors and lower abdominals safely in a seated position with no spine loading.

                Modified Bird-Dog

                Modified Bird-Dog

                Bodyweight
                Spinal erectors · Core · Glutes

                3 × 8 each side

                Start on hands and knees on the mattress. Keeping your core braced and back flat, slowly extend your right arm forward and your left leg straight back. Hold for a couple of seconds, return to the start, and switch sides.

                Builds foundational core stability and protects your spine without requiring you to get down on a hard floor. The mattress surface provides a forgiving base.

                Option B — With a weight system
                Goblet Squat

                Goblet Squat or Leg Press

                Compound
                Quads · Glutes · Hamstrings

                3 × 10–12

                One of the most valuable lower-body movements for fatty liver and triglyceride control.

                Build the largest muscle sink while preserving joint-friendly form.

                Romanian Deadlift

                Romanian Deadlift

                Compound
                Hamstrings · Glutes · Lower back

                3 × 10–12

                Hinge at the hips and lower the weight while keeping the spine neutral.

                Posterior-chain strength and lower-body metabolic load.

                Dumbbell Floor Press

                Dumbbell Floor Press (or Push-ups)

                Upper body press
                Chest · Shoulders · Triceps

                3 × 10–12

                Since you are training at home, the floor press is a safe, shoulder-friendly bench-press alternative that lets you push challenging dumbbell loads with control.

                If no dumbbells are available, use push-ups with the same set and rep structure.

                Bent-Over Row

                Bent-Over Dumbbell Row (Dumbbells)

                Upper back pull
                Upper Back · Lats · Biceps

                3 × 10–12

                Balances out all pressing work, helping protect posture and pull the shoulders back.

                Hinge at the hips, keep the torso stable, and drive elbows back without shrugging.

                Option C — Resistance band protocol
                Monster Walk

                Monster Walk

                Resistance band
                Glute medius · Hip stability · Lower body

                3 × 10–14 steps each direction

                Builds a stronger lower-body metabolic sink while training side-hip control for better movement stability.

                Use lateral side-to-side monster walk steps with the band around the ankles.

                Resistance Band Row

                Resistance Band Row

                Highly recommended
                Back · Biceps

                3 × 12–15

                Anchor band at waist height, step back, and pull elbows toward ribs.

                Heavier band → single-arm row → incline row.

                Option D — Recovery day protocol
                Gentle Walk

                Gentle Walk

                Recovery day
                Recovery · Circulation · Mobility

                15-30 minutes, comfortable pace

                Keeps your body moving on recovery days while still supporting metabolic health and consistency.

                Relaxed posture, easy nasal breathing, and a pace where conversation stays comfortable.

                Stretches

                Stretches

                Recovery day
                Mobility · Recovery · Joint comfort

                10-20 minutes of gentle full-body stretching

                Supports tissue recovery, eases stiffness, and helps maintain range of motion between training days.

                Move slowly, breathe steadily, and stop short of pain while holding each stretch with control.

                Bike Ride

                Bike Ride

                Recovery day
                Blood flow · Recovery · Lower-body flush

                15-35 minutes, flat and easy pace

                Can add variety on recovery days and keep a steady movement habit when you want a change of pace.

                A very light, flat bike ride at a conversational pace, just enough to turn the pedals and flush blood through your quads without feeling resistance.

                Aquatic Center Recreation

                Aquatic Center Recreation

                Recovery day
                Joint-friendly cardio · Recovery · Full-body relief

                15-30 minutes, relaxed effort

                A future change-of-pace option that can make recovery days feel more enjoyable and sustainable.

                Water provides wonderful buoyancy that unloads your joints entirely while offering gentle resistance and a soothing cooldown for sore muscles.

                Gardening

                Gardening

                Recovery day
                Gentle movement · Recovery · Upright activity

                15-40 minutes at easy pace

                A practical way to stay active on lower-intensity days while avoiding hard training stress.

                Simple, low-intensity tasks in the yard that keep you moving upright without heavy lifting or strenuous exertion.
