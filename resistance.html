💪 Resistance — Building the Metabolic Sink

Use this page for the strength work that builds the muscle needed to store and burn more glucose and triglyceride-derived fuel.

The principle More muscle mass creates a larger metabolic sink, and that matters for fatty liver, post-meal glucose, and triglyceride handling. Resistance work is the structural fix that supports everything else.
Frequency and structure

2–3 days per week, not consecutive days.

10–15 per set at a challenging but controlled effort.

3 sets per exercise.

60–90 seconds between sets.

Option A — Bodyweight protocol
Chair Squat

Chair Squat

Bodyweight
Quads · Glutes · Hamstrings

3 × 12–15

Stand in front of a chair, sit down slowly, stand back up without using your hands.

Pause squat → full squat → single-leg squat.

Glute Bridge

Glute Bridge

Bodyweight
Glutes · Hamstrings · Core

3 × 15–20

Lie on back, knees bent, feet flat. Drive hips up and lower slowly.

Hold at top → single-leg bridge → elevate feet.

Step-Up

Step-Up

Bodyweight
Quads · Glutes · Balance

3 × 10 each side

Step up onto a low step and alternate leading leg.

Slow lowering phase → higher step → light backpack.

Resistance Band Row

Resistance Band Row

Highly recommended
Back · Biceps

3 × 12–15

Anchor band at waist height, step back, and pull elbows toward ribs.

Heavier band → single-arm row → incline row.

Option B — With a weight system
Goblet Squat

Goblet Squat or Leg Press

Compound
Quads · Glutes · Hamstrings

3 × 10–12

One of the most valuable lower-body movements for fatty liver and triglyceride control.

Build the largest muscle sink while preserving joint-friendly form.

Romanian Deadlift

Romanian Deadlift

Compound
Hamstrings · Glutes · Lower back

3 × 10–12

Hinge at the hips and lower the weight while keeping the spine neutral.

Posterior-chain strength and lower-body metabolic load.
