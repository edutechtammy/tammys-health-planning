Bone Broth Soup Base — Master Recipe

The foundational batch-cook recipe for the week. Chicken or beef bones simmered for 10–24 hours with activated garlic, fresh ginger, and a splash of ACV to pull collagen and minerals from the bone matrix. The result — a deeply flavoured, gelling broth — is the cooking liquid behind the Ginger-Garlic Millet Bowl, the base for weekly soups, a warm morning drink, and the glycine source that supports both gut lining repair and liver phase II detoxification. Make one large batch on the weekend; use it every day of the week.

⏱ Prep: 15 min 🔥 Simmer: 10–24 hrs 📦 Yield: 8–10 cups ❄️ Freezes: up to 3 months

✅ The gelatin quality test: After chilling overnight, a properly made bone broth will set like a loose jello — it should hold its shape when you tilt the jar. This gel = high gelatin content = hydroxyproline and proline-rich collagen peptides that directly repair intestinal tight junctions (Celiac gut healing) and support liver collagen synthesis. If your broth does not gel, return it to the pot and simmer uncovered for 1–2 more hours to reduce. Add more bones next batch (chicken feet are the highest-gelatin addition you can make).

Bone broth as a standalone warm drink has near-zero glucose impact. Log a test if pairing with a higher-GI food to isolate that food's contribution.

👩‍🍳 Instructions
  1. 1
    Optional: roast bones first
    Spread bones on a tray, roast at 200°C / 400°F for 25–30 minutes until golden. Adds colour, depth, and Maillard compounds. Skip for a lighter, cleaner broth — both are equally therapeutic.
  2. 2
    Activate the garlic
    Crush all 6 cloves, rest 5 minutes before adding to the pot. Some allicin survives gentle long simmering; the rest converts to stable diallyl sulfide compounds that retain AMPK activity.
  3. 3
    Combine and cold-start
    Add bones, vegetables, garlic, ginger, ACV, peppercorns, bay leaves, and kombu to a large stockpot or slow cooker. Cover with cold water — the cold start draws proteins and minerals out more gradually, producing a clearer broth. Do not add salt yet.
  4. 4
    Bring up slowly, skim foam
    Bring to just below a boil over medium heat (about 20 minutes). Grey-brown foam will appear — skim it off with a ladle. Continue skimming until foam stops, then reduce to the absolute lowest simmer: liquid should tremble, not bubble.
  5. 5
    Simmer (see timing table)
    Chicken: 10–14 hours. Beef/lamb: 16–24 hours. Slow cooker LOW: 18–24 hours. Do not rush — collagen → gelatin conversion requires sustained low heat over time. A hard boil shortens this.
  6. 6
    Strain and chill
    Strain through a fine mesh sieve. Cool 30 minutes at room temperature, then refrigerate overnight. Skim solidified fat from surface next day. Season with salt once chilled and tasted at full concentration.
  7. 7
    Portion and store
    Refrigerate up to 5 days. Freeze in 1-cup portions for up to 3 months. A properly made broth gels solid when cold — this is the gelatin quality marker. If liquid, return to pot and reduce further.

🔄 How to use this broth through the week

🥣 Millet cooking liquid Replace all water with bone broth when cooking millet (Ginger-Garlic Millet Bowl). The grain absorbs the glycine, minerals, and shogaols directly. Zero additional prep time; major therapeutic upgrade.
☕ Warm morning drink Heat 1 cup and drink warm before breakfast — especially valuable on Dawn Phenomenon mornings when glucose is elevated and appetite is disrupted. Near-zero calorie load, immediate gut support, glycine activates liver glucuronidation from first thing.
🍳 Shakshuka sauce starter Add ½ cup to the tomato sauce base instead of extra water. Deepens flavour and adds glycine to the egg dish. The calcium from broth also contributes to oxalate binding alongside the feta.
🥬 Vegetable braising liquid Use as the cooking liquid for braised greens (kale, bok choy, zucchini). Adds minerals without added fat or sodium. Especially useful for Celiac days when gut absorption needs maximum mineral support.
🫙 Weekly soup base Add whatever protein and low-oxalate vegetables you have — shredded chicken, diced zucchini, bok choy, carrots. Season with garlic and ginger. A different soup every week from the same broth foundation.
💊 Medication / supplement rinse A small warm cup alongside fat-soluble supplements (Vitamin D, K2, EVOO-based curcumin) aids absorption. The fat from marrow and the warmth improve fat-soluble nutrient uptake without a full meal.

🔬 Why this recipe is foundational

  • Glycine — the most under-dosed amino acid in modern diets: Glycine is the most abundant amino acid in collagen and the rate-limiting substrate for glutathione synthesis (the master antioxidant). MTHFR impairs the methylation cycle that recycles homocysteine — glycine from bone broth provides an alternative route to glutathione via the transsulfuration pathway. It is also a primary substrate for phase II liver glucuronidation, directly clearing toxins and lipid peroxidation products in fatty liver.
  • Proline and hydroxyproline — tight junction repair: In Celiac disease, the intestinal villi and tight junctions between enterocytes are damaged by gluten-triggered inflammation. Proline and hydroxyproline are the structural amino acids of collagen, which is the extracellular matrix on which those tight junctions are anchored. Regular gelatin/collagen intake directly supports the regeneration of this architecture — not just the symptoms.
  • ACV in the simmer liquid: Acetic acid lowers the pH of the liquid slightly, which solubilises calcium phosphate and magnesium from the bone matrix. This is why broth made with ACV is meaningfully higher in minerals than broth without it. The acid flavour cooks off completely; you will not taste it in the finished broth. Use liquid ACV in cooking for this step — the capsule form is for oral use before meals.
  • Cold-start vs. hot-start: Starting bones in cold water and heating slowly extracts proteins as a gradual process, producing a clearer broth with more intact collagen chains. Hot-start causes rapid surface protein coagulation and a cloudier result — not harmful, but lower gelatin clarity. This also affects texture when used as a cooking liquid for millet or grains.
  • Chicken feet: If you can source them, chicken feet are the single highest-gelatin addition to bone broth. The tissue between the toe bones is almost pure collagen. Even one or two feet per batch will noticeably increase gel strength. Asian grocery stores carry them fresh or frozen reliably.
  • The gelatin test: After overnight chilling, the broth should set to a loose jello consistency. This is not the same as fat solidifying (fat will be white and solid on top — skim that off). The gel itself is translucent and amber-coloured. If it does not gel at all, either the simmer time was too short, the heat was too high (boiling breaks gelatin chains), or not enough collagen-rich material was used. All three are correctable on the next batch.
