Ginger Carrot Slaw with Apple, Sweet Pepper & Lemon

A raw slaw of five ingredients that is stronger on paper than it might look — this is one of the highest vitamin C dishes in the collection, and fresh ginger gingerols are among the most well-studied acute anti-inflammatory compounds available from whole food. Coarsely grated carrot, very finely grated fresh ginger, thinly sliced sweet bell pepper and apple (skin on for quercetin), dressed with fresh lemon juice and a pinch of salt. No cooking, no added sugar, no oil required — though a small drizzle of EVOO at serving meaningfully increases beta-carotene absorption from the carrot. Ready in 15 minutes. Keeps well for a day dressed. All six badges.

🌿 What each ingredient is doing
🧬 Every ingredient earns its place

🥕 Carrot — beta-carotene, falcarinol, prebiotic fibre

Carrot's primary bioactive is beta-carotene — one of the most abundant dietary carotenoids and a direct antioxidant, particularly relevant in mitigating the oxidative stress associated with APOE4. Beta-carotene is fat-soluble, meaning it requires fat in the same meal for meaningful absorption. Raw carrot retains more falcarinol (a polyacetylene compound with direct antiproliferative evidence) than cooked, making this raw preparation preferable. Carrot also provides prebiotic soluble fibre that feeds Lactobacillus and Bifidobacterium. GI of raw carrot is approximately 16 — significantly lower than cooked carrot (~47), because cooking breaks down the cell wall and releases the sugars more rapidly. Keeping this slaw raw is a meaningful advantage for Dawn Phenomenon management. Fat note: a drizzle of EVOO at serving is optional but nutritionally meaningful — studies show beta-carotene absorption increases 3–6× when consumed with a small amount of fat. The dish is completely valid without it; it is simply more effective with it.

🫚 Fresh ginger — gingerols, NF-κB suppression, anti-nausea

Fresh ginger contains gingerols — the active form of ginger's bioactive compounds. When dried or heated, gingerols convert to shogaols (also bioactive but different profile). In this raw preparation all the gingerol content is preserved intact. Gingerols are direct inhibitors of NF-κB — the same inflammatory signalling pathway targeted by quercetin (apple skin), anethole (fennel in Recipe 27), and rosmarinic acid (herbs in Recipes 27 and 28). This means ginger is hitting the same anti-neuroinflammatory target that is particularly relevant for APOE4. Gingerols are also the most well-studied natural anti-nausea compounds, and have evidence for mild blood pressure reduction (DP/fatty liver relevant). Grate very finely — large fibrous pieces are unpleasant texturally and the heat-producing compounds concentrate in coarse pieces.

🫑 Sweet bell pepper — vitamin C, luteolin, quercetin

Sweet bell peppers contain more vitamin C per gram than oranges — a medium red pepper has approximately 150–190mg vitamin C, which is 1.5–2× the daily RDI in a single vegetable. Vitamin C is directly relevant here on multiple levels: it's a cofactor in collagen and carnitine synthesis; it increases the bioavailability of non-haem iron (important if you eat plant-based iron sources); and — specifically for MTHFR — it enhances folate bioavailability and stability. Vitamin C is also directly involved in recycling oxidised vitamin E back to its active form (APOE4 antioxidant network relevance). Red and orange peppers have significantly more beta-carotene and lycopene than green peppers — use red or orange for the best nutritional profile. Peppers are essentially zero oxalate.

🍎 Fuji apple — quercetin, pectin, low GI (keep the skin)

The apple appears in this recipe for the same reasons as in Recipes 26 and 27: the skin contains quercetin (NF-κB suppression, APOE4 relevant) and the flesh provides pectin prebiotic fibre. In a raw slaw, the apple adds a crisp sweetness and structural contrast to the grated carrot. Skin-on, matchstick-cut or thin slices integrate best with the texture of the grated carrot and julienned pepper. Do not peel. The pectin also helps the slaw hold together slightly — it acts as a very mild natural binder in the dressing once the lemon juice begins to draw moisture from the vegetables.

🍋 Lemon juice — citrate, vitamin C, dressing acid

Fresh lemon juice serves three roles here: flavour acid (brightens and balances the sweetness of the carrot and apple), vitamin C supplementation (stacks with the pepper's already very high vitamin C content), and — specifically for kidney stone prevention — urinary citrate. Citrate is one of the primary inhibitors of calcium oxalate crystal formation in the urine, and dietary citrate from lemon juice is the basis of the standard dietary recommendation for kidney stone prevention. Use fresh-squeezed only — bottled lemon juice often has additives and significantly lower active vitamin C due to oxidation during storage and pasteurisation.
⚗️ Notes

💡 Beta-carotene absorption — the optional EVOO drizzle

Beta-carotene is fat-soluble: it dissolves into fat in the digestive tract and is absorbed alongside dietary fats. In the absence of any fat in the meal, beta-carotene absorption from raw carrot is low — studies show absorption can increase 3–6× when even a small amount of fat is consumed with the same meal. A teaspoon or two of EVOO drizzled over the slaw at serving is the simplest way to unlock the carrot's full beta-carotene benefit without changing the character of the dish. This is an optional enhancement, not a requirement — the slaw is excellent and nutritionally meaningful as-is, and the vitamin C from the pepper and lemon is fully water-soluble and needs no fat. If you're serving this alongside a meal that includes EVOO or other fats (e.g., alongside the turkey meatloaf or braised pork), the fat in those dishes is sufficient.

✅ All five ingredients — zero Celiac risk, zero condition concerns

Fresh carrot, fresh ginger, fresh bell pepper, fresh apple, and fresh lemon juice are all inherently gluten-free with no cross-contamination risk. No labels to check. Every ingredient is low-to-zero oxalate at the quantities used. No purine concern (kidney stone). No saturated fat (APOE4). No carbohydrate of concern (DP) — raw carrot GI is ~16, significantly lower than cooked. No ingredient in this slaw creates any issue for any of the six conditions.

✅ Raw carrot GI vs cooked — keeping it raw is a meaningful advantage

Raw carrot has a GI of approximately 16. Cooked carrot rises to approximately 47 — nearly three times higher — because heat breaks down the cell wall, releasing sugars for rapid absorption. This raw slaw preparation is therefore significantly better for Dawn Phenomenon blood sugar management than a cooked carrot dish using the same quantity of carrot. The intact cell wall structure also means more prebiotic fibre reaches the large intestine intact rather than being digested in the small intestine.

👩‍🍳 Method
  1. 1
    Grate or julienne the carrots
    Peel the carrots and grate on the coarse side of a box grater, or cut into thin julienne strips about 5cm long. Coarse grating is faster and gives a softer texture once dressed; julienne gives more structure and slightly less moisture release. Either works well. Place in a large mixing bowl.
  2. 2
    Grate the ginger — fine is important
    Scrape the ginger skin off with a teaspoon (faster than a peeler, wastes less). Grate on a microplane or the finest side of a box grater. Fine grating disperses the gingerol compounds throughout the slaw and avoids unpleasant fibrous chunks. Start with 2cm, add to the carrot, and taste after the lemon goes on — you can always add more. Add to the bowl.
  3. 3
    Slice the pepper and apple
    Core and thinly slice the pepper into strips or julienne. Thinly slice or matchstick-cut the apple — skin on, do not peel (quercetin is in the skin). If there will be more than a couple of minutes before the lemon goes on, toss the apple briefly in a few drops of lemon juice to prevent browning. Add both to the bowl.
  4. 4
    Dress with lemon juice and salt
    Squeeze the lemon juice over the bowl and add a pinch of salt. Toss everything together well — get all the grated ginger off the bottom of the bowl and distributed evenly. Taste: adjust lemon if it needs more brightness, salt if it tastes flat. The ginger heat develops over a minute or two, so let it sit briefly before judging how much heat is present.
  5. 5
    Rest 5–10 minutes
    Let the slaw sit for 5–10 minutes before serving. The lemon draws a small amount of moisture from the vegetables, the ginger distributes evenly, and the flavours integrate. This brief rest transforms it from a mix of individual components into a cohesive dish.
  6. 6
    Optional: drizzle EVOO at serving
    If adding EVOO, drizzle 1–2 tsp over the slaw just before serving and toss lightly. This does not change the flavour noticeably but significantly increases beta-carotene absorption from the carrot. Skip if you are eating this alongside a meal that already contains EVOO or other fats — the fat from those dishes is sufficient.

🫙 Make-ahead and storage

  • Undressed components: Grated carrot and sliced pepper keep for 2 days in separate airtight containers in the fridge. Slice the apple fresh — it browns too quickly to store cut.
  • Dressed slaw: Holds well for up to 1 day refrigerated. Unlike lettuce or baby spinach, grated carrot doesn't go limp when dressed — it actually improves slightly overnight as the lemon juice and ginger marry together. The apple softens a little but remains pleasant.
  • Ginger intensity over time: Ginger flavour intensifies as the slaw sits — what tastes mildly spiced after 10 minutes will taste noticeably spicier the next day. If you are making ahead, start with less ginger than you think you need and taste after 30 minutes.
  • Pairing suggestions: Works well alongside the turkey meatloaf (Recipe 28) — the brightness cuts through the richness of the glaze. Also pairs with the braised pork (Recipe 27) or the hard boiled eggs (Recipe 25) for a simple protein-plus-slaw lunch. Can be served as a side to any protein in the collection.
  • Scaling up: This recipe scales easily — double or triple for a larger family meal or to have leftovers through the week. The proportions are forgiving; adjust ginger and lemon to taste at the larger quantity.

🩸 Low GI — raw carrot GI ~16 (far lower than cooked carrot); apple ~36 with skin; peppers negligible. No concern for routine monitoring at normal serve sizes.
