👨‍👩‍👧‍👦 Why This Matters for Our Family

Both parents carry MTHFR C677T and Celiac Disease — both are autosomal, meaning each child has a meaningful chance of inheriting one or both. Getting tested and starting methylated B-vitamins and a strict GF diet early prevents decades of subclinical damage.

🧬 MTHFR C677T Both parents carry this variant. Reduces folate processing ~40–70%. Simple fix: methylfolate (not folic acid) + B2. Test: request MTHFR genotype + homocysteine level from your GP.
🌾 Celiac Disease First-degree relatives have ~10% risk vs ~1% in general population. Can present silently for years. Test: tTG-IgA antibody (must be eating gluten at time of test). Do not go GF before testing.
🧠 APOE Genotype Dad carries two APOE3 (e3/e3) — the neutral, most common form. Mom carries one APOE4 copy. Each child has a ~50% chance of inheriting one APOE4 from Mom and will have e3/e4. Knowing early means you can start brain-protective habits in your 30s–40s when they matter most. Test: direct-to-consumer (23andMe) or ask your GP.
🌅 Pre-diabetes / Dawn Phenomenon MTHFR + stress + family metabolic history = higher risk. A fasting insulin + glucose (HOMA-IR) test at your annual physical is all it takes to catch this early. Reversible with diet and sleep — much harder once A1c climbs.
💡 The simplest first step for any family member Ask your doctor for: tTG-IgA (Celiac) · Homocysteine · MTHFR genotype · Fasting insulin + glucose. Four tests. One blood draw. Enormous peace of mind — or a very actionable early warning.
⚠️ Important: Do not go gluten-free before Celiac testing The tTG-IgA test requires active gluten exposure to detect antibodies. If you suspect Celiac, get tested first, then go GF after confirmation.
