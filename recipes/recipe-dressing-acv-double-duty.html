ACV Double-Duty Dressings

Three dressings where the apple cider vinegar does double work: it's the flavour acid and simultaneously delivers the pre-diabetes benefit of acetic acid with every serving. Acetic acid inhibits alpha-amylase and alpha-glucosidase — the starch-to-glucose enzymes — slowing glucose absorption from anything starchy in the meal. All dried seasonings only; standard 2–3 week shelf life. Use raw ACV with the mother for the full acetic acid dose.

📏 12 oz batch = 12 × 1-oz squeeze bottles. Formula: 16 tbsp EVOO + 6 tbsp ACV + 2 tbsp water = 24 tbsp = 12 fl oz. Divides evenly: half = 6 oz · third = 4 oz · quarter = 3 oz.
⚡ Why ACV is in all three: The pre-diabetes benefit is dose-dependent and happens per meal, not accumulated over time. 1 oz of these dressings delivers roughly ½ tbsp ACV — about 250–300 mg acetic acid. The effect is most relevant when the salad accompanies a meal with any significant carbohydrate. Use one of these three on those meals preferentially.

5 Honey-Mustard ACV Dressing
✓ 2–3 weeks refrigerated
Ingredients — 12 oz batch
  • 16 tbspExtra-virgin olive oil (= 1 cup)
    • Acid
  • 6 tbspApple cider vinegar, raw with mother
  • 2 tbspWater
    • Flavour
  • 1 tbspDijon mustard
  • 1 tspRaw honey
  • ¼ tspGarlic powder
  • ½ tspFine sea salt
  • ¼ tspBlack pepper
16 + 6 + 2 = 24 tbsp = 12 fl oz ✓
1 tsp honey in 12 oz batch = <0.5g honey per 1-oz serving — negligible blood sugar impact at this dilution
Method
  1. 1
    Combine
    Add all ingredients to jar. The Dijon mustard is the emulsifier — add it directly to the jar, no premixing needed.
  2. 2
    Shake
    Seal and shake vigorously 30–45 seconds. The honey and Dijon together create a surprisingly stable emulsion — this one holds together longer than a plain vinaigrette.
  3. 3
    Fill & label
    Fill 12 × 1-oz silicone bottles. Green standard label. Refrigerate. Shake before use.
What's working: ACV acetic acid — pre-diabetes benefit per serving. Dijon lecithin — natural emulsifier for stability. Raw honey — negligible at dose; adds palatability without meaningful glucose load. This is the most crowd-friendly of the three ACV dressings — honey-mustard is a familiar flavor that translates immediately. Best on: mixed greens, coleslaw-style salads, as a dipping sauce for raw vegetables, over chicken. Oxalate: trace, negligible per serving.
6 ACV-Dill Dressing
✓ 2–3 weeks refrigerated
Ingredients — 12 oz batch
  • 16 tbspExtra-virgin olive oil (= 1 cup)
  • 6 tbspApple cider vinegar, raw with mother
  • 2 tbspWater
    • Herbs & seasoning
  • 2 tspDried dill weed
  • ½ tspGarlic powder
  • ½ tspFine sea salt
  • ¼ tspBlack pepper
  • optionalPinch onion powder
16 + 6 + 2 = 24 tbsp = 12 fl oz ✓ · This one separates quickly — shake before every use
Method
  1. 1
    Combine
    Add all ingredients to jar. No emulsifier in this recipe (no Dijon or mustard) — it will separate cleanly and require shaking before each use. This is expected.
  2. 2
    Shake
    Seal and shake 20 seconds to combine. The dill flecks will distribute throughout. Shake each silicone bottle before squeezing — the herb flecks can settle.
  3. 3
    Fill & label
    Fill 12 × 1-oz silicone bottles. Green standard label. Refrigerate.
What's working: Dried dill — ferulic acid and flavonoids with mild anti-inflammatory activity; notably less folate than fresh dill but still a positive contributor. ACV acetic acid delivered per serving. The flavor profile is clean, bright, and cool — this pairs naturally with cucumber-forward salads and anything with fish. The tzatziki-adjacent character makes it feel familiar without being identical. Best on: cucumber-tomato salads, cold salmon, as a light dip for cut vegetables. Oxalate: negligible per serving.
7 Golden Anti-Inflammatory Dressing
✓ 2–3 weeks refrigerated
Ingredients — 12 oz batch
  • 16 tbspExtra-virgin olive oil (= 1 cup)
  • 6 tbspApple cider vinegar, raw with mother
  • 2 tbspWater
    • Anti-inflammatory compound
  • ½ tspGround turmeric
  • ¼ tspBlack pepper — mandatory for curcumin bioavailability
  • ½ tspDried ground ginger
    • Balance
  • 1 tspRaw honey (cuts the earthy bitterness)
  • ½ tspFine sea salt
16 + 6 + 2 = 24 tbsp = 12 fl oz ✓ · Bottle will be golden-yellow — label clearly to avoid confusion with the others
⚠️ Turmeric is moderate-oxalate: always eat this dressing with a calcium-containing food (cheese on the salad, feta, yogurt dressing alongside) to bind oxalate in the gut before absorption.
Method
  1. 1
    Combine
    Add all ingredients to jar. Turmeric and ginger are dry powders — they will not fully dissolve but will suspend in the oil. The honey helps integrate the powders.
  2. 2
    Shake vigorously
    Seal and shake hard for 45 seconds — the dry spices need more agitation. Shake each bottle firmly before squeezing, every time. The turmeric settles noticeably.
  3. 3
    Fill & label
    Fill 12 × 1-oz silicone bottles. Green standard label — but consider a special gold sticker or marker noting "GOLDEN" to distinguish from the other bottles. Refrigerate.
What's working: Turmeric — curcumin inhibits amyloid-β aggregation and tau phosphorylation (APOE4 neuroprotection), suppresses NF-κB (neuroinflammation), reduces hepatic lipogenesis (fatty liver). Black pepper is mandatory — piperine raises curcumin bioavailability by ~2000% by blocking first-pass liver metabolism. Without it, most curcumin is excreted unused. Dried ginger — shogaols and gingerols activate AMPK, complement ACV's glucose mechanism. Honey at 1 tsp per 12-oz batch = negligible dose, primarily a palatability aid for the earthier profile. Note: this is an acquired taste — it may not be an immediate crowd favourite but is the most therapeutically dense of the five sets. Oxalate: moderate due to turmeric — use with calcium-containing food at same meal.
