Chia Chocolate Pudding

A simple chocolate pudding made by combining ground chia, cocoa, and a monkfruit/allulose sweetener with milk, then chilling until thick and creamy. It is a low-effort dessert or breakfast option with a texture that improves the longer it sits, and it can be frozen after the overnight chill for a rich, ice-cream-like finish.

⏱ 10 min prep + 12 hr chill 🍽 2–3 servings ❄️ Chill overnight; freeze after set if desired
Net carbs: low Saturated fat: low
APOE4 Celiac / GF DP / Blood Sugar Fatty Liver MTHFR Kidney Stone
Ingredients
Core ingredients
  • 3 TBSP ground chia seeds
  • 3 TBSP cocoa powder
  • 3 TBSP monkfruit and allulose sugar substitute
  • 1.5 c milk
Method
  1. 1
    Combine the dry mix
    Add all dry ingredients to a mason jar and stir well so the cocoa and sweetener are evenly distributed.
  2. 2
    Add the milk gradually
    Pour in the milk slowly, stirring as you go or shaking the jar until the mixture fills the jar and the dry ingredients are fully moistened.
  3. 3
    Shake well
    Seal the jar and shake vigorously until the pudding is smooth and no dry pockets remain.
  4. 4
    Refrigerate overnight
    Place the jar in the refrigerator for a minimum of 12 hours. Less time can create a higher glucose spike and a looser texture.
  5. 5
    Set and serve
    Shake the sealed jar a few times over the chill period to keep the chia distributed. For a frozen dessert, transfer to the freezer after the 12-hour chill for a rich ice cream texture.

Timing note

The overnight chill is not just about texture — it also helps the chia fully hydrate and reduces the chance of a higher glycemic rise. A minimum of 12 hours is the sweet spot for a thicker, more satisfying pudding.

Practical notes

  • Use ground chia for the smoothest texture; whole seeds will create a more rustic, grainier pudding.
  • Monkfruit/allulose works well here because it dissolves more cleanly than some other sweeteners and does not leave a harsh aftertaste.
  • For a richer dessert, a splash of extra milk before serving can loosen the texture without changing the flavor profile.
  • This is a good make-ahead option for breakfast or a dessert when you want a chocolate fix without a heavy sugar load.

Best served cold and well chilled for the most stable texture and the lowest glycemic impact.
