🎂 Celebration / Dessert — Spring Birthday Cake

Spiced Carrot Celebration Cake with Labneh Honey Frosting

The spring birthday cake — rebuilt for every condition. Grated fresh carrot provides beta-carotene, falcarinol (a neuroprotective and liver-protective polyacetylene compound not present in pumpkin), and the moisture that makes carrot cake stay soft for days. The flour architecture is the same as Recipe 31 (Pumpkin Cake) — sorghum, millet, tapioca, psyllium husk — with one key technique difference: grated carrot is a wet ingredient that manages its own moisture during baking, so the oil is higher and the batter starts looser. Coconut sugar (GI ~35, caramel depth that is right for warm spice) and maple syrup replace white sugar. The frosting is labneh whipped with honey, vanilla, and lemon zest — the exact cream cheese frosting analogue that carrot cake traditionally calls for, live-culture and probiotic. All six conditions. ~10–12mg oxalate per slice.

🧬 What carrot is doing that pumpkin isn't
🥕 Carrot-specific compounds

🟢 Falcarinol — the compound that distinguishes carrot

Falcarinol is a polyacetylene found in carrots (and parsley, parsnip, celery) that has direct evidence for neuroprotective and liver-protective activity not present in pumpkin. Animal model studies show falcarinol reduces precancerous lesions in the colon and has anti-inflammatory activity via COX-1 and COX-2 inhibition — a different pathway to beta-carotene. It is fat-soluble and is preserved in baking. The EVOO in the batter means its absorption is supported the same way as beta-carotene. For APOE4: the neuroprotective evidence is an additional argument for carrot over pumpkin at this life stage. For fatty liver: the hepatoprotective evidence is direct. Neither compound is a pharmaceutical dose in a single slice — but across regular consumption the signal is real and cumulative.

🟠 Beta-carotene — high, fat-soluble, absorption supported by olive oil in batter

Raw carrot is one of the highest beta-carotene sources per gram (~8285mcg/100g). Baking converts some beta-carotene but also breaks down cell walls, improving bioavailability of what remains. The olive oil in the batter is present throughout the crumb — every bite of cake has fat-soluble carotenoid absorption support built in, the same principle as the EVOO drizzle on the slaw (Recipe 29). The labneh frosting adds further fat contact at the point of eating.

🌾 Prebiotic fibre — inulin + soluble fibre, gut-microbiome pathway

Carrots contain both inulin-type fructooligosaccharides and soluble fibre (pectin). In a baked cake the fibre is retained. Inulin is a prebiotic that feeds Bifidobacterium and Lactobacillus — the same Lactobacillus species in the labneh frosting. A slice of this cake feeds the gut bacteria that the frosting delivers. The combination is genuinely complementary — the cake and frosting together have a synbiotic (prebiotic + probiotic) effect that neither has alone.
💧 The key technique difference — grated carrot moisture management

⚗️ Why this batter looks wetter than the pumpkin cake — and why that is correct

The most common mistake in carrot cake (especially GF carrot cake) is responding to the looser batter by adding more flour. Do not. Grated carrot releases moisture progressively during baking — the moisture you see in the raw batter becomes part of the steam that lifts the crumb and produces the characteristic soft, dense-moist texture of a good carrot cake. The tapioca starch and psyllium husk in this blend are specifically good at managing that released moisture.

  • One gentle press, not wringing: Spread the grated carrot on a tea towel and give it one firm press to remove surface water. Do not wring it dry — you need that moisture in the batter. The goal is to remove the free water that would make the batter sloppy, not the structural moisture bound inside the carrot cells.
  • Finely grated, not coarsely shredded: Fine grating distributes the carrot moisture more evenly through the crumb. Coarse shreds create pockets of wet carrot in an otherwise dry crumb. Use the fine side of a box grater or the grating disc of a food processor.
  • More oil than the pumpkin cake (100ml vs 80ml): Pumpkin purée contains its own fat-like moisture that olive oil partly replaces. Grated carrot does not — the additional 20ml of olive oil compensates. This is why carrot cake is traditionally an oil cake rather than a butter cake.
  • The 5-minute batter rest is essential here: The psyllium and tapioca will absorb the carrot's surface moisture during this rest and the batter will visibly firm up. After the rest, the batter should look like a thick muffin batter — scoopable, not pourable.

🎂 Method
  1. 1
    Preheat & prepare tin
    175°C / 350°F (fan 160°C). Grease a 23cm/9-inch round tin or standard loaf tin, base lined with baking paper. Two-layer: two 20cm/8-inch tins, reduce bake time to ~35–40 minutes.
  2. 2
    Prepare the carrots one press, not wringing
    Peel and finely grate 300g of carrots. Spread on a clean tea towel and give one firm press to remove surface moisture. Do not wring dry — retain most of the moisture. Set aside.
  3. 3
    Whisk dry ingredients psyllium must be even
    Whisk sorghum, millet, tapioca, psyllium husk, baking powder, baking soda, salt, and all five spices until completely uniform. Psyllium must be distributed evenly before any liquid is added.
  4. 4
    Combine wet ingredients
    Whisk coconut sugar and maple syrup. Add eggs one at a time. Add olive oil, vanilla, and lemon juice. Whisk smooth. This batter is looser than the pumpkin version — correct.
  5. 5
    Fold wet into dry, then fold in carrot — do not beat
    Pour wet into dry and fold until almost combined. Add grated carrot (and drained pineapple if using) and fold until no dry streaks remain. The batter will look wetter than pumpkin cake batter. Do not add more flour.
  6. 6
    Rest batter 5 minutes extra important here
    The psyllium and tapioca will absorb the carrot moisture during this rest. The batter will firm noticeably — after 5 minutes it should be scoopable, not pourable.
  7. 7
    Pour, smooth, bake
    Pour into tin, smooth top with wet spatula. Bake 50–60 min (round) or 55–65 min (loaf). Skewer test is the reliable indicator — not colour. Tent with foil from 35 min if browning too fast.
  8. 8
    Cool completely non-negotiable for GF
    Cool in tin 15 min, turn out onto a wire rack, cool fully before frosting — 2 hours at room temp or 1 hour refrigerated. Made the day before is recommended — spice flavour deepens overnight and the crumb firms for cleaner slicing.
  9. 9
    Make labneh frosting — same as Recipe 31
    Labneh + raw honey + vanilla + lemon zest + pinch of Ceylon cinnamon. Stir cold until smooth. Taste and adjust honey. Refrigerate until needed, up to 24 hours ahead.
  10. 10
    Frost, garnish, refrigerate
    Spread frosting over cold cake. Scatter broken walnuts if using. Refrigerate 30 min after frosting before serving. Keeps 2 days frosted, 5 days unfrosted, refrigerated.
⚗️ Condition notes

🔍 GF label checks — same two as Recipe 31

Baking powder: check for wheat starch (some brands use it as filler). Rumford aluminium-free is reliably GF. Vanilla extract: look for GF-labelled or confirm the alcohol base is not grain-derived. Pure vanilla extract is almost always GF; "vanilla flavouring" is less reliable.

🍍 Crushed pineapple — optional, low oxalate, traditional addition

Crushed pineapple (60g, well-drained) is a traditional carrot cake addition that adds moisture, a subtle tropical brightness, and a small amount of bromelain (anti-inflammatory enzyme). Pineapple oxalate is low (~2mg/100g) — at 60g across 12 slices the per-slice oxalate contribution is negligible (~0.1mg). Well-drain in a sieve and press firmly — excess pineapple juice will make the batter too wet. Use canned in juice, not in syrup.

✅ No raisins — the traditional addition that doesn't work for this collection

Classic carrot cake recipes often include raisins. Raisins are ~21mg oxalate per 30g serving — at a typical 80–100g addition across a whole cake, per-slice oxalate climbs ~6–8mg before the other ingredients are counted. Combined with the carrot oxalate, the total per slice would approach 20–22mg, still technically low but no longer the low-footprint cake this is built to be. The walnuts at garnish quantity are the right textural substitute.

🗒 Make-ahead, variations, storage

  • Best made the day before: Same principle as the pumpkin cake. Bake the day before, wrap unfrosted and refrigerate, frost on the day. The crumb firms for cleaner slicing and the spice flavour deepens.
  • Two-layer spring presentation: Two 20cm/8-inch tins, 35–40 min bake. The layer cake with a visible frosting layer in the middle is the classic carrot cake presentation — especially right for a spring birthday. The orange-flecked crumb against the white labneh frosting cross-section is visually excellent.
  • Cupcakes: This batter makes 18–20 cupcakes. Fill liners ¾ full. Bake at 175°C for 20–25 minutes. Cool completely before frosting. Good for birthday parties where individual portions are practical.
  • Purple carrot variation: Purple carrots contain anthocyanins in addition to beta-carotene — the same anthocyanins in blueberries (Recipe 30) with direct APOE4 relevance. Replace 100–150g of the orange carrot with purple carrot. The batter will be a deep burgundy-orange that bakes to a striking purple-flecked crumb. Beta-carotene and falcarinol content are comparable; anthocyanin content is additive.
  • Freezing: Unfrosted cake freezes well for 3 months — wrap tightly in plastic then foil. Thaw overnight in the refrigerator. Do not freeze frosted; labneh frosting separates on thawing.
  • Lemon zest in the frosting: Essential, not optional. The lemon zest against the carrot-spice combination is the element that makes the frosting feel specifically right for this cake rather than generic. Increase to 1½ tsp if you want the citrus note more pronounced.
