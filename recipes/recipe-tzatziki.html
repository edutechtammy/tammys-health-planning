If that liquid enters the dip, tzatziki turns watery and separates within 20 minutes on the table. Everything else is just stirring.

Tzatziki — Greek Yogurt, Cucumber & Garlic Dip

Classic Greek tzatziki: thick strained yogurt, grated cucumber squeezed bone-dry, 5-minute activated garlic, EVOO stirred in, fresh dill, and lemon juice. No cooking, 15 minutes of active work. Your Sugar Shift yogurt strained overnight through cheesecloth makes an outstanding base — or skip that step entirely and use your labneh straight from the jar. Use as a dip for raw vegetables and GF bread, a sauce over grilled chicken or salmon, or a natural partner alongside Ladenia or Shakshuka at a mezze spread.

⏱ Prep: 15 min (incl. cucumber salting) 🔥 Cook: none ⏳ Total: ~15 min + 15 min rest 🍽 Yield: ~4 servings as dip 🗓 Keeps: 3–4 days refrigerated

  • to tasteSalt & black pepper⬜ zero
  • To serve

  • sprigFresh dill⬜ garnish
Yogurt choice: Labneh = thickest, no straining needed, richest texture. Sugar Shift yogurt strained overnight through a cheesecloth-lined sieve = slightly looser, more probiotic complexity. Either works — do not use unstrained yogurt or the dip will be thin even after drying the cucumber.
👩‍🍳 Method
  1. 1
    Activate the garlic first
    Crush or finely mince the garlic. Set aside for 5 minutes. Since tzatziki is never heated, this rest is the only opportunity for alliinase to convert alliin to allicin. Start prepping the cucumber during the wait.
  2. 2
    Grate and dry the cucumber
    Grate the cucumber on the large holes of a box grater. Place in a clean kitchen towel. Sprinkle the ½ tsp salt over it and leave 5 minutes. Then fold the towel around the cucumber and squeeze firmly — twist and press repeatedly until wringing produces almost nothing. Taste: if very salty, give a quick rinse and re-squeeze. The shreds should feel almost dry to the touch.
  3. 3
    Combine everything
    In a bowl, stir together the yogurt (or labneh), the dry cucumber shreds, the rested garlic, the EVOO, the lemon juice, and the chopped dill. Stir the EVOO in — don't just drizzle it on top — it distributes evenly and gives the dip a silkier body. Season with salt and black pepper to taste.
  4. 4
    Taste, adjust, rest
    Taste and adjust: more lemon juice brightens, more garlic intensifies, more dill deepens the herbal note. Cover and refrigerate for at least 15 minutes — the garlic and dill continue developing as the flavours marry. Tzatziki made 30+ minutes ahead is noticeably better than freshly stirred.
  5. 5
    Serve
    Spoon into a shallow bowl. Make a small well in the centre with the back of a spoon and drizzle EVOO into it. Lay a fresh dill sprig on top. Serve alongside raw vegetable sticks, GF bread or Ladenia squares, or use as a sauce over grilled chicken, salmon, or lamb.
What's working here: Garlic 5-min rest → allicin (AMPK activation, glutathione induction for MTHFR, hepatoprotective — raw garlic delivers the highest allicin dose of any preparation) · EVOO stirred in → oleocanthal + oleic acid (COX inhibition, APOE4 neuroprotection — raw application preserves oleocanthal fully) · Sugar Shift yogurt or labneh → live probiotic strains (gut-liver axis support, B12, calcium, high protein for blood sugar stability) · Lemon juice → urinary citrate (kidney stone prevention, inhibits calcium oxalate crystal formation) · Fresh dill → one of the better culinary herb sources of folate per gram (MTHFR-relevant in raw form; cooking destroys 50–60% of folate) · Cucumber → high water content, very low oxalate, contributes citrulline and vitamin K. Nothing in this bowl is accidental.

🍽 Ways to use it

🥒 Classic vegetable dip plate Cucumber slices, carrot sticks, celery, and bell pepper strips arranged around a bowl of tzatziki. All low oxalate. All Celiac-safe. No prep beyond cutting. Works as a snack, starter, or side to any main.
🫓 With Ladenia Tzatziki alongside Ladenia squares is the natural Mediterranean pairing — the cool, creamy dip contrasts the warm, oily flatbread. Serve the marinated feta on the same plate and you have a full mezze spread from three recipes in this library.
🍗 Sauce over grilled chicken Spoon liberally over grilled or baked chicken breast or thighs. The garlic + EVOO in the tzatziki doubles as a finishing sauce. Works equally well over baked salmon — the dill is a natural fish herb.
🌿 Mint variation Replace the fresh dill with an equal amount of fresh mint for a slightly sweeter, more refreshing version — common in Turkish and Lebanese variations. Mint has rosmarinic acid (anti-inflammatory) and is gentler on GI than garlic-heavy versions if you're in a reactive phase.

🔬 Why each component is here

  • Labneh vs strained Sugar Shift yogurt — use either, for different reasons: Labneh is already thick enough to use straight from the jar — no overnight straining needed. Your homemade labneh has the same live cultures as the Sugar Shift yogurt it was drained from. If you use Sugar Shift yogurt, strain it overnight in a cheesecloth-lined sieve in the fridge — the whey drips off and you're left with a labneh-like consistency. Either produces an excellent, thick tzatziki. Do not use unstrained yogurt — even after squeezing the cucumber dry, the extra liquid in thin yogurt will make the dip loose.
  • Raw garlic — highest allicin of any garlic preparation: Cooked garlic loses virtually all allicin activity at heat above ~60°C. Raw garlic that has been crushed and rested 5 minutes has completed the alliinase-mediated conversion and delivers the maximum allicin dose. Tzatziki is one of the few dishes where you eat garlic truly raw — which also means the 5-minute rest matters here more than anywhere else.
  • EVOO stirred in, not drizzled: Stirring EVOO into the yogurt distributes oleocanthal and oleic acid throughout every bite. Drizzling only on top means the polyphenol benefit is concentrated in the first spoonful. In a raw application like this, oleocanthal is fully intact — no heat degradation at all.
  • Fresh dill — raw for folate: Fresh herbs lose 50–60% of their folate content when cooked. Dill is a reasonable culinary folate source; keeping it raw in tzatziki preserves that content. For MTHFR, dietary folate from whole-food sources accumulates meaningfully across meals — it's not a therapeutic dose from one tablespoon, but it adds up. Dried dill works for flavour but loses most folate in the drying process.
  • Lemon juice — urinary citrate: The small amount of lemon juice here contributes urinary citrate, which inhibits calcium oxalate crystal formation in the kidney — the primary mechanism for kidney stone prevention beyond hydration. Combined with the low-oxalate profile of the whole dip, tzatziki is genuinely kidney-friendly.
  • Celiac safety: All ingredients are naturally gluten-free. No cross-contamination risk in this recipe — verify only your yogurt brand if not using homemade.
