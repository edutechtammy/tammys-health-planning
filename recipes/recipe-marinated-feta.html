Marinated Feta in Chili & Herb EVOO

Creamy brine feta layered with fresh jalapeño, whole dried chilies, dried oregano, fresh parsley, and lemon zest — then submerged in cold-pressed extra-virgin olive oil. No cooking, no heat. Ten minutes of assembly, 30 minutes of patience, and it's ready. A genuine Mediterranean mezze that works as a calcium source, a capsaicin delivery vehicle, a folate hit from raw parsley, and the best possible use of good EVOO. Keeps refrigerated for two weeks, and the infused oil is its own reward as a finishing drizzle over salads or eggs.

⏱ Prep: 10 min 🔥 Cook: none ⏳ Marinate: 30 min minimum (overnight is better) 🍽 Yield: ~4 servings as mezze 🗓 Keeps: 2 weeks refrigerated

👩‍🍳 Method
  1. 1
    Drain and portion the feta
    Remove feta from its brine and blot gently with a clean cloth or paper towel. Tear or cut into rustic chunks, roughly 2–3 cm each. Crumbly feta will break into smaller pieces — both textures work.
  2. 2
    Layer the jar
    In a clean 500 mL glass jar, layer feta chunks with jalapeño slices, dried chilies, and a pinch of dried oregano between each layer. Scatter lemon zest through the layers as you build — don't leave it all sitting on top.
  3. 3
    Pour the EVOO — do not heat it
    Pour cold-pressed extra-virgin olive oil over the layered feta until it is mostly submerged. Do not heat the oil — oleocanthal degrades with heat. Raw EVOO poured cold is both pharmacologically superior and more flavourful.
  4. 4
    Finish with fresh parsley
    Scatter the chopped fresh parsley over the top. It sits above the oil line, stays bright green and folate-intact until you serve. Seal the jar.
  5. 5
    Marinate, then serve
    Leave at room temperature for 30 minutes minimum — the oil needs time to carry the heat and herb compounds into the feta. For best flavour, refrigerate overnight. The oil solidifies when cold — this is normal. Pull from the fridge 30 minutes before serving so the oil returns to liquid.
What's working here: Raw EVOO → oleocanthal at full potency (COX-1/COX-2 inhibition, hepatoprotection, APOE4 neuroprotection — all degrade with heat) · Capsaicin via jalapeño + dried chili → improves insulin sensitivity, slows gastric emptying, activates PPAR-α and TRPV1 in the liver to reduce hepatic fat accumulation · Feta calcium → passively binds chili oxalate in the gut at each serving · Fresh raw parsley → retains higher folate content than cooked parsley (directly supports MTHFR methylation) · Lemon zest → limonene (hepatoprotective monoterpene, anti-inflammatory). Nothing in this jar is decorative.
🗓 Storage: Seal tightly and refrigerate. Keeps up to 2 weeks. The EVOO solidifies when cold — pull from the fridge 30 minutes before serving. Do not store at room temperature after the initial marination period (treat like any fresh dairy jar). The infused oil is useful on its own: drizzle over salads, eggs, roasted vegetables, or GF bread as a finishing oil. If the oil level drops, top up with fresh EVOO rather than discarding the remaining feta.

🍽 How to use it

🥒 Classic mezze plate Serve with cucumber slices, olives, carrot sticks, and GF crackers or millet bread. Spoon some of the chili oil over the plate as well — it is the best part.
🥗 Salad finisher Crumble a few pieces over any leafy salad and drizzle a spoonful of the infused oil over the top instead of dressing. Replaces both the cheese and the dressing in one move.
🍳 Egg pairing Spoon over fried or poached eggs — the chili oil bastes the whites as you eat. Excellent with za'atar on top. Works equally well as a Shakshuka finish in place of plain crumbled feta.
🌿 Mild version (active GI sensitivity) Skip the jalapeño, reduce to 1 dried chili, and double the oregano to 2 tsp. Add a few sprigs of fresh thyme instead. All the flavour compounds from the oil still work — just without the capsaicin load on a reactive day.

🔬 Why each component is here

  • Raw EVOO — the whole point: Oleocanthal is the peppery phenolic at the back of the throat in quality cold-pressed EVOO. It inhibits COX-1 and COX-2 — the same mechanism as ibuprofen — and is the most studied EVOO compound for APOE4 neuroprotection and hepatic fat reduction. Heat above 40 °C accelerates its degradation. A raw marination like this is pharmacologically preferable to cooking with EVOO, not just a flavour choice. Use a real cold-pressed, single-origin EVOO here if you have one.
  • Capsaicin (jalapeño + dried chili): Capsaicin slows gastric emptying slightly — a small but real contribution to post-meal glucose curve flattening. More relevantly for fatty liver, capsaicin activates PPAR-α and TRPV1, both of which reduce hepatic fat accumulation through distinct mechanisms. The quantity in this recipe (1 jalapeño + 3–4 small dried chilies) is a meaningful dose, not decorative heat. If your gut is reactive on a given day, reduce to the mild version above — the oil benefits remain, just without capsaicin.
  • Fresh parsley — raw specifically: Cooking destroys 50–60% of folate. Parsley is one of the better herb sources of folate per gram, and keeping it raw (scattered on top, uncooked) means you retain most of that. For MTHFR, where even small dietary folate contributions matter across the day, this is worth noting. 2–3 tbsp of chopped parsley delivers approximately 15–20 mcg folate — not a therapeutic dose on its own, but it accumulates across meals.
  • Feta calcium — passive oxalate management: The jalapeño and dried chilies carry a low-moderate oxalate load. The feta is present at every serving, and calcium binds a fraction of dietary oxalate as insoluble calcium oxalate in the gut — reducing urinary absorption. This requires no active management. The pairing is already built into the recipe the way the original creator put it together.
  • Lemon zest, not lemon juice: The zest carries limonene (a monoterpene with hepatoprotective and anti-inflammatory properties in cell and animal studies) plus flavonoids. Lemon juice would add urinary citrate (kidney stone protection) but acidifies the oil rapidly and begins to curdle the feta over days of storage. If you want the citrate benefit, squeeze a lemon wedge over each serving rather than marinating with juice.
  • Celiac / GF: All ingredients are naturally gluten-free. Check your EVOO label (pure olive oil is always GF) and any packaged dried chili label — some flavoured or prepared chili products use flour as an anti-caking agent. Plain whole dried chilies from a spice shop are safe.
