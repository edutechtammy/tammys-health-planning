🧠 APOE4 priority recipe

This is the highest-DHA recipe in the library. Salmon is the single most important food for APOE4 carriers — DHA (docosahexaenoic acid) is the primary structural fatty acid of neuronal membranes, and APOE4 carriers have impaired endogenous DHA synthesis from ALA precursors. Pre-formed DHA from oily fish bypasses this conversion bottleneck entirely. The golden paste coating adds curcumin (amyloid-β aggregation inhibitor) and EVOO oleocanthal (neuroinflammation suppressor) to the same meal. Aim for 2–3 servings of oily fish per week as a minimum target.

Golden Paste Salmon — Turmeric, Black Pepper & EVOO

Salmon coated in a turmeric-black pepper-EVOO-garlic paste and baked or pan-seared until the coating sets into a fragrant golden crust. The simplest recipe in the library and, for APOE4 carriers, arguably the most important. The fat in the EVOO carries the fat-soluble curcumin directly into the fish during cooking. Black pepper's piperine raises curcumin bioavailability ~2000%. The salmon itself delivers the highest pre-formed DHA of any common food — the fatty acid that APOE4 carriers cannot synthesise efficiently and cannot afford to be deficient in. Zero-oxalate throughout. Ready in 25 minutes.

⏱ Prep: 10 min 🔥 Cook: 12–15 min ⏳ Total: ~25 min 🍽 Yield: 2 servings

DHA per serving context: Atlantic salmon (farmed) ≈ 1,800–2,200mg DHA per 150g serving. Sockeye salmon (wild) ≈ 1,100–1,400mg. Sardines ≈ 750–900mg per tin. The recommended intake for APOE4 carriers in cognitive protection research is 1,000–2,000mg DHA per day from food sources. One serving of this recipe covers that range. Sardines + eggs in other recipes supplement on non-salmon days.

Salmon itself has zero GI. If you log a food test, the variable is the side dish — use this as an opportunity to test a new low-oxalate grain or vegetable pairing rather than the salmon itself.

Lowest-oxalate recipe in the library. Salmon and EVOO are zero-ox. 👩‍🍳 Instructions
  1. 1
    Activate the garlic
    Crush 3 garlic cloves and rest for 5 minutes before combining with the other paste ingredients. Begin preheating the oven to 200°C / 400°F (or heat the pan) during this window.
  2. 2
    Make the golden paste
    Stir EVOO, turmeric, black pepper, lemon zest, lemon juice, salt, and optional spices together in a small bowl. Add the rested garlic. The EVOO solubilises the curcumin into the fat phase immediately — the paste should be a deep golden-orange colour. This is the correct form for maximum bioavailability.
  3. 3
    Dry and coat the salmon
    Pat fillets completely dry — surface moisture is the enemy of crust formation. Spread the golden paste over the flesh side evenly. Let rest 5 minutes while the oven finishes preheating.
  4. 4a
    Oven method (preferred)
    Bake paste-side up at 200°C / 400°F for 12–15 minutes depending on thickness. The paste sets and becomes fragrant; the flesh flakes at the thickest point when done. No flipping needed. Best for batch cooking — cook 4 fillets at once and refrigerate the extras.
  5. 4b
    Pan method (alternative)
    Heat a splash of EVOO in a non-stick or cast-iron pan over medium heat. Skin-side down first — 4–5 minutes until the skin is crispy and colour has crept halfway up. Flip carefully onto paste side — 2–3 minutes to form a golden crust. The Maillard reaction on the turmeric produces visible browning without degrading curcumin at these temperatures.
  6. 5
    Rest, plate, serve
    Rest 2 minutes. Ideal internal temperature: 52–55°C / 125–130°F (medium) — retains maximum omega-3 content and moisture. Plate with a lemon wedge and fresh herbs. Add side dish alongside.
Layered mechanism summary: Salmon DHA → pre-formed omega-3 bypasses APOE4-impaired ALA→DHA conversion → neuronal membrane structural support, reduces Aβ aggregation · EVOO oleocanthal → NF-κB / COX inhibition (neuroinflammation, hepatic inflammation) · EVOO oleic acid → hepatocyte insulin signalling → fatty liver + DP · Curcumin in EVOO fat phase → max bioavailability, NF-κB suppression, hepatic lipid metabolism · Piperine from black pepper → CYP1A inhibition, slows curcumin clearance → ~2000% bioavailability increase · Garlic allicin (5-min pre-rest) → AMPK, phase II liver enzymes, glutathione (MTHFR) · Lemon juice → urinary citrate (kidney stone). Every component is doing specific work.

Bok choyVery low oxalate, calcium-rich relative to other greens. Halve, sear cut-side down in EVOO for 3 min per side.
Roasted cauliflowerLow oxalate, low GI, holds roasting flavours well. Toss with EVOO, cumin, paprika — matches the golden paste profile.
AsparagusLow oxalate, B-vitamin profile (folate, B6) supports MTHFR methylation. Roast at 200°C for 10 min with EVOO.

⚠️ Avoid spinach (very high ox), Swiss chard (very high ox), and beet greens (very high ox) as sides with this recipe. These are incompatible with kidney stone management regardless of the main dish.

🔄 Variations

🧀 Add feta at serving (kidney stone) A small crumble of feta alongside adds calcium. Lemon juice in the paste already provides urinary citrate, but if the side dish has any moderate-ox content, feta covers it without disrupting the main flavour.
🌿 Ginger paste upgrade Add 1 tsp fresh grated ginger to the golden paste. Gingerols + curcumin are complementary anti-inflammatory pathways, both working in the EVOO fat phase. Adds brightness to the flavour profile alongside the lemon.
🐟 Sardine substitute (budget / convenience) When salmon isn't available, apply the golden paste to 2 tins of sardines (drained), spread on a baking tray, and warm in the oven for 5 minutes. DHA is lower (~750mg vs. 2000mg) but the therapeutic paste still delivers full curcumin + allicin benefit.
❄️ Batch cook for the week Double the recipe (4 fillets), bake all at once. Refrigerate extras for up to 3 days. Cold golden paste salmon over cucumber and kale makes a fast no-cook lunch — the paste flavour deepens overnight and the cold omega-3s remain fully intact.

🔬 Why each component is here

  • Why EVOO is in the paste and not used as a cooking spray: Curcumin is fat-soluble — it dissolves in lipids, not water. By combining turmeric directly with EVOO in the paste, the curcumin molecules are already dispersed in the fat before they contact heat. This means when the salmon cooks, the curcumin is carried through the flesh by the EVOO rather than sitting on the surface. Spraying cooking oil separately and adding dry turmeric on top creates a less efficient distribution of the active compound.
  • Why freshly ground black pepper — not pre-ground: Piperine is volatile and oxidises relatively quickly after grinding. Pre-ground pepper has meaningfully lower piperine content than freshly ground. For the ~2000% curcumin bioavailability enhancement, freshly ground pepper is correct. A small pepper grinder next to the spice rack is the only tool required.
  • Why skin-on salmon: The skin acts as a natural heat buffer — it protects the delicate omega-3 fatty acids in the flesh from direct high heat during pan-searing. DHA and EPA are polyunsaturated fats that oxidise readily when overheated. Skin-on also makes it easier to achieve a crispy exterior without overcooking the interior past the 52–55°C sweet spot. The skin itself is edible and contains collagen and additional omega-3s.
  • Why 52–55°C internal temperature: FDA recommends 63°C for food safety, which is fine. However, at 63°C salmon is fully opaque and firm throughout — and the DHA-rich cell membranes in the flesh have been exposed to more heat for longer. Medium (52–55°C) means the flesh is still slightly translucent at the very centre and noticeably more moist. If food safety is a concern (pregnancy, immunocompromise), cook to 63°C — the DHA reduction is not dramatic, and it is still far superior to any non-fish alternative.
  • Why EVOO oleocanthal matters for APOE4 specifically: APOE4 carriers have reduced efficiency in clearing amyloid-β (Aβ) plaques from the brain. Oleocanthal, a phenolic compound unique to EVOO, has been shown to upregulate the two primary Aβ clearance proteins (P-gp and LRP1) in the blood-brain barrier. This is a direct mechanistic target — not a general anti-inflammatory effect. The amount of oleocanthal in 2 tbsp of high-quality EVOO (the amount in this paste) is within the range used in research models. Quality matters: look for EVOO with a harvest date within the last 12 months and a peppery, slightly bitter finish (sign of high polyphenol content).
  • Why this recipe works as a liver recipe too: Fatty liver impairs beta-oxidation — the process by which hepatocytes burn fat for fuel. The omega-3s EPA and DHA from salmon directly activate PPAR-alpha, the nuclear receptor that upregulates beta-oxidation gene expression. Simultaneously, curcumin suppresses SREBP-1c (the transcription factor that drives de novo lipogenesis — new fat production in the liver). These are complementary mechanisms: omega-3s increase fat burning, curcumin reduces fat synthesis. Together in the same meal they work synergistically on the same pathway imbalance that creates fatty liver.
