🦠 The 8 Sugar Shift Strains — what each one is doing
🧬 Sugar Shift strain profile — why this blend is more targeted than standard yogurt cultures

Standard commercial yogurt uses 2 strains (L. bulgaricus and S. thermophilus) chosen for texture and mild acidity, not therapeutic function. Sugar Shift contains 8 strains with specific documented activities. The Prebio Plus feeds all of them during the 18-hour ferment, producing a higher viable count than capsule supplementation alone.

🥛 Why A2 Brand Milk (ultra-pasteurized) + cream + Prebio Plus

✅ A2 Brand Milk (ultra-pasteurized) — two reasons this is the right choice

A2 protein: Regular whole milk contains both A1 and A2 beta-casein. During digestion, A1 casein breaks down into beta-casomorphin-7 (BCM-7) — an opioid peptide that has been shown in some people to increase intestinal permeability. When the entire therapeutic purpose of strains like L. reuteri PCR07 and B. bifidum in this yogurt is to repair the gut lining (Celiac recovery, leaky gut, gut-liver axis), using A1-containing milk works against those strains. A2 milk removes BCM-7 entirely — the A2 protein structure does not produce it. For Celiac recovery and fatty liver in particular, this is a meaningful distinction, not a marketing one. The key to the benefits is pasture-fed. grain fed cows don't produce the C-15:0 that makes this milk so valuable to health. Most commercial milk is grain-fed cow milk. Milk from small scale farms is often grass-fed, so if A2 disappears from shelves look to local farmers selling thier excess milk.

Ultra-pasteurized: UHT milk has been heated to ~280°F — significantly higher than standard pasteurization (~160°F). This eliminates virtually all competing bacteria. The Sugar Shift strains have no competition during the 18-hour ferment. No pre-heating to 180°F and cooling is needed — skip that step entirely. The milk is already a clean fermentation medium. More consistent and predictable outcome batch to batch.

💡 The cream addition — why this yogurt is thicker and richer than standard

Adding 2 cups of cream to 6 cups of milk raises the fat percentage significantly above standard whole milk (roughly 3.5% fat → approximately 10–12% fat in this blend). Higher fat content means: thicker texture without straining, slower glucose absorption from any residual lactose, richer mouthfeel that makes it a genuinely satisfying food rather than a medicinal one. The cream is full-fat and unmodified — no low-fat substitution. The fat is doing the work.

✅ Prebio Plus — feeding the strains during fermentation

Prebio Plus provides prebiotic fibers (inulin-type fructans and related oligosaccharides) that selectively feed Bifidobacterium and Lactobacillus strains. Adding it to the ferment means the strains are feeding on a preferred food source during the full 18 hours — which increases the viable count in the finished yogurt compared to fermentation without prebiotics. This is a synbiotic approach: probiotic strains + their preferred prebiotic food in the same vessel at the same time.
🧼 Sterilization — the most important step

⚠️ Sterilize everything that touches the milk or yogurt

Jars, lids, whisk, spoons, ladles, measuring scoops — anything in contact with the milk before and during fermentation must be sterilized. A single non-sterile utensil introduces competing bacteria that can outcompete the Sugar Shift strains at 100°F, producing an off-flavour yogurt, a failed set, or occasionally a batch that smells wrong.
  • Dishwasher method: Run a full hot cycle with drying. Do not dry with a tea towel after — they carry bacteria. Retrieve directly from the dishwasher.
  • Boiling water method: Pour boiling water into each jar and over each utensil. Tip out and allow to air dry on a clean rack. 60 seconds of contact with boiling water is sufficient.
  • Do not use soap-rinsed equipment without fully rinsing: soap residue can inhibit culture activity. Rinse thoroughly after washing.

Essentially zero — whole milk, cream, and probiotic powder are all negligible oxalate sources. No dietary oxalate concern at any serving size.
🥛 Method
  1. 1
    Sterilize all equipment
    Jars, lids, whisk, measuring scoops, any utensils. Dishwasher hot cycle or boiling water rinse. Air dry — no tea towels.
  2. 2
    Combine A2 Brand Milk and cream — no heating needed
    Pour 6 cups A2 Brand Milk (ultra-pasteurized) and 2 cups heavy cream into the sterilized jar. No need to heat — A2 Brand Milk is already ultra-pasteurized, a clean medium.
  3. 3
    Add Sugar Shift + Prebio Plus
    Add 2 scoops Sugar Shift and 2 scoops Prebio Plus. Whisk thoroughly until fully dissolved with no visible powder clumps. Even distribution = even colonisation of the batch.
  4. 4
    Set Ferment Pro: 100°F / 18 hours
    Place the jar in the Ferment Pro. Set temperature to 100°F and timer to 18 hours. Seal and start. The unit will automatically shift to refrigeration mode when the timer completes.
  5. 5
    Do not disturb — 18 hours
    Leave completely undisturbed for the full 18 hours. Do not open, stir, or move the jar. The culture matrix needs stillness to set properly.
  6. 6
    Retrieve when ready — already chilled
    When you're ready, remove from the Ferment Pro. The yogurt is ready to eat or store. Refrigerator life: up to 2 weeks. For thicker texture, strain 2–4 hours (makes Sugar Shift labneh).

🍽️ How to use it — serving ideas

  • Plain with a drizzle of raw honey: The tanginess of an 18-hour ferment pairs well with a small drizzle of raw honey — the same combination used in the Labneh Berry Parfait (Recipe 30). The honey is negligible glycaemic load alongside the fat and protein of this yogurt.
  • With the Chop Chop Salad (Recipe 34): Use as the labneh component — spoon directly onto the salad in place of store-bought labneh or feta. The fresher, more complex flavour of the home-fermented yogurt elevates the salad.
  • Strain it into Sugar Shift labneh: Line a fine mesh strainer with butter muslin or a clean kitchen cloth. Pour the finished yogurt in and refrigerate for 2–4 hours for a thick, spreadable labneh consistency. 8–12 hours produces a firm, cream-cheese-style labneh. All the same therapeutic value with more concentrated protein per gram.
  • As a breakfast base: A cup of this yogurt with blueberries (75g) is a complete breakfast — probiotic delivery, APOE4-targeted pterostilbene, and a protein-fat base that buffers the Dawn Phenomenon glucose window effectively into late morning.
  • Hemp seed mix-in (B1 + complete protein + omega-3): Stir 2–3 tablespoons (20–30g) of hulled hemp seeds into the finished yogurt at serving. Hemp seeds are one of the better plant sources of Thiamine B1 (~0.24–0.36 mg per 30g serve, ~20–30% daily target) — relevant given that pre-diabetes and insulin resistance increase B1 demand by keeping PDH (pyruvate dehydrogenase) in constant use. Hemp also brings complete protein (all 9 essential amino acids), ~2.5g omega-3 ALA, and ~210mg magnesium per 30g serve. When this yogurt is your evening snack alongside the Benfotiamine supplement (Life Extension, taken with this meal for fat-soluble absorption), the hemp seeds add a meaningful food-source B1 contribution on top of the supplement dose. The mild, nutty flavour disappears easily into the yogurt. No preparation needed — stir in straight from the bag.
  • Ceylon cinnamon addition: A pinch of Ceylon cinnamon stirred in at serving adds GLUT4 activation for blood sugar management — the bedtime dose strategy from the supplement table works equally well as a yogurt stir-in. The flavour pairing works.
  • Starter for the next batch: Reserve 2–3 tablespoons of finished yogurt as a starter for the next batch in place of (or in addition to) the Sugar Shift powder. The strains multiply during the 18-hour ferment — re-using the yogurt as starter extends a batch economically. Re-powder with fresh Sugar Shift every 3–4 batches to keep the strain profile strong.
