Small-Batch Almond Oil Mayo

A low-noise homemade mayo made with almond oil rather than seed oils or extra virgin olive oil. It emulsifies quickly in a narrow jar with an immersion blender, and it avoids the bitterness that happens when EVOO is blended into a mayonnaise base. This version is clean, rich, and useful for dressings, burgers, tuna salads, and dipping.

⏱ 5 min prep 🥄 Makes ~1 cup ❄️ Keeps 1-2 weeks in fridge
Celiac / GF DP / Blood Sugar Kidney Stone
Net carbs: very low Oil choice: almond oil only

Important note

Do not use extra virgin olive oil here. It turns intensely bitter when emulsified by a blender, and the flavor overwhelms the mayo. Almond oil gives a clean, mild taste and a smooth final texture.
Ingredients
  • 1 largeegg, room temperature
  • 1 cupalmond oil
  • 1 tbsplemon juice or apple cider vinegar
  • Pinchsalt
Method
  1. 1
    Set up the jar
    Place the egg, almond oil, lemon juice or apple cider vinegar, and a pinch of salt in a tall, narrow jar or beaker that just fits the head of your immersion blender. Let it rest briefly so the egg settles at the bottom under the oil.
  2. 2
    Blend from the bottom
    Set the blender firmly at the bottom of the jar, turn it on high, and do not move it for 20 seconds. Keep it still until you see a thick white emulsion form at the base.
  3. 3
    Pull in the remaining oil
    Slowly tilt and raise the blender to pull the remaining oil down into the mix until it thickens completely. Taste and adjust with a tiny bit more salt or acid if needed.

Why almond oil

It is mild, neutral, and stable when emulsified. Extra virgin olive oil develops bitter compounds during blending and turns harsh in a mayo texture.

Texture tip

The blender should stay still at the bottom at first. The emulsion starts where the ingredients first meet, and moving too early breaks the process.
Ideas to Use It Before It Runs Out
  • Chimichurri Mayo / Herb Aioli Dip
    How to use: Blend 2 tbsp of your almond-oil mayo with fresh minced garlic, chopped parsley, cilantro, red wine vinegar, and a pinch of red pepper flakes.
    Protocol fit: Functions as a dip for cucumber slices, celery, or raw bell peppers, or as a rich steak topping for your deli roast beef or anniversary ribeye. Net carbs remain near zero.
  • Single-Serving Tuna or Salmon Salad
    How to use: Mix 1 tbsp mayo with 50 g of canned wild salmon or tuna, a dash of Dijon mustard, and finely diced celery or dill pickle.
    Protocol fit: Pairing clean protein with healthy monounsaturated fat keeps the protein portion small (under ~12 g) while delivering satiety without exogenous carbs.
  • Deviled Egg Yolks / Egg Salad Micro-Doses
    How to use: Mash 1 tbsp mayo into hard-boiled egg yolks with mustard and smoked paprika.
    Protocol fit: Divide into 20 g to 25 g micro-portions to stay well within your 2 to 5 g hourly protein target for the grazing queue.
  • Warm Broccoli / Asparagus Tartar Sauce
    How to use: Whisk 1 tbsp mayo with chopped capers, a squeeze of fresh lemon juice, and black pepper. Drizzle over warm steamed broccoli or grilled asparagus.
    Protocol fit: The acetic acid in lemon juice and capers aids digestive clearance, while the healthy fat coats non-starchy greens for gastric stretch.
  • Spicy Chipotle Dip for Raw Veggies
    How to use: Stir a pinch of chipotle powder or smoked paprika and a drop of lime juice into 1–2 tbsp of mayo.
    Protocol fit: Serves as an effortless, zero-carb dip for hourly vegetable rations like sliced English cucumber or radishes.
Notes

Use the mayo within 1-2 weeks and keep it refrigerated. You can also add a little garlic or Dijon if desired, but keep the base recipe simple so the almond oil flavor stays clean. This version is a good recipe to keep on hand when packaged mayo contains ingredients that trigger your symptoms or preferences.
