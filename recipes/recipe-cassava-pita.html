Cassava Flour Pita Bread (GF)

Gluten-free flatbread made from cassava flour, EVOO, water, and salt — cooked dry in a hot cast iron skillet in 2 minutes per side. Cassava is the closest GF flour to wheat in pliability: it folds and wraps without splitting, which is what makes it the right choice for gyros, mezze wraps, and scooping dips. Only four ingredients. No yeast, no rising, no oven. The whole batch is done in 30 minutes.

⏱ Prep: 15 min (incl. 10 min dough rest) 🔥 Cook: ~2 min per side in cast iron ⏳ Total: ~30 min 🍽 Makes: 6 pitas (~18 cm each) 📅 Best: same day — does not store well

🩺 How to run this food test — cassava pita standalone

  1. Choose a morning when you have a clean fasting baseline (no unusual overnight reading). Record fasting glucose before eating anything.
  2. Make a batch of pitas. Eat 2 plain pitas — no butter, no dip, no filling. Just pita and water. This isolates cassava as the only variable.
  3. Note the time you started eating. Record a T+60 reading and a T+120 reading on your CGM.
  4. Record how you feel: any GI discomfort, bloating, energy dip, or unusual hunger within 2 hours. Cassava is high starch — some people notice a hunger rebound at T+90 even if the glucose curve looks fine.
  5. Log the results in the food testing tracker. The key number is the T+120 return — whether glucose has come back close to fasting baseline by 2 hours.
✅ Good to proceed to gyros if:
T+120 returns within ~20–25 mg/dL of fasting baseline. No GI symptoms. No hunger rebound within 2 hours.
⚠️ Pause and reassess if:
T+120 is still elevated (>25 mg/dL above fasting). Noticeable GI bloating. Hunger returns strongly before 2 hours — may indicate rapid glucose clearance with reactive dip.
🧾 Ingredients — 6 pitas
  • Dough
  • 1 cup (120g)Cassava flour — spooned & levelled, not packed⬜ zero ox · GF
  • ¼ tspFine sea salt⬜ zero
  • ¼ tspGF baking powder (optional)⬜ slight lift
  • 3 tbspEVOO⬜ zero · pliability
  • ⅓ cupWarm water + 1–2 tbsp as needed⬜ adjust by brand
Cassava flour note: Measure by spooning into the cup and levelling — do not scoop directly or you pack too much flour and the dough cracks. The water amount varies by brand. Start with ⅓ cup and add by the teaspoon until the dough feels like smooth Play-Doh: not sticky, not crumbly.
👩‍🍳 Method
  1. 1
    Mix dry ingredients
    Whisk the cassava flour, salt, and baking powder (if using) in a medium bowl. Cassava flour clumps — whisking first breaks any lumps before liquid is added.
  2. 2
    Add fat, then water
    Add the EVOO and rub it into the flour with your fingertips until the texture resembles damp sand. Then add the warm water gradually — a little at a time — mixing until a smooth, soft dough ball forms. It should feel like Play-Doh: holds together, not sticky, not crumbly. Add water by the teaspoon if it's crumbling; dust in a pinch of flour if it's sticking.
  3. 3
    Rest 10 minutes — do not skip
    Cover the dough with a towel or invert the bowl over it. Leave at room temperature for 10 minutes. Cassava absorbs liquid slowly — this rest lets it fully hydrate. Dough rolled before resting cracks at the edges.
  4. 4
    Divide and roll thin
    Divide rested dough into 6 equal balls. Roll each between two sheets of parchment paper to roughly 18 cm (7 inches) wide and 3 mm (⅛ inch) thick. Thin = pliable. Thick = cracker. Edge cracks can be pinched back together — the dough is workable.
  5. 5
    Cook dry in a very hot pan
    Heat a cast iron skillet over medium-high heat until very hot — a drop of water should sizzle away immediately. Cook each pita dry, no oil, for 2 minutes per side. Light golden-brown spots will appear. Don't walk away — they cook fast.
  6. 6
    Stack under a towel immediately
    As each pita comes off the pan, place it on a plate and cover the stack with a clean towel. The trapped steam keeps the pitas soft and pliable. Left uncovered, they stiffen in minutes. Use the same day.
What this bread is and isn't: Cassava flour is almost pure starch from the cassava root — it has no gluten, no gums, no fibre to speak of. What it has is a starch structure that behaves more like wheat starch than any other GF flour: it binds when hydrated, stays pliable when warm, and folds without splitting. EVOO in the dough contributes oleocanthal and slows starch digestion slightly (fat-coating effect on amylose). The baking powder is optional — a pinch gives a small amount of lift that makes the pita a touch softer but is not needed for structure. This is a carbohydrate portion: the food test tells you whether your liver and pancreas handle cassava starch well before you start wrapping fillings in it.

🔬 Conditions — what to know

  • Celiac / GF: Cassava flour is naturally gluten-free — no wheat, barley, or rye contamination at source. Check your brand for "certified GF" labelling if you are highly sensitive to cross-contamination. Otto's brand is the most consistently sourced and widely available certified option.
  • Blood sugar / Dawn Phenomenon: Cassava is ~70% starch by dry weight — the glycaemic impact is real and individual. The whole reason to test this standalone is to find out where your personal curve lands before adding protein, fat, and sauces that will complicate the reading. A good T+120 return here means the starch alone is manageable; you then have the option to improve it further with a protein-and-fat-rich filling like gyro chicken which blunts the starch spike.
  • APOE4: Cassava is starch — neutral from an APOE4 perspective. No saturated fat concern, no oxidised lipid concern. The EVOO in the dough is a small positive. As a carbohydrate vehicle for lean protein (gyros), it is an acceptable use of a starch portion.
  • Fatty liver: Same as blood sugar note. Starch-heavy meals contribute to hepatic de novo lipogenesis if consumed in large quantities with poor glucose clearance. Two pitas as a wrap for a protein-dominant gyro is a different context from eating 4–5 plain pitas. The test establishes your baseline before you add that context.
  • Kidney stone: Cassava flour is very low oxalate — negligible contribution. No calcium pairing required. EVOO contributes zero oxalate. This is one of the cleaner GF bread options from a kidney stone management standpoint.
  • MTHFR: Cassava has no special interaction with methylation pathways. It is a neutral starch. The EVOO and any fillings will carry the functional value here.
  • Same-day use — why it goes stiff: Cassava starch retrogrades (recrystallises) as it cools, which is why pitas stiffen within hours. Refrigerating accelerates this dramatically — cold cassava pita becomes cracker-brittle. If you want to prepare ahead, make the dough balls, refrigerate them (covered), and roll and cook fresh. The dough keeps up to 24 hours refrigerated; the cooked pita does not.
