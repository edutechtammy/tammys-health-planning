Roasted Zucchini with Lemon-Garlic Parmesan Crust

Your original recipe — EVOO, garlic powder, salt, parmesan, 375°F — is already solid. This version keeps exactly that base and adds four targeted upgrades that work with the existing flavour rather than against it: lemon zest in the coating (urinary citrate, brightness), dried oregano (rosmarinic acid, anti-inflammatory), pumpkin seeds scattered on top for the last 10 minutes (zinc, magnesium, crunch), and a cold fresh-garlic lemon-EVOO drizzle at the finish so allicin is fully preserved. The garlic powder stays for roasting flavour. The fresh garlic is added cold at the end — same two-garlic approach used in the broccoli cheese sauce.

🥒 What zucchini is bringing to the table
🧬 Zucchini — underrated for all six conditions
🟢 Very low oxalate
~2–4mg/100g — one of the lowest-oxalate vegetables available. After transitioning off almonds and reducing other high-oxalate sources, zucchini is a vegetable you can eat freely without oxalate tracking.
🫀 Potassium — kidney stone protective
Potassium increases urinary citrate excretion, which inhibits calcium oxalate crystal formation. Same mechanism as avocado in the rice paper rolls. Combined with the lemon juice in the finish drizzle, you're getting the citrate benefit from two sources.
🧬 Folate — MTHFR relevant
Zucchini is a meaningful source of dietary folate. For MTHFR C677T, dietary folate supports the methylation cycle — each high-folate meal matters. Roasting preserves folate reasonably well compared to boiling (which leaches water-soluble B vitamins into the cooking water).
👁 Lutein + zeaxanthin — APOE4
Zucchini skin is a source of lutein and zeaxanthin, carotenoids concentrated in the retina and in brain tissue. APOE4 carriers have elevated oxidative stress in the brain; lutein has direct antioxidant activity in neural tissue. Don't peel the zucchini — the compounds are highest in the skin.
🩸 Very low GI — Dawn Phenomenon
Zucchini is predominantly water and fibre with very few digestible carbohydrates. Combined with the fat from EVOO and parmesan, this dish has a negligible glucose impact — making it a safe DP-friendly side at any meal including at dinner without affecting overnight insulin behaviour.
🫁 Gut motility — Celiac
The fibre in zucchini (primarily pectin and hemicellulose) provides gentle prebiotic substrate. Combined with the oregano (antimicrobial properties that support a healthy gut microbiome balance) and the cold garlic in the finish (allicin's direct antimicrobial and prebiotic activity), this dish supports the gut lining that Celiac disease compromises.
✨ What was added to your original recipe — and why each fits
🔧 Four upgrades — all work with the existing flavour, not against it

🍋 Lemon zest in the coating + fresh lemon juice in the finish drizzle

Lemon zest mixed into the EVOO coating before roasting adds a bright, slightly caramelised citrus note that is a natural partner for parmesan (the same pairing you'd find in Italian cooking). The fresh lemon juice in the cold drizzle at the end provides the urinary citrate benefit — citric acid is absorbed and excreted as citrate in urine, directly inhibiting calcium oxalate crystal formation. Zest before you juice the lemon — it's much easier.

🌿 Dried oregano in the coating

Oregano + parmesan + olive oil is a classic Italian flavour combination — this is not a flavour stretch at all. Dried oregano contains rosmarinic acid (same anti-inflammatory compound as in rosemary — APOE4 relevant for neuroinflammation reduction) and carvacrol (antimicrobial compound supporting gut microbiome balance, relevant post-Celiac). Half a teaspoon is enough to register in the flavour without dominating. Dried thyme is an equally good substitute if you prefer a slightly more subtle herb note.

🌱 Pumpkin seeds — last 10 minutes only

Scattered over the top after the first 20 minutes of roasting so they toast rather than burn. Pumpkin seeds are one of the best dietary sources of zinc and magnesium — both critical for APOE4 (zinc is required for amyloid-β clearance; magnesium deficiency accelerates cognitive decline) and for general metabolic function in DP and MTHFR. At ~17mg oxalate/100g, a 15–20g garnish adds only 2–3mg oxalate — negligible. The toasted seeds add a satisfying crunch to what is otherwise a soft, yielding vegetable. They catch and hold the parmesan as it melts.

🧄 Cold fresh-garlic finish drizzle

The garlic powder stays — it provides roasting flavour and browns slightly in the oven, creating depth. The fresh garlic is additional. Crush one clove, rest 5 minutes (the allicin-forming enzyme reaction that completes in this window), then combine with EVOO and fresh lemon juice cold. Drizzle over the hot zucchini straight from the oven — the heat blooms the garlic aroma without destroying the allicin. This is the same two-garlic approach used in the broccoli cheese sauce (Recipe 19): one garlic for cooking flavour, one cold for allicin.

🟡 Optional: nutritional yeast blended with the parmesan

OPTIONAL — flavour is mild when blended 50/50 with parmesan

Nutritional yeast has a savoury, slightly nutty, umami flavour that is close enough to parmesan that when the two are combined you taste parmesan — not nutritional yeast. The reason to add it: it's a concentrated source of B vitamins including folate and B12 (MTHFR, fatty liver), and it adds an extra layer of umami without any additional sodium or fat. Start with 1 tbsp nutritional yeast to 2 tbsp parmesan. If you find the flavour neutral (as most people do at this ratio), you can increase the yeast over time.

🌶 Black pepper in the coating

Already a natural addition to anything savoury but worth noting: piperine, the active compound in black pepper, increases the bioavailability of a wide range of compounds — including curcumin (if you later eat anything turmeric-based), beta-carotene, and several B vitamins. A quarter teaspoon in the coating costs nothing flavour-wise and pays a small but real absorption dividend.

  1. 1
    Preheat + prep the zucchini
    Preheat your oven to 190°C (375°F). Line a baking tray with baking paper. Cut each zucchini into spears: halve lengthways, then halve each half again lengthways, giving you 4 long spears per zucchini — 8 spears total. Aim for pieces that are roughly 8–12mm thick at the widest point. Alternatively, slice into rounds approximately 10mm thick — rounds work but have less surface area for the parmesan crust. Do not peel.
  2. 2
    Make the coating
    In a large bowl, combine 2 tbsp EVOO, garlic powder, lemon zest, dried oregano, salt, and black pepper. Mix well. Add the zucchini spears and toss until every surface is coated. Spread on the lined tray in a single layer, cut-side up — the flat cut faces up to receive the parmesan. No overlapping: if the pieces steam against each other they will go mushy rather than roast.
  3. 3
    Add the parmesan crust
    Combine the grated parmesan and nutritional yeast (if using) in a small bowl. Sprinkle evenly over the top of each spear. It will sit loosely at this point and bond to the surface as it melts during roasting.
  4. 4
    Roast for 20 minutes
    Place the tray in the preheated oven on a middle or upper rack. Roast for 20 minutes until the parmesan is golden and the cut surfaces of the zucchini are beginning to brown at the edges. The zucchini should still have some firmness at this point — not collapsing yet.
  5. 5
    Add pumpkin seeds — back in for 8–12 more minutes
    Pull the tray out briefly. Scatter the raw pumpkin seeds evenly over the top of the zucchini spears — they'll nestle into the softened parmesan crust. Return to the oven for a further 8–12 minutes. Check at 25 minutes total: you want the seeds to be lightly golden and slightly puffed, the parmesan a deep golden, and the thin edges of the zucchini just caramelised. Every oven is different — at 25 minutes assess and decide whether another 3–5 minutes is needed. Watch closely after 25 minutes as they can go from perfect to overdone quickly at this stage.
  6. 6
    Crush garlic while the oven does its work
    While the zucchini is in its final 10 minutes, crush the fresh garlic clove and set aside to rest for 5 minutes. This window lets the alliin-alliinase enzyme reaction complete and allicin form fully. Combine the rested garlic with 1 tbsp EVOO and 1 tbsp fresh lemon juice in a small bowl. Do not add heat at any point — this drizzle stays cold throughout.
  7. 7
    Remove from oven and finish cold
    Take the tray out of the oven. Immediately drizzle the cold garlic-lemon-EVOO mixture over all the pieces. The residual heat of the zucchini will bloom the garlic aroma without cooking off the allicin. Serve directly from the tray or transfer to a plate. Best eaten within a few minutes — the parmesan crust softens as it sits.

🔪 Cut thickness matters more than you'd expect

  • Spears (recommended for this recipe): Quarter each zucchini lengthways. More flat surface area means more contact with the parmesan crust and more caramelisation. The flat face sits up and holds the seeds. Best presentation too — you can see the green skin edge alongside the golden crust.
  • Rounds (10mm): Work well, cook slightly faster, easier to eat as finger food. The parmesan doesn't form as continuous a crust. Good for serving alongside a dip (like labneh) where you want individual pieces to pick up.
  • Thinner than 8mm: Will dry out and go papery rather than tender in the centre. The parmesan crust is done before the zucchini has cooked through, leading to burnt crust and hard texture.
  • Thicker than 12mm: The inside stays soft and the outside may not caramelise enough. You end up with steamed texture rather than roasted. Fine if that's your preference, but allow 5–8 extra minutes and don't expect as much browning.
  • Water content note: Zucchini is ~95% water. Some people salt it before cooking to draw out moisture (15–20 minutes, then pat dry). This does improve browning but adds a step. At 190°C with plenty of tray space and pieces not overlapping, you get good results without pre-salting.
🍽 What to serve it with
🐟 Baked or pan-seared salmon Natural Mediterranean pairing. Serve the zucchini alongside the salmon — the lemon-garlic drizzle bridges both.
🥚 Scrambled or poached eggs Good breakfast or lunch plate. The parmesan crust on the zucchini echoes egg flavours. Keep eggs soft — don't overcook.
🧈 With labneh as a dip The tart labneh (sour cream style) cuts through the richness of the parmesan. Serve spears standing up with the labneh alongside.
🥗 Over leafy greens Serve the hot zucchini over rocket or spinach — the heat wilts the greens slightly and the lemon drizzle doubles as a dressing. Add cherry tomatoes.
🍗 Alongside roast chicken A classic roasting-tray pairing. The garlic-lemon-parmesan profile works with almost any roast protein.
🌯 Inside rice paper rolls Cool the roasted zucchini spears completely and use as a warm-flavoured filling in the rice paper rolls (Recipe 23). The parmesan crust adds an unexpected savoury layer.
