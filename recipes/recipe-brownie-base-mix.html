Bulk Brownie Base Mix for Gluten-Free Flour Testing

A large-batch dry mix that removes the need to measure cocoa, sugar, milk powder, salt, and leavening every time you want to test a new gluten-free flour combination. Dutched cocoa (Hershey's Special Dark) provides deep chocolate flavor with a neutral pH that won't react with baking powder. The mix is shelf-stable and portions out at exactly 73.4g per single-egg batch — since eggs can't be easily scaled except in whole units, the single-egg batch becomes the natural testing unit for systematic flour experimentation. Standardized fudgy brownie technique ensures consistent results across all flour tests.

⏱ 10 min prep + 10 min rest + 2 hr chill 📦 Makes 1116g base mix 🍫 Yields ~15 single-egg test batches 🔥 32–36 min bake at 325°F
Celiac / GF Base
🥄 Base Mix Ingredients
Dry Ingredients (Total: 1116g)
  • 856g Granulated sugar
  • 160g Hershey's Special Dark Dutched Cocoa
  • 82g Dry milk powder
  • 14g Salt
  • 4g Double-acting baking powder
Method
  1. 1
    Combine & Whisk
    Measure all ingredients into a large bowl. Whisk thoroughly to break up cocoa lumps and ensure even distribution of the baking powder and salt throughout the mix.
  2. 2
    Store
    Transfer to an airtight container. Label with the date and ingredient list. Store at room temperature. Shelf-stable for several months.
  3. 3
    Portion for Testing
    For a single-egg test batch, measure out exactly 73.4g of base mix. This ratio is designed around 1 egg as the anchor ingredient — eggs can't be easily scaled except by whole units, making this the natural testing portion size. Record which flour blend you use for each test.
🧪 Ingredient Notes

Dutched Cocoa

Hershey's Special Dark is alkalized (dutched), giving it a neutral pH that won't react with baking powder during storage. Natural cocoa is acidic and would activate the baking powder over time.

Dry Milk Powder

Adds richness and helps with browning. If dairy-sensitive, you can substitute coconut milk powder at the same weight. This will slightly alter the flavor but maintains the texture.

Single-Egg Batch Logic

The 73.4g portion size is calculated around 1 egg as the anchor ingredient. Eggs can't be fractionally scaled like liquids or powders, so single-egg batches become the most practical testing unit for small-scale flour experimentation.

Why Tapioca Starch?

60g of tapioca starch is standardized across all test batches because it creates a tender, fudgy crumb without masking the characteristics of the test flour. Tapioca's neutrality allows cassava, millet, or sorghum to express their unique textures.

The Chill Makes It Fudgy

The 2-hour+ refrigeration isn't optional — it's what transforms the texture from cakey to fudgy. Chilled fats set up differently when baked, and full starch hydration before baking maximizes moisture retention.

5×7 Glass Dish Standard

All tests use the same 5×7 inch glass casserole dish. Glass bakes more evenly at low temperatures than metal, and the standardized size keeps thickness consistent across all flour tests for fair comparison.

🌾 Gluten-Free Flour Testing Strategy

This base mix allows you to isolate the variable you're testing — the flour. By keeping the cocoa, sugar, leavening, milk powder, and tapioca starch (60g) constant across all tests, you can directly compare how different GF flours perform: cassava for chewiness, millet for nuttiness, sorghum for structure, or custom blends. The single-egg batch size (73.4g base mix) makes testing economical — you're not committing to a large batch until you've verified the flour blend works. The standardized fudgy brownie method (sugar-egg meringue, 10-minute flour rest, 2-hour+ chill, 325°F bake) ensures that texture differences come from the flour being tested, not from inconsistent technique. Measure all flours by weight (not volume) for consistency. Start with 30–40g of test flour per batch.

📝 Usage Notes
Single-Egg Test Batch — Standardized Fudgy Brownie Protocol

For each single-egg test batch using 73.4g of this base mix, add:

  • • 73.4g brownie base mix (measured from this bulk batch)
  • • 1 large egg — the anchor ingredient (cannot be easily scaled)
  • • 60.0g tapioca starch/flour — standardized across all tests to maximize fudginess
  • • Test flour(s) — type and amount listed in the testing log below (cassava, millet, sorghum, oat, almond, chickpea, rice flours, or blends)
  • • 1/4 cup (60ml) neutral oil — avocado, light olive, or melted coconut oil
  • • 1/2 tsp vanilla extract
  • • Chocolate chips to taste (optional; use GF-certified chips if needed)

🎯 Fudgy Brownie Assembly Method

  1. Create sugar-egg meringue base: In a bowl, whisk together the 73.4g brownie base mix and 1 egg vigorously until you create a light, fluffy meringue-like texture. This incorporates air and dissolves the sugar into the egg.
  2. Add vanilla and flour, then rest: Add the vanilla extract, 60g tapioca starch, and your test flour (see testing log for specific flour and amount). Stir to combine. Let the mixture sit for 10 minutes — this allows the flours to absorb the liquid before the oil coats them, maximizing moisture retention.
  3. Add oil and chocolate chips: After the 10-minute rest, add the 1/4 cup oil and stir until just combined. Fold in chocolate chips if using. The batter will be thick.
  4. Pour and chill: Pour batter into a greased 5×7 inch glass casserole dish (standardized across all tests). Cover and refrigerate for 2 hours to overnight. This chilling step maximizes starch hydration, chills the fats to swing texture from cakey to fudgy, and allows sugars to migrate to the surface for a shiny, crackled top.
  5. Bake low and slow: Preheat oven to 325°F (163°C). Bake chilled batter for 32–36 minutes. The low temperature coaxes gentle leavening and even baking in glass, maximizing fudginess. Test doneness with a butter knife — it may not come out completely clean due to the fudgy texture, but should not have wet batter clinging.
  6. Cool completely: Allow brownies to cool completely in the pan before cutting. This allows the fudgy center to fully set. Cooling takes 1–2 hours at room temperature.

Testing note: Record the test flour type, amount, actual bake time, texture notes, and how the crackled surface developed. The tapioca starch (60g) and chilling step are constants across all tests — only the test flour changes.
