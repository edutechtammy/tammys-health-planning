Herb-Crusted Baked Chicken Breast (GF)

Chicken breast coated in a gluten-free herb crust — millet breadcrumbs (from your own Olive & Feta bread) or almond meal, with fresh parsley, thyme, lemon zest, and garlic — baked at 220°C until the crust is golden and the breast is moist. The crust does two things: it insulates the breast from direct oven heat (more even cooking, less drying) and creates a flavour layer that makes plain baked chicken a different dish entirely. Parsley is a deliberate MTHFR folate choice at ~177mcg per 100g. Millet crumbs use your existing bread as the base — nothing extra to buy. This is the chicken strips format: slice after resting and serve over quinoa, alongside guacamole, or on its own with roasted asparagus.

⚠️ Parsley is the folate booster here — 3 tbsp fresh flat-leaf parsley per serve contributes ~35µg DFE, which is unusually high for an herb quantity. This is why the recipe specifies do not skip the parsley. Chicken breast is a clean high-selenium, high-choline protein.

🍞 Millet crumbs vs almond meal — choosing your crust

✅ Millet crumbs (recommended) Made from your existing Olive & Feta Cheese Bread — no extra ingredients. Lighter crust, crispier texture, closer to traditional breadcrumbed chicken. GI contribution from the crumbs is small (40g per 2 servings, and baking reduces the available starch). Best for the "chicken strips" slicing format.
🌰 Almond meal (lower GI alternative) Negligible GI — almond meal is ~50% fat and ~20% protein with minimal digestible carbohydrate. Produces a denser, more textured crust that is slightly chewier and less crispy. Better if you are in an active glucose-monitoring phase and want to eliminate even the small crumb GI contribution.
📏 Crust thickness matters Press the mixture firmly onto the chicken — a thin even layer is better than a thick uneven one. Thick patches take longer to brown and may still be wet when the thin areas are perfect. Aim for 3–4mm consistent coverage.
🥚 Why egg as binder (not EVOO alone) EVOO on the surface of the chicken is too slick for the dry crumb mixture to adhere to cleanly. Egg white's protein network sets during baking, forming a firm bond between the chicken surface and the crust. Without it, the crust separates and slides off when sliced.

The millet crumb crust contributes ~20g millet crumbs per serving — low quantity, low GI risk. If uncertain, use almond meal crust instead (negligible GI). Food-test with the full meal if serving over millet or quinoa.

To make millet crumbs: Leave 2–3 slices of Olive & Feta Cheese Bread uncovered for a day to dry out slightly, or dry in a 120°C oven for 5 min. Pulse in a food processor to coarse crumbs. Store in an airtight container for up to 3 days.
👩‍🍳 Instructions
  1. 1
    Preheat and activate finishing garlic
    Set oven to 220°C (200°C fan). Crush the finishing garlic cloves and rest 5 minutes. Prepare millet crumbs if not already done (see note in ingredients panel).
  2. 2
    Make the crust mixture
    Combine millet crumbs (or almond meal), finely chopped parsley, thyme, lemon zest, minced garlic, 1 tbsp EVOO, salt, and pepper. Mix until the herbs are evenly distributed and the mixture holds loosely when pressed. The parsley should be finely chopped — coarse pieces steam rather than bake and produce wet patches in the crust.
  3. 3
    Coat the chicken
    Pat each breast completely dry. Beat the egg. Brush or dip each breast in beaten egg (top and sides). Press the herb crust mixture firmly onto the egg-coated surface — aim for an even 3–4mm layer. Press down firmly so it adheres. Place crust-side up on a lined baking tray.
  4. 4
    Drizzle and bake
    Drizzle the remaining 1 tbsp EVOO over the crust of both breasts. Bake 20–22 minutes at 220°C until the crust is deep golden and the internal temperature reaches 74°C. If the crust is browning too fast, cover very loosely with foil for the last 5 minutes. Do not cover from the start — the steam will make the crust soggy.
  5. 5
    Finish with activated garlic, lemon, and rest
    Remove from oven. Scatter the activated garlic over the hot crust — press lightly so it sticks. Squeeze over lemon juice. Rest 5 minutes. Slice across the grain to serve as strips, keeping the crust intact on each slice. Serve over quinoa, alongside guacamole, or with roasted asparagus.

🍽 Serving format — the chicken strips use cases

🥑 Sliced over quinoa with guacamole The Thursday lunch format. Slice on an angle, fan over rinsed quinoa with cucumber and bell pepper, add guacamole alongside. The herb crust adds a textural layer that plain chicken over quinoa lacks. A complete MUFA + protein + resistant starch meal.
🌿 With roasted asparagus Both bake at 220°C — put the asparagus tray in 10 minutes after the chicken. They finish together. The herb crust complements asparagus's light flavour without overpowering it.
🥗 Cold over salad (next day) Slice cold and serve over romaine, cucumber, and bell pepper with EVOO and lemon. The crust holds its texture reasonably well overnight in the fridge — better than any plain baked breast cold. This is the Wednesday lunch (eggs + sliced chicken) elevated.
🥬 With kale sautéed in EVOO Tuesday dinner format. The parsley folate in the crust + kale folate in the sauté = two concentrated MTHFR folate sources in the same meal alongside the EVOO MUFA from both the crust and the kale cooking.
🍞 As the sourdough sandwich filling Slice thin and use as a filling for the Olive & Feta Cheese Bread — with guacamole as the spread and cucumber slices. The millet-crumb crust on the chicken becomes the structural element. A complete portable lunch.
🫙 Alongside sauerkraut The herb flavour profile works well against sauerkraut's acidity. The crust adds satisfying texture to what would otherwise be a soft-textured plate. The parsley + sauerkraut ferment combination provides both folate and live cultures in a single meal.

🔬 Why parsley is not optional — MTHFR folate context

  • Parsley folate at therapeutic quantities: Fresh flat-leaf parsley contains approximately 177mcg of natural folate per 100g. Three tablespoons of finely chopped parsley (approximately 12–15g) provides around 20–25mcg folate — a meaningful contribution to the daily folate load for MTHFR. This is not equivalent to a supplement but it represents the kind of accumulated daily folate delivery from whole foods that, over weeks, makes a measurable difference to methylation cycle substrate availability. The meal plan uses parsley, coriander, and kale consistently across recipes specifically for this reason.
  • Natural folate vs synthetic folic acid: MTHFR C677T impairs the conversion of synthetic folic acid (used in fortified foods and most supplements) into 5-methyltetrahydrofolate. Natural dietary folate (from parsley, dark greens, asparagus, eggs) enters the methylation cycle at a different point that partially bypasses the impaired enzyme. This does not mean supplements are wrong — 5-MTHF supplementation (the active form) bypasses the enzyme entirely. But it does mean that accumulating natural folate from multiple sources throughout the day has independent value even when supplementing.
  • Why two garlics (crust garlic + finishing garlic): Same principle as the kefir marinade recipe — these are different functions. The garlic minced into the crust bakes in for background flavour throughout the meat. It is not the allicin delivery — allicin is completely destroyed within 2 minutes at 220°C. The finishing garlic (5-minute pre-rest, scattered onto the hot crust post-bake) is the allicin delivery. Using only crust garlic gives you flavour but no allicin. Using only finishing garlic gives you allicin but a slightly blander crust. Both together give you both outcomes simultaneously at zero additional cost.
  • Celiac: confirming the crust is truly GF: Millet is naturally gluten-free. However, millet bread can be contaminated if produced in a facility that also handles wheat. If you bake the Olive & Feta Cheese Bread yourself from certified GF millet and sorghum flour (as the recipe specifies), the crumbs from your own loaf are guaranteed gluten-free. Almond meal is naturally GF with essentially no contamination risk. Either crust option is safe for active Celiac disease — the key is confirming the source of your millet crumbs.
