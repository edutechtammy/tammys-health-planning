Shakshuka — Eggs in Spiced Tomato-Pepper Sauce

Eggs poached in a rich, deeply spiced tomato and roasted pepper sauce with 5-minute activated garlic, turmeric-black pepper, cumin, and smoked paprika. Naturally gluten-free, protein-dense, and ready in 35 minutes. The whole egg delivers phosphatidylcholine for liver fat export, garlic activates AMPK, turmeric targets hepatic inflammation — and the feta at the finish handles the tomato oxalate load with calcium binding. Works for breakfast, lunch, or a fast dinner.

⏱ Prep: 10 min 🔥 Cook: 25 min ⏳ Total: ~35 min 🍽 Yield: 2 servings

👩‍🍳 Instructions
  1. 1
    Activate the garlic first
    Crush or finely chop all 4 garlic cloves. Set aside for 5 minutes. This is the most important timing step — alliinase is destroyed by heat and allicin only forms during this pre-heat rest. Start the onion and pepper while the garlic sits.
  2. 2
    Sauté onion and pepper
    Heat EVOO in a wide pan over medium heat. Add onion and red bell pepper. Cook 7–8 minutes, stirring occasionally, until softened and the onion is translucent. The fat from EVOO will carry the spices in the next step.
  3. 3
    Add garlic and bloom spices
    Add the rested garlic. Stir 1 minute until fragrant. Add turmeric, black pepper, cumin, smoked paprika, cayenne (if using), and salt. Stir and cook 60 seconds — the fat blooms the fat-soluble curcumin and spice compounds, intensifying flavour and bioavailability simultaneously.
  4. 4
    Add tomatoes and simmer
    Pour in crushed tomatoes. Stir to combine and reduce heat to medium-low. Simmer uncovered for 10 minutes, stirring occasionally, until the sauce thickens and deepens in colour. Taste and adjust salt.
  5. 5
    Poach the eggs
    Use a spoon to press 4 shallow wells in the sauce. Crack one egg into each well. Cover with a lid and cook on low for 5–8 minutes: 5 min = runny yolk, 8 min = set. Check at 5 and continue to your preference. The yolk is where the phosphatidylcholine lives — don't overcook if fatty liver is a concern.
  6. 6
    Finish and serve
    Scatter crumbled feta over the top (the calcium in feta binds residual dietary oxalate from the tomato). Add fresh parsley or coriander, squeeze a lemon wedge over each portion, and serve directly from the pan. Pair with GF bread or millet slices if adding a carb component.
Layered mechanism summary: Whole eggs → phosphatidylcholine (VLDL-mediated liver fat export, directly targets fatty liver) · Garlic 5-min rest → allicin (AMPK, glutathione induction for MTHFR) · Turmeric bloomed in EVOO → curcumin + piperine + fat carrier (APOE4 neuroprotection, hepatic NF-κB suppression) · Smoked paprika + cumin → anti-inflammatory phenolics · Feta finish → calcium binds tomato oxalate in gut before renal absorption · Lemon → urinary citrate (kidney stone prevention). No step is decorative.

🔄 Variations

🐟 Sardine Shakshuka (APOE4 upgrade) Add 1 tin of sardines (drained) to the sauce before poaching the eggs. The sardine calcium also contributes to oxalate binding. Reduce eggs to 2 if adding sardines to keep portions balanced. DHA + eggs = maximum APOE4 brain support in one dish.
🧀 Dairy-free calcium swap Replace feta with sardines-with-bones as the calcium source, or serve alongside 4 oz A2 whole milk. The pairing must happen at the same meal to be effective — calcium needs to be present in the gut at the same time as the oxalate.
🫑 Add kale (MTHFR folate) Stir a large handful of finely chopped kale into the sauce 5 minutes before adding the eggs. Kale is high in folate (directly supports MTHFR methylation), vitamin K2, and low-oxalate compared to spinach. Do not use spinach — very high oxalate.
🌿 Make it a DP dinner Serve as the main at dinner with no added carb on the side. The protein and fat from eggs + feta will keep the glucose flat through the night, reducing the amplitude of the Dawn Phenomenon glucose rise by morning. Log a food test to confirm your personal response.

🔬 Why each component is here

  • Eggs — soft-to-medium yolk preferred: Egg yolks contain phosphatidylcholine, the primary substrate for VLDL assembly in the liver. VLDL exports packaged triglycerides out of hepatocytes — impaired VLDL assembly is one mechanism through which fat accumulates in fatty liver. Cook yolks to your preference but note that very hard yolks oxidise some of the delicate phospholipids.
  • Garlic — always the 5-minute pre-rest: Alliinase is a heat-labile enzyme. Once garlic hits a hot pan, the conversion of alliin to allicin stops. The 5-minute room-temperature rest after crushing ensures maximum allicin yield before any heat exposure. Three AMPK activators are in this dish (garlic allicin, turmeric curcumin, EVOO oleic acid) — each targets a different pathway node.
  • Turmeric bloomed in EVOO at the spice step: Curcumin is fat-soluble. Cooking it briefly in EVOO with black pepper (piperine) before the tomatoes go in is not just flavour development — it maximises bioavailability before the water-based tomato liquid dilutes the fat phase.
  • Feta at the finish — not during cooking: Calcium binds oxalate in the gut as an insoluble complex that passes through without absorbing. This binding happens in the digestive tract, so the calcium must arrive at the same meal. Adding feta cold at the end preserves its texture and live culture content (if using traditional feta). Do not omit if kidney stone prevention is a priority.
  • Smoked paprika and cumin: Paprika (from red peppers) is a source of capsanthin — an anti-inflammatory carotenoid with direct antioxidant activity. Cumin has been shown to modestly improve fasting glucose and insulin sensitivity in small trials. Both are low-oxalate. Both bloom well in EVOO at medium heat.
  • No spinach: Traditional shakshuka sometimes includes spinach. Spinach is very high oxalate (~755mg per cooked cup) — incompatible with kidney stone management. Kale is the substitution if you want added greens (low oxalate, high folate). Red bell pepper already provides vitamin C and carotenoids from a low-oxalate source.
