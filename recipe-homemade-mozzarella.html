30-Minute Homemade Mozzarella (Farm Fresh Milk, Low-Sodium)

Fresh mozzarella from Farm Fresh Milk in 30 minutes — citric acid, liquid rennet, and your hands. Traditional Italian-style: whole milk gives a creamier result and about 10–15% more yield per litre than part-skim. No added salt unless you choose it — salt in fresh mozzarella is purely flavour, not preservation or structural. The fat in mozzarella is predominantly odd-chain C15:0 and monounsaturated oleic acid — not the even-chain palmitic acid (C16:0) that drives the APOE4 and Fatty Liver saturated fat concern. Farm Fresh whole milk also maximises C15:0 content. The citric acid contributes a small amount of dietary citrate (kidney stone benefit). The whey byproduct has the same B2/MTHFR value as labneh whey — reserve it. Three variations on this page: the plain fresh cheese, a herb & garlic EVOO marinade, and baked mozzarella sticks using millet breadcrumbs (GF, not deep-fried).

🛒 Two things you need that aren't in a normal kitchen

Liquid rennet Available at health food stores, specialty food stores, and online (cheese-making suppliers). Liquid rennet is more reliable than tablet rennet for beginners. Store in the fridge after opening — it lasts months. A small bottle makes many batches. Vegetable rennet is fine and GF.
Citric acid (food grade) Available at health food stores, some supermarkets (baking aisle), Indian grocery stores, and online. Also sold as a natural preservative and descaler. Extremely cheap and a small packet lasts a very long time. Confirm food-grade on the label.
⚠️ Milk must not be UHT UHT milk will not form curds — the proteins are permanently denatured by the ultra-high heat treatment. Use Farm Fresh Milk from the local farmstand — it is standard pasteurised and will curd perfectly. Important: the Walmart A2 Brand Milk is ultra-pasteurized (UHT) and will not work for this recipe, regardless of how much citric acid or rennet you add.
Filtered water for dissolving Use filtered or bottled water when dissolving both the citric acid and the rennet. Chlorine in tap water can interfere with curd formation. The milk itself can be tap-sourced — it's only the dissolving water that matters.
🧾 Ingredients — base mozzarella
  • Cheese base
  • 1 litreFarm Fresh Milk (pasteurised — local farmstand; do not use Walmart A2 Brand, which is UHT and will not curd)
  • 1.5 tspCitric acid (food grade)
  • ¼ tspLiquid rennet (vegetable rennet is fine)
  • 120mlFiltered water — divided (60ml for each)
  • Optional
  • ¼ tspFine sea salt — knead in during stretching if desired
🥛 Kefir-milk variation (adds lactic tang)
Replace 200–250ml of the Farm Fresh Milk with plain homemade kefir (keep the remaining ~750–800ml as Farm Fresh Milk). Do not use 100% kefir — kefir at pH ~4.2–4.5 is too acidic for rennet to form stretchable curds; you'd get paneer-style crumbles instead. At the 20–25% ratio, kefir's natural lactic acid partially replaces the citric acid dose: start with 1 tsp citric acid instead of 1.5 tsp, then use the pH probe at Step 1 to confirm you're at 5.9–6.2 before proceeding — kefir acidity varies enough batch-to-batch that the probe replaces guesswork here. The lactic acid and fermentation flavour compounds survive the 85°C stretch step — live organisms die but the acid stays — giving the finished cheese a genuine tang. Particularly useful when using mozzarella in the Olive & Feta Cheese Bread as a lower-sodium feta substitute, where the olives and garlic cover the salt gap.
📡 pH probe care — between steps
Rinse the probe tip thoroughly with filtered or distilled water (not tap — chlorine can affect readings) before each dip and immediately after. Shake or blot dry — do not rub the tip. Do not leave the probe submerged between checks; remove it as soon as the reading stabilises. Between batches, return the probe to its storage solution (or a small amount of pH 4 buffer if storage solution is empty) to keep the glass electrode hydrated. A dry electrode drifts and gives false readings. Calibrate against a known buffer solution (pH 4 and pH 7) if readings seem off by more than ±0.2.

🔧 Troubleshooting

Milk won't set
Milk is UHT — the Walmart A2 Brand Milk is UHT and cannot be used here. Or rennet was added too hot (above 49°C kills it). Start over with Farm Fresh Milk from the local farmstand.
Curd won't stretch
Not hot enough — microwave another 30 sec. Also check pH of pooled whey (target 5.0–5.3) — above 5.4 means insufficient acidification, not just temperature. See pH check #3 in Step 5.
Grainy texture
Overcooked or overstretched. Work quickly and stop once glossy.
Very little curd
Expect ~270–290g from 1L Farm Fresh Milk. Significantly less usually means the milk wasn't whole fat (accidentally bought reduced fat), or the milk was close to expiry.
👩‍🍳 Base method — 30 minutes
  1. 1
    Dissolve citric acid into cold milk
    Dissolve 1.5 tsp citric acid in 60ml cold filtered water. Pour into a large heavy-bottomed pot. Add the cold Farm Fresh Milk and stir to combine. Starting cold is important — adding citric acid to warm milk can cause premature clumping.
    📡 pH probe check #1 — before heating
    Rinse probe tip with filtered water, shake dry, then dip into the cold milk. Wait for the reading to stabilise (~10–15 sec). Target: pH 5.9–6.2.
    — Above 6.2: dissolve an extra ⅛ tsp citric acid in 1 tbsp filtered water, stir in, recheck.
    — Below 5.8: the milk is too acidic — curds will form prematurely during heating. Add a small splash of fresh cold milk to dilute slightly and recheck.
    — Kefir-milk batches vary: your kefir's acidity changes batch-to-batch (24-hour vs 36-hour kefir). This is the checkpoint where you adjust citric acid dose precisely rather than guessing. Aim for 5.9–6.0 for the kefir variation.
  2. 2
    Heat to 32°C — check pH and temperature together before rennet
    Heat over medium-low to 32°C (90°F), stirring gently. Before adding rennet: rinse probe with filtered water, dip into the warm milk — reading should still be pH 5.9–6.2. Temperature and pH together determine whether rennet will work. Remove from heat. Dissolve ¼ tsp liquid rennet in 60ml cold filtered water. Add to the milk and stir with a gentle up-and-down motion for exactly 30 seconds. Stop. Cover. Do not disturb for 5 minutes. Rinse probe immediately after use — don't leave it sitting in the warm pot.
  3. 3
    Cut the curd
    The milk should have set into a firm gel that pulls away from the sides cleanly. Cut into 2cm cubes with a long knife — straight vertical cuts in a grid, then angled cuts. Uniform pieces release whey evenly.
  4. 4
    Heat to 41°C and drain
    Return to medium-low heat, stirring gently, to 41°C (105°F). Curds will shrink and firm. Remove from heat. Scoop curds into a microwave-safe bowl with a slotted spoon. Reserve all the whey in a jar.
  5. 5
    Microwave and stretch
    Microwave curds on high 1 minute. Drain excess whey. Wearing heatproof gloves, stretch and fold the mass repeatedly — it should pull like taffy. If it tears, microwave another 30 seconds. Stretch until the surface is smooth and glossy. Knead in ¼ tsp salt here if using.
    📡 pH probe check #3 — if curd won't stretch
    If the curd tears instead of stretching despite being hot enough, check the pH of the pooled whey at the bottom of the bowl (not the curd itself — the probe reads liquid more accurately). Target: pH 5.0–5.3.
    — Above 5.4: the curd hasn't acidified enough to reach the stretch window. Dissolve ⅛ tsp citric acid in 1 tbsp filtered water, drizzle over the curd, fold it in, and microwave another 30 seconds. Recheck.
    — Below 4.8: over-acidified — the curds will be soft and slightly sour. Still usable but won't have the classic elastic pull. Use in the bread loaf or marinade rather than as fresh balls.
    — This check is especially useful for kefir batches where starting pH was close to the lower edge of the target range.
  6. 6
    Shape and set in ice water
    For a ball: fold into a smooth round and drop into ice water 5 minutes.
    For sticks: roll into a log ~3cm diameter on a clean surface, cut into 10cm lengths, drop into ice water. See the sticks section below for the full baked sticks method.

💧 Reserve the mozzarella whey — same value as labneh whey

  • B2 (riboflavin) — MTHFR: Mozzarella whey carries the water-soluble B vitamins from the milk, including riboflavin (the direct MTHFR enzyme cofactor). 1 litre of milk produces roughly 700–750ml of whey — don't discard it.

🫙 Herb & Garlic EVOO Marinated Mozzarella
🧾 Marinade ingredients
  • Marinade base (per 250g mozzarella)
  • 100mlExtra virgin olive oil (EVOO) — the entire flavour vehicle
  • 3 clovesGarlic, crushed — rest 5 minutes, then add to cold oil (allicin)
  • 1 tspLemon zest (unwaxed lemon)
  • 1 sprigFresh rosemary (or ½ tsp dried)
  • 3 sprigsFresh thyme (or ½ tsp dried)
  • ½ tspDried chilli flakes — optional, APOE4 anti-inflammatory
  • ½ tspWhole black peppercorns — freshly cracked
  • 1Small bay leaf
No vinegar in this marinade — commercial marinated mozzarella often uses white wine vinegar or balsamic. Both are fine for some conditions but vinegar is high oxalate and can push sodium up in processed forms. Lemon zest handles all the needed acidity with zero oxalate and adds urinary citrate (kidney stone benefit). The EVOO does the rest.
👩‍🍳 Marinating method
  1. 1
    Activate the garlic
    Crush 3 garlic cloves and rest 5 minutes. This activates allicin. The garlic will go into cold oil — it is never heated, so allicin is fully preserved. Do not skip the rest period even though the garlic is going into a cold marinade.
  2. 2
    Combine the marinade
    Pour EVOO into a clean glass jar (wide-mouth mason jar works best). Add the activated garlic, lemon zest, rosemary sprig, thyme sprigs, chilli flakes (if using), cracked pepper, and bay leaf. Stir gently. Do not heat the oil — the herb flavours infuse at room temperature over the marinating period.
  3. 3
    Cut and submerge the mozzarella
    Pat the mozzarella dry after removing from the ice water. Cut into bite-sized pieces (bocconcini-style, roughly 3–4cm) or leave as a whole ball if marinating for slicing later. Submerge fully in the marinade — if the EVOO doesn't fully cover, top up. Every surface needs oil contact for even flavour penetration.
  4. 4
    Marinate — minimum 2 hours, best overnight
    Seal the jar and refrigerate. The EVOO will solidify in the fridge — this is normal, it reliquefies at room temperature. For best flavour, marinate overnight. Remove from fridge 15–20 minutes before serving to allow the EVOO to come back to liquid. Use within 5 days.
  5. 5
    The oil is also a product — don't discard it
    The herb and garlic-infused EVOO left after the mozzarella is eaten is a finished flavoured oil. Use it as a bread dip, drizzle over shakshuka, dress a salad, or spoon over roasted asparagus. It contains all the allicin that leached out of the garlic during marinating.
🧆 Baked Mozzarella Sticks (GF Millet Crumb — Not Deep-Fried)

⚠️ Why baked not fried — and why millet crumbs specifically

Deep frying = oxidised PUFA (APOE4) Deep frying at 180°C+ in any oil oxidises polyunsaturated fats rapidly, producing lipid peroxides and aldehydes. For APOE4, oxidised lipids are a direct cardiovascular and neurological risk beyond just the sat fat content. Baking at 220°C with a thin EVOO drizzle on the crust produces Maillard browning without the oxidation risk of submerging in hot oil.
Millet crumbs = already in your pantry You're already making Olive & Feta Cheese Bread. The crumbs from day-old slices are your GF coating — same ones used in the herb-crusted chicken. Zero extra ingredients to source. A wheat-based breadcrumb coating would trigger Celiac. A commercial GF crumb could contain hidden starches. Your own millet crumbs are one ingredient.
The freeze step is non-negotiable Mozzarella melts very rapidly. If you bake without freezing first, the cheese melts out of the crumb coating before the outside browns. Freeze the coated sticks for at least 45 minutes (ideally 2 hours) — the frozen core gives the crust time to set and brown before the cheese reaches melt temperature. Do not skip this.
Shaping during stretching Shape the sticks during the hot stretching phase — before the ice water set. Roll the hot stretched curd into a log ~3cm diameter on a clean surface. Cut into 10cm lengths. Drop into ice water immediately to set the shape. Once cold and firm they hold their shape through coating and freezing perfectly.
🧾 Baked sticks — additional ingredients
  • Coating
  • 80gMillet breadcrumbs (from day-old Olive & Feta Cheese Bread)
  • ½ tspDried Italian herbs (thyme, oregano, basil) — or za'atar
  • ¼ tspGarlic powder (flavour note in crust — therapeutic garlic added to dip)
  • ¼ tspFine sea salt + black pepper
  • 1Egg, lightly beaten (binder)
  • 1 tbspEVOO — to drizzle over before baking
  • Dipping sauce (condition-appropriate)
  • 3 tbspPassata or crushed tomatoes (check sodium — choose low-salt)
  • 1 cloveGarlic, crushed 5-min rest, stirred in cold (allicin dip)
  • 1 tspEVOO stirred through the sauce
  • PinchDried oregano + chilli flakes
👩‍🍳 Baked sticks method
  1. 1
    Shape during stretching
    During the hot stretching phase (Step 5 of the base recipe), instead of forming a ball, roll the stretched curd into a smooth log approximately 3cm in diameter on a clean work surface. Cut into 10cm lengths with a sharp knife. Drop immediately into ice water and rest 5 minutes to set shape firmly.
  2. 2
    Make the crumb coating
    Combine millet crumbs, dried herbs, garlic powder, salt, and pepper in a shallow dish. Beat the egg in a second shallow dish.
  3. 3
    Coat the sticks — double-dip for best coverage
    Pat each stick dry. Dip in egg → press into crumb mixture → dip in egg again → press into crumbs again. The double coat gives a thicker, more uniform crust that holds up better during baking and is less likely to crack open. Place on a baking-paper-lined tray.
  4. 4
    Freeze minimum 45 minutes — this step is critical
    Place the coated sticks (on the tray) in the freezer for at least 45 minutes, ideally 2 hours. The frozen core is what prevents the cheese melting out before the crust browns. If making a large batch, freeze solid and transfer to a zip-lock bag — bake directly from frozen whenever needed, adding 3–4 minutes to the cooking time.
  5. 5
    Bake at 220°C until golden
    Preheat oven to 220°C (200°C fan). Drizzle EVOO lightly over the frozen sticks. Bake 8–10 minutes directly from frozen until the crust is deep golden and you can see the cheese just beginning to soften inside (small amount of melted cheese at the ends is normal and correct). Do not overbake — 2 minutes past perfect and the cheese bursts out entirely.
  6. 6
    Make the dip while baking
    Combine passata, 5-minute activated garlic (crushed and rested — never heated), EVOO, oregano, and chilli flakes in a small bowl. Stir. The dip is cold — the garlic allicin is fully preserved. Serve immediately alongside the hot sticks.
Mechanism summary: Farm Fresh Milk (A2 Jersey cow, grass-fed) → ~40–60% higher C15:0 (odd-chain sat fat — protective, not the APOE4/Fatty Liver concern) vs grain-fed; mozzarella fat is predominantly C15:0 + oleic acid (C18:1), not palmitic acid (C16:0) · Citric acid process → dietary citrate contribution (Kidney Stone, urinary citrate) · Zero added salt option → low sodium, less urinary calcium excretion (Kidney Stone) · EVOO marinade → MUFA delivery, allicin vehicle (APOE4, Fatty Liver) · Cold-activated garlic in marinade and dip → allicin fully preserved (MTHFR glutathione, APOE4) · Lemon zest in marinade → citrate (Kidney Stone), zero oxalate · Millet crumb coating → GF by construction (Celiac), zero wheat starch risk · Baked not fried → no lipid peroxidation (APOE4) · Mozzarella whey → B2 riboflavin (MTHFR enzyme cofactor)

Fresh mozzarella is near-zero carbohydrate — negligible GI. The baked sticks add a small millet crumb GI contribution (same as the herb-crusted chicken). Food-test the sticks as a meal component if uncertain, not as an isolated item.

🔬 Why the freeze step works — and batch freezing for weekly sticks

  • The physics of the freeze step: Mozzarella begins melting at approximately 55–60°C. A preheated 220°C oven reaches that temperature at the surface of the cheese within 2–3 minutes. A frozen core (at -18°C) takes 6–8 minutes to reach melt point — which is exactly the window the crust needs to set and brown via Maillard reaction. Without freezing, the cheese melts before the crust browns and runs out through any crack in the coating. This is why every commercial frozen mozzarella stick is frozen before sale.
  • Batch production for a weekly supply: Make a double or triple batch of sticks during Saturday prep-day. Coat all the sticks, freeze solid on trays, then transfer to a labelled zip-lock bag once frozen solid. They keep in the freezer for up to 6 weeks. Bake directly from frozen — add 3–4 minutes to the time (12–13 minutes total from frozen solid). This replaces the commercial store package entirely and costs a fraction of the price per stick.
  • Why garlic powder in the crust rather than activated garlic: Activated allicin garlic in the crumb coating would be baked at 220°C, which destroys allicin immediately. Garlic powder baked into the crust provides background flavour only — this is deliberate. The allicin delivery comes from the cold dipping sauce (crushed garlic, 5-minute rest, added cold). Two functions, two delivery methods — exactly the same principle as the kefir chicken two-garlic method.
  • Vinegar absence in the marinade is intentional: Commercial marinated mozzarella almost always uses white wine or apple cider vinegar for acidity. Vinegar is relatively high oxalate, and balsamic is very high oxalate. Lemon zest provides all the brightness needed with zero oxalate load and the additional urinary citrate benefit for kidney stone prevention. The EVOO alone is sufficient to preserve the mozzarella safely for 5 days refrigerated — the acid is flavour, not preservation.
  • UHT milk failure — why it matters to understand: If your mozzarella doesn't set at all — the milk stays liquid after the rennet rest — the milk is almost certainly UHT. UHT processing heats milk to 140°C for 2–4 seconds, which permanently denatures the casein proteins that form curds. No amount of citric acid or rennet will fix this. Use Farm Fresh Milk from the local farmstand — it is standard pasteurised and will form curds. The Walmart A2 Brand Milk is ultra-pasteurized and cannot be used. If sourcing milk elsewhere, check the label: if it says "UHT," "ultra-pasteurized," or "ultra-high temperature," do not use it.
