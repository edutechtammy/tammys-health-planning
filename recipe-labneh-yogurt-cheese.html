Sugar Shift Labneh (Strained Yogurt Cheese)

Sugar Shift probiotic yogurt strained cold through cheesecloth for 12–24 hours until it reaches a soft, spreadable cream-cheese consistency. This is labneh — a staple of Middle Eastern cooking, not a workaround. The live cultures (including the Sugar Shift proprietary strains) survive entirely in the curd solids and remain fully active in the finished cheese. It has dramatically lower saturated fat than commercial cream cheese, higher protein, zero additives, and no wheat starch — just yogurt, time, and cold. The whey that drains off is a valuable byproduct: rich in B2 (riboflavin, MTHFR cofactor), lactoferrin, and some live culture carryover. Don't pour it down the drain. Flavour variations below: two savoury, two sweet — all condition-specific.

👩‍🍳 Base method — the strain
  1. 1
    Set up the straining rig
    Line a sieve or colander with two layers of cheesecloth, a clean muslin cloth, or a clean thin kitchen towel. Set the sieve over a bowl deep enough that the sieve sits above the whey without touching it — if the sieve sits in the whey it will stop draining. Pour the full 500g tub of Sugar Shift yogurt into the cloth. Fold the cloth loosely over the top.
  2. 2
    Refrigerate 12–16 hours — do not strain at room temperature
    Place the whole setup in the refrigerator. Cold straining keeps the cultures active and eliminates any spoilage risk from leaving dairy out. Strain overnight Friday → ready Saturday morning for the prep-day batch. Do not stir, press, or disturb — let gravity do the work.
  3. 3
    Transfer and season
    Unwrap the cloth and scoop the labneh into an airtight container. The texture should be soft and spreadable — holds its shape on a spoon but smears easily. Stir in a small pinch of sea salt if desired (optional — keeps sodium low for kidney stone management). The labneh is now the base for any flavour variation below, or use plain.
  4. 4
    Reserve the whey — do not discard
    Pour the collected whey into a clean jar and refrigerate. See the whey uses panel below. It keeps for 5–7 days.

💧 The whey — what's in it and what to do with it

  • B2 (riboflavin) — MTHFR: Whey carries a significant portion of the milk's water-soluble B vitamins, including riboflavin. Riboflavin is the direct cofactor for the MTHFR enzyme — the enzyme that is impaired in MTHFR C677T. Don't pour it out.
  • Use in the Ginger Bug feed in place of water — adds B vitamins and a small culture boost to the ferment without affecting the flavour noticeably.
  • Use as the liquid in Kefir Overnight Grain Porridge — replaces water or extra kefir, adds protein and B vitamins to the soak.
  • Add to bone broth soups — stir in at the end (off heat) to preserve the B vitamins. Adds a slight tang and additional protein.
  • Use as the liquid base for shakshuka sauce — replaces some of the tomato liquid and adds a background richness.

🧈 Flavour range — mild cream cheese to full sour cream

The flavour of your labneh will vary significantly depending on how tart the yogurt was before straining. This is a feature, not a problem — it gives you a different end product depending on what you start with, and both ends of the spectrum are genuinely useful.

🥛 Fresh yogurt → mild cream cheese
Made from a fresh, lightly fermented yogurt (young, not very sour), the labneh tastes soft, milky, and rich — very close to a good cream cheese. This is the version you want spread on cassava crackers with lemon zest, swirled into the bone broth cheese sauce, or used in the sweet variations (vanilla, cinnamon blueberry).
🫙 Tart/aged yogurt → sour cream
Made from yogurt that has been in the fridge for a while and developed a pronounced tang, the labneh tastes and behaves almost exactly like thick sour cream — the same cooling, sharp dairy flavour. This is the version your first batch produced. It is genuinely excellent used anywhere you'd reach for sour cream.
What goes well with the sour cream version
🍳 Eggs: A spoonful of tart savoury labneh on the side of scrambled eggs — or stirred in off-heat at the end — replaces the sour cream dollop completely. Adds live cultures and protein the sour cream version doesn't have.
🍲 Soups and chilis: Swirl a tablespoon on top of bone broth soups, lentil soup, or any chili just before serving (not while cooking — heat kills the cultures). The tang cuts through the richness exactly as sour cream does. The tart labneh is more flavourful than the mild version here.
🧅 French onion and ranch-style dip: Tart savoury labneh is an ideal base for any dip that would normally use sour cream or crème fraîche. The garlic-herb and lemon za'atar variations below already have that profile. Thin slightly with a little EVOO and lemon juice if you want a pourable dressing consistency.
🥒 Raw vegetables: Cucumber slices, carrot sticks, celery — the tart savoury labneh used as a dip is the functional equivalent of a sour cream ranch dip, with live cultures, much lower saturated fat, and none of the additives. The cassava crackers (Recipe 21) and raw vegetables together make a complete snack board with this as the dip.
🍞 Savoury bread: Spread thickly on the olive cheese bread (Recipe 11) or on cassava crackers. The tang works particularly well with the herbs in the olive bread — the combination reads as bread + cultured butter + herbs. A few grinds of black pepper on top and a drizzle of EVOO finishes it.
🥗 Salad dressing base: Thin with lemon juice and EVOO (1 tbsp labneh + 1 tbsp lemon + 2 tbsp EVOO + garlic + herbs, whisk or shake) — makes a creamy dressing with the tang of a yogurt-based dressing and the probiotic benefit of a cultured product. Good on any raw salad including the cucumber and carrot combinations above.

Tip: If you want to deliberately make the sour cream version, leave your yogurt in the fridge for an extra 3–5 days before straining — the Lactobacillus cultures continue producing lactic acid slowly in the cold, building the tartness. To make the mild cream cheese version, use the freshest yogurt you have.

🧄 Savoury variations

🧄 Garlic, Herb & Lemon Labneh

The most versatile savoury option. The activated garlic (5-minute crush rule) + lemon together provide allicin delivery and urinary citrate in the same spread. Parsley adds MTHFR folate. Goes on everything.

Stir into 250g base labneh: 2 cloves garlic, crushed and rested 5 minutes (allicin) · 2 tbsp fresh flat-leaf parsley, finely chopped · 1 tbsp fresh chives, finely chopped · 1 tsp lemon zest + squeeze of lemon juice · 1 tbsp EVOO stirred through · Pinch sea salt · Freshly ground black pepper
MTHFR folate (parsley) Citrate (lemon) EVOO MUFA Allicin (garlic)
Serve on: Olive & Feta Cheese Bread · alongside herb-crusted chicken · with cucumber and bell pepper sticks · as a base under roasted asparagus · on the Ginger-Garlic Millet Bowl instead of the egg
🌿 Za'atar & EVOO Labneh

Za'atar is a Middle Eastern spice blend typically containing dried thyme, sumac, sesame, and salt — all low oxalate, all APOE4-compatible. Sumac is particularly interesting: high in flavonoids, anti-inflammatory, and gives a lemony tartness without adding liquid. Drizzle EVOO over the top rather than stirring through — the pool of oil on the surface is traditional and prevents the surface from drying out in the fridge.

To finish 250g base labneh: 1–1.5 tbsp za'atar spice blend (check ingredients — should be thyme, sumac, sesame, salt; no wheat) · 1.5 tbsp EVOO drizzled over the surface · Optional: pinch of dried chilli flakes · Pinch sea salt if the za'atar is unsalted
EVOO + sumac flavonoids Thyme anti-inflammatory Near-zero GI
Serve on: Spread in a shallow bowl with the EVOO pool on top — tear the Olive & Feta bread and dip · with sliced cucumber and radish · alongside any chicken recipe · as a mezze component with guacamole
⚠️ Check za'atar blend label for wheat — some commercial blends use wheat starch as a filler. For Celiac, only buy a blend whose ingredients list is thyme, sumac, sesame seeds, and salt — nothing else. Or make your own (see below) and you know exactly what's in it.
🌿 Make your own za'atar — 5 minutes, keeps 6 months
Za'atar is simply a ratio blend of herbs and sumac — no cooking, no special equipment, just measuring and stirring. The classic ratio is:
  • 2 tbsp dried thyme (or a mix of thyme + dried oregano)
  • 1 tbsp ground sumac — the sour red powder you already have; this is what gives za'atar its lemony tartness
  • 1 tbsp sesame seeds — toasted in a dry pan 2 minutes until golden, or untoasted; toasted adds a nuttier depth
  • ¼ tsp fine salt
Combine in a small jar, shake, done. Taste and adjust — more sumac for more tartness, more thyme for more herbal depth. No sumac? A small pinch of lemon zest stirred through the finished labneh gives a similar brightness, though the flavour is different. Store in a sealed jar at room temperature; the spice shelf is fine.

🍫 Sweet variations

🍋 Lemon & Ceylon Cinnamon Labneh

No added sugar — the sweetness comes from the lemon zest brightness and the vanilla note of Ceylon cinnamon. Ceylon cinnamon (cinnamaldehyde) improves insulin sensitivity and slows gastric emptying, making this the most DP-specific sweet option. The lemon zest provides citrate for kidney stone prevention with zero GI impact. A very small amount of raw honey can be stirred in if sweetness is needed — this is the only context in the meal plan where a small honey quantity is acceptable (the protein and fat of the labneh fully blunts the glucose response).

Stir into 250g base labneh: 1 tsp lemon zest (unwaxed lemon) · ½ tsp Ceylon cinnamon (not Cassia — coumarin hepatotoxicity) · ½ tsp vanilla extract (no added sugar — check label) · Optional: ½ tsp raw honey only if sweetness is needed · Pinch sea salt (amplifies perceived sweetness)
Cinnamaldehyde insulin Citrate (lemon zest) Ceylon — zero coumarin
Serve on: Olive & Feta Cheese Bread as a breakfast spread · alongside the Kefir Overnight Grain Porridge as a protein side · with fresh blueberries or sliced strawberries · as a dip for the trail mix (unexpected but it works)
🫐 Blueberry & Walnut Labneh

Anthocyanins from blueberries are the key APOE4 compound here — they cross the blood-brain barrier and reduce neuroinflammation directly. Walnuts add ALA omega-3 and a textural crunch element. No added sugar — the fresh blueberries provide all the sweetness needed. Mash half the blueberries in rather than leaving them all whole to get a ripple of colour and flavour through the labneh. The EVOO in the base keeps the fat profile APOE4-appropriate even with the walnuts added.

Fold into 250g base labneh: 60g fresh blueberries — half mashed in, half folded in whole · 20g walnuts, roughly chopped · ½ tsp Ceylon cinnamon · ½ tsp vanilla extract · Optional pinch sea salt
Anthocyanins (blueberry) ALA omega-3 (walnut) Protein + fat blunts berry GI Low oxalate fruit
Serve on: Olive & Feta Cheese Bread · layered with the Kefir Overnight Grain Porridge as a topping · as a standalone breakfast bowl with extra blueberries on top · with a small handful of trail mix crumbled over

📊 Labneh vs commercial cream cheese — why the swap is worth making

✅ Saturated fat — APOE4 & Fatty Liver Commercial full-fat cream cheese: ~20g sat fat per 100g. Sugar Shift labneh (full-fat yogurt base): approximately 4–6g sat fat per 100g after straining. The straining removes water, concentrating protein and fat, but the starting sat fat in the yogurt is far lower than cream cheese to begin with.
✅ Live cultures — DP Blood Sugar Commercial cream cheese has no live cultures — it is heat-treated. Sugar Shift labneh retains the full live culture profile including the proprietary Sugar Shift strains associated with improved glucose metabolism and GLP-1 stimulation. Cold-straining only — cultures are not killed.
✅ Protein — all conditions Straining concentrates the whey proteins into the curd. Labneh has approximately 8–10g protein per 100g. Commercial cream cheese has approximately 5–6g. The protein increase matters for satiety, for DP blood sugar stability, and for liver function (adequate dietary protein supports hepatic fat export).
✅ No additives — Celiac Commercial cream cheese frequently contains stabilisers (locust bean gum, carob gum) and some contain wheat starch as a filler. Sugar Shift labneh is one ingredient: yogurt. Zero contamination risk, zero hidden gluten, zero additives.

🔬 Why cold straining preserves the Sugar Shift cultures

  • Culture survival temperature range: Most Lactobacillus and Bifidobacterium strains remain viable and active between 4°C and 45°C. Refrigerator temperature (~4°C) slows their metabolism significantly but does not kill them — they enter a dormant state and reactivate when consumed. Straining cold keeps every culture alive that was alive in the original tub.
  • Why the cultures stay in the solids, not the whey: Live bacteria in yogurt are predominantly embedded in the protein matrix of the curd — the casein network that forms during fermentation traps them physically. The whey that drains off carries some bacteria, some B vitamins, lactose, and minerals, but the bulk of the live culture population remains in the strained solids. This is why labneh has probiotic activity that Greek yogurt (also strained) also retains.
  • Garlic off-heat rule applies to savoury labneh: The garlic in the savoury herb variation is added to the cold finished labneh — it is never heated. This is the correct method for allicin delivery: crush → 5-minute rest → add cold or off-heat. Stirring activated garlic into cold labneh preserves 100% of the allicin formed during the rest period.
  • Za'atar wheat check is not optional for Celiac: Za'atar is traditionally wheat-free, but commercial blends vary. Several mass-market blends include wheat starch as a flow agent or filler. Read the ingredients list. The Za'atar & EVOO flavour card above includes a full make-your-own recipe (2 tbsp dried thyme, 1 tbsp sumac, 1 tbsp sesame seeds, ¼ tsp salt) — making your own guarantees no cross-contamination risk.
  • Why butter destroys allicin and labneh doesn't: Compound garlic butter is almost always used warm — spread on toast straight from the toaster, melted into hot bread, or used as a hot finishing sauce. Any heat above 60°C begins destroying allicin rapidly; the typical toast surface temperature is well above this. The allicin in garlic herb butter is largely gone before it reaches the table. The garlic in herb labneh is added to cold strained yogurt and never heated — the 5-minute activated allicin is fully intact at the point of eating. This is not a minor difference: allicin is the primary therapeutic compound in garlic for AMPK activation, glutathione support (MTHFR), and cardiovascular endothelial function (APOE4). Getting it cold-delivered in a daily spread versus heat-destroyed in butter is a meaningful daily compound difference.
  • Honey in the sweet variations: Raw honey in a very small quantity (½ tsp across the full batch = ~0.5g per 80g serve) is acceptable in the context of labneh because the protein and fat completely blunt any glucose response. This is not a licence to increase the quantity — ½ tsp per batch is a flavour note, not a sweetener dose. If you are actively glucose-monitoring a session, log it and observe. Most people with DP will see no measurable response at this quantity when eaten with the labneh.
