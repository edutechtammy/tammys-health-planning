Caprese Hasselback Chicken

Chicken breasts sliced accordion-style and stuffed with fresh homemade mozzarella, vine tomato, and basil — seared on the stovetop then finished in the oven so the mozzarella melts into the pockets while the surface crisps. Finished with a splash of plain balsamic vinegar and fresh basil. The Hasselback technique exposes maximum surface area for flavour development while keeping the cheese insulated from direct heat, so it melts cleanly without running out. Every ingredient is already in the protocol — this is a presentation technique as much as a recipe. Chicken breast delivers ~250–350mg carnosine per serving, directly supporting the AGE supplement stack.

The oven-bake finish is gentler than high-heat grilling and preserves more carnosine. 🔬 Why each ingredient earns its place

Homemade mozzarella — not store-bought Your homemade A2 Farm Fresh mozzarella provides C15:0, sphingomyelin/MFGM phospholipids, casein phosphopeptides (gut oxalate binding), and K2 (fermentation-generated). Commercial store mozzarella is typically A1 milk, lower C15:0, and often lower K2. The distinction matters most for the APOE4 and fatty liver conditions. Fresh mozzarella also has zero lactose and near-zero glycemic impact.
Fresh tomatoes — low oxalate, not a concern Fresh vine tomatoes contain ~5mg oxalate per medium tomato — among the lowest of any vegetable. Sun-dried tomatoes concentrate oxalate; fresh tomatoes do not. The tomato + mozzarella calcium pairing has zero kidney stone implication here. Lycopene from cooked or heated tomatoes is more bioavailable than raw, but the slight oven warmth from the bake window provides enough heating to enhance absorption without "cooking" the tomato to mush.
Plain balsamic vinegar — not glaze Balsamic glaze is reduced balsamic — the water evaporates and the sugar concentrates dramatically (a typical glaze is ~50% sugar by weight). A full drizzle could add 10–15g sugar per serving, producing a meaningful spike. Plain unreduced balsamic vinegar at ½–1 tsp per serving delivers the characteristic dark vinegar flavour and acetic acid (same mechanism as ACV) at negligible glucose cost. The drizzle is applied post-oven so the acetic acid is not cooked off.
Activated garlic — 5-min crush before heat Allicin forms when alliinase enzyme contacts air after crushing. Adding garlic directly to a hot pan destroys alliinase before allicin can form. The sear step adds the activated garlic to the pan around the chicken (not directly onto high heat) — the pan comes off full sear temperature at that point and the garlic goes into a hot-but-not-blazing environment. The oven finish continues at a lower temperature, completing allicin delivery.
Hasselback technique — why it matters culinarily The accordion cuts serve two purposes: they increase surface area for seasoning and crust development, and they create insulated pockets that hold the mozzarella without a foil tent or cover. Mozzarella melts at ~55°C — the pocket geometry keeps the cheese in contact with the chicken while the outer surface reaches full browning temperature. Without the pockets, the cheese would melt onto the pan before the interior reaches temperature.
EVOO + tomato — lycopene bioavailability Lycopene is a fat-soluble carotenoid. Consuming tomato without dietary fat leaves ~5–10% of lycopene absorbed. In the presence of EVOO, absorption rises to ~40–60%. The EVOO in this dish is present both in the pan and drizzled over the tops before baking — the tomato slices are cooking in a fat-rich environment throughout, maximising the hepatoprotective lycopene delivery.

This dish is protein + fat with trace carbohydrate (tomato + a few drops of balsamic). GI impact is negligible standalone. If serving over millet or with GF bread, test the full plate combination.

🧾 Ingredients — 2 servings

  • 2Chicken breasts (~180–200g each)⬜ zero

  • ¼ tspSea salt + black pepper⬜ zero
  • 2 tbspEVOO (1 for sear, 1 for tops)⬜ zero
  • Stuffing
  • 100gFresh homemade mozzarella, sliced ~5mm⬜ zero
  • 1 mediumVine tomato, sliced ~5mm🟢 low
  • 8–10Fresh basil leaves🟢 low
  • 2 clovesGarlic, crushed (5-min rest — allicin)🟢 low
  • Finish
  • 1–2 tspPlain balsamic vinegar — NOT glaze⬜ trace
  • handfulFresh basil (to serve)🟢 low
  • pinchChilli flakes (optional)⬜ trace
⚠️ Balsamic glaze ≠ balsamic vinegar. Glaze is reduced and ~50% sugar by weight. Use only plain unreduced balsamic vinegar, applied after the oven, not before or during cooking. ½–1 tsp per breast is sufficient — you want flavour accent, not a sauce.
👩‍🍳 Instructions
  1. 1
    Activate garlic first — start the 5-min clock
    Crush both garlic cloves. Set aside for 5 minutes before they go near heat. Start the clock before doing anything else. Preheat oven to 200°C (180°C fan) now.
  2. 2
    Hasselback the chicken
    Place each breast smooth-side up. Make deep cuts across the breast every 2cm — cutting ~⅔ of the way through, not all the way. 5–7 cuts per breast. Season all over (including into the cuts) with Italian seasoning, salt, and pepper.
  3. 3
    Stuff the pockets
    Into each cut: mozzarella slice → tomato slice → basil leaf, repeating to fill. Press down gently. The mozzarella should be slightly proud of the cut — it will settle as it melts. Don't overstuff or the pockets won't close during searing.
  4. 4
    Sear the base
    Heat 1 tbsp EVOO in an oven-safe stainless or cast iron pan over medium-high. Place chicken stuffed-side up — don't flip yet. Sear the bottom for 2–3 minutes until golden. The stuffed top faces up throughout — you're building the base crust only.
  5. 5
    Add garlic and bake
    Scatter the activated garlic around the chicken (not directly on top). Drizzle 1 tbsp EVOO over the stuffed tops. Transfer the pan directly to the oven. Bake 20–22 minutes until internal temp reads 74°C at the thickest point between cuts, and the mozzarella is visibly melted and lightly golden at the edges.
  6. 6
    Rest, finish, serve
    Rest 3 minutes out of the oven. Drizzle ½–1 tsp plain balsamic vinegar over each breast — the residual heat blooms the aroma without cooking off the acetic acid. Scatter fresh basil and chilli flakes. Serve immediately — mozzarella sets as it cools.
Mechanism summary: EVOO sear (MUFA → APOE4 LDL oxidation protection) · activated garlic (allicin → AMPK, glutathione S-transferase, hepatic lipogenesis inhibition) · homemade A2 mozzarella (C15:0, K2, casein CPP, sphingomyelin/MFGM) · chicken breast → ~250–350mg carnosine/serving = primary dietary AGE inhibitor, glyoxalase-independent · tomato lycopene + EVOO = ~40–60% lycopene absorption vs ~5–10% fat-free · plain balsamic vinegar acetic acid (ACV-adjacent, applied post-oven) · oven-bake finish = lower AGE formation than dry high-heat methods

🥬 With sautéed lacinato kale The same low-oxalate kale from the Tuscan Chicken recipe works here — sauté in EVOO + garlic while the chicken bakes. Vitamin K from the kale complements the K2 from the mozzarella.

🔬 Protocol notes

  • Why the cuts go ⅔ through, not all the way: A full cut creates individual medallion-style slices that fall apart when you stuff and move them. The uncut bottom third acts as a hinge that holds the breast together as one piece through searing, baking, plating, and slicing at the table. If you accidentally cut through, the breast still tastes identical — it just won't present as neatly.
  • Mozzarella — fresh vs low-moisture: Low-moisture (block) mozzarella holds its shape longer in the oven but produces a rubbery texture in the pockets. Fresh homemade mozzarella melts more quickly (and more beautifully) — this is the desirable outcome. The mozzarella should be fully melted and creamy in the pockets by the time the chicken is done. If yours is running out of the pockets, the slices are too thick — aim for 4–5mm, not thicker.
  • Oven-safe pan requirement: This recipe requires a pan that can go from stovetop to oven — stainless steel, cast iron, or carbon steel. Non-stick pans with plastic handles cannot go in a 200°C oven. If you only have non-stick, complete the sear and then transfer the chicken to a parchment-lined baking tray for the oven step (the pan juices won't transfer, but the result is comparable).
  • Balsamic vinegar sourcing: Any plain balsamic vinegar (not labelled "glaze", "cream", or "reduction") is correct. Modena DOP is the traditional Italian variety with a clean sweet-acid balance. Avoid anything thick enough to coat a spoon at room temperature — that is a reduction regardless of label. Applied at ½–1 tsp per serving, the glucose contribution is under 1g.
  • Tomato variety: Vine-ripened tomatoes have the best flavour and highest lycopene for this recipe. Roma tomatoes also work — their denser flesh holds shape better in the pockets and doesn't release as much water during baking, which reduces steaming inside the cuts. Cherry tomatoes can be halved and used but the pocket geometry is harder to fill evenly.
