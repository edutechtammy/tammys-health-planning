🏝 What is Ladenia? Ladenia (λαδένια) is a traditional thick flatbread from the Cycladic island of Kimolos, Greece. The name comes from ladi (λάδι) — Greek for olive oil. Americans call it Greek pizza because it looks like one, but it predates pizza as a concept and is fundamentally different: no tomato sauce, no cheese in the traditional version, and the defining feature is the generous EVOO that soaks through the dough from both the oiled pan below and the drizzle on top during baking. Traditional toppings: fresh tomatoes, onion, kalamata olives, capers, oregano. This version adds crumbled feta on top — as many island home cooks do — which also handles the calcium-oxalate pairing for the tomato load.
⚠️ Tomato oxalate strategy: Tomatoes are a moderate-oxalate ingredient. The crumbled feta in this recipe is the calcium source that binds dietary oxalate in the gut before absorption. Do not skip the feta if kidney stone prevention is a priority. If you prefer to keep it strictly traditional (no feta on top), serve with a portion of the Marinated Feta as a side — the calcium from that serving handles the tomato oxalate at the same meal.

Ladenia — GF Greek Island Flatbread (Kimolos-Style)

A gluten-free millet-sorghum batter pressed flat in a generously oiled sheet pan, topped with fresh tomatoes, onion rings, kalamata olives, capers, crumbled feta, and dried oregano — then soaked in EVOO and baked until the bottom is golden and the tomatoes have caramelised at the edges. No yeast, no kneading, no stretching. The batter is poured directly into the pan, rested briefly so the psyllium husk binds, then topped and baked. The oil-soaked bottom crust is the whole point.

⏱ Prep: 20 min (incl. 15 min rest) 🔥 Bake: 28–32 min at 400°F ⏳ Total: ~52 min 🍽 Yield: 4 main or 6–8 mezze 🥘 Pan: 9×13 inch rimmed sheet pan

👩‍🍳 Method
  1. 1
    Oil the pan — generously
    Preheat oven to 400°F (200°C). Pour 3 tablespoons of EVOO into a 9×13 inch rimmed sheet pan. Tilt to coat the entire base. The oil is not greasing — it is an ingredient. It soaks through the dough from below during baking and is what gives traditional Ladenia its character. Do not reduce it.
  2. 2
    Mix the batter
    Whisk all dry ingredients together in a large bowl (millet flour, sorghum flour, tapioca starch, psyllium husk, baking powder, salt, oregano, black pepper). In a separate bowl, whisk the eggs, then add the ½ cup EVOO and kefir and whisk to combine. Pour wet into dry and stir until a thick, pourable batter forms.
  3. 3
    Pour and spread flat
    Pour the batter straight into the oiled pan. Spread evenly using a wet spatula — wet hands also work well. The target thickness is 1.5–2 cm (about ¾ inch). The batter will be slightly sticky; dampening your tools prevents it pulling back.
  4. 4
    Rest 15 minutes
    Leave the pan at room temperature for 15 minutes. The psyllium husk continues hydrating and binds the batter into a firmer, more cohesive layer. Skipping this rest causes toppings to partially sink. Use this time to prep the toppings.
  5. 5
    Add toppings
    Layer from bottom to top: onion rings first (they need the most direct heat), then tomato slices slightly overlapping, then kalamata olives and capers scattered across. Crumble feta evenly over everything. Scatter the remaining teaspoon of dried oregano. Season with a pinch of salt and a twist of black pepper.
  6. 6
    Drizzle EVOO over all toppings
    Drizzle 3–4 tablespoons of EVOO over the toppings in an even pass — over the tomatoes, the feta, the olives. This is the final oil layer that caramelises the surface during baking. Don't skip it and don't be shy with it.
  7. 7
    Bake and rest
    Bake at 400°F for 28–32 minutes. Check at 28 minutes: the base is ready when you can lift a corner with a spatula and the underside is golden-brown, and the tomatoes show slight caramelisation at their edges. Rest 5 minutes in the pan before cutting into squares. Use a spatula to serve — the oil-soaked bottom crust is part of the dish.
What's working here: EVOO (below and above) → oleocanthal + oleic acid (APOE4 neuroprotection, hepatic fat reduction, COX inhibition — EVOO at oven temp loses less oleocanthal than high-heat sautéing) · Kalamata olives → hydroxytyrosol (one of the most potent olive polyphenols, anti-inflammatory, hepatoprotective) · Capers → quercetin (highest dietary source per gram — COX inhibitor, AMPK activator, hepatoprotective, anti-inflammatory) · Onion → quercetin (same compound, reinforces capers) · Feta → calcium binds tomato oxalate in the gut as insoluble calcium oxalate before renal absorption · Kefir in batter → mild fermentation, lowers glycaemic index of the whole bread vs unfermented GF flour · Millet-sorghum base → lower GI than rice-based GF flour, Celiac-safe, provides sustained glucose profile · Oregano → carnosic acid and rosmarinic acid (antimicrobial, anti-inflammatory phenolics). Every ingredient earns its place.

🔄 Variations & serving ideas

🧀 Pair with Marinated Feta (mezze plate) Serve squares of Ladenia alongside a spoonful of the Marinated Feta in Chili & EVOO. The chili oil from the marinated feta jar doubles as a dipping oil for the Ladenia crust. The calcium from the marinated feta also handles any tomato oxalate if you decide to reduce the feta crumble on top.
🫑 Add roasted bell pepper Layer thin strips of roasted red bell pepper under the tomatoes. Bell pepper is low oxalate, adds colour, and contributes capsanthin (anti-inflammatory carotenoid). No change to the calcium pairing needed.
🌿 Herb-forward version Add a small handful of chopped fresh parsley and a few sprigs of fresh thyme scattered over the top along with the oregano, just before baking. Fresh parsley contributes folate (MTHFR-relevant) at a higher level than dried. Both go on top — not in the batter.
🐟 Anchovy upgrade (APOE4 / MTHFR) Lay 6–8 oil-packed anchovy fillets over the top before the EVOO drizzle. They melt into the surface during baking, disappear visually, and add preformed DHA, B12, and selenium — a genuine APOE4 and MTHFR upgrade. The salt from the anchovies means you should omit the final seasoning pinch.

🔬 Why each component is here

  • Two rounds of EVOO — pan and drizzle: Traditional Ladenia is known for the quantity of olive oil used. In the pan, the oil rises through the dough as it bakes, creating the characteristic oily, slightly crisp bottom crust. The drizzle over toppings caramelises the surface. EVOO at 400°F oven temperature retains more oleocanthal than high-heat stovetop cooking — the oven temperature is reached gradually and the oil isn't held at that temperature the whole time the way a sauté pan is. This is still one of the best high-volume EVOO applications you can do.
  • Capers — not a garnish: Capers have one of the highest concentrations of quercetin of any food — up to 234 mg per 100g dry weight. Quercetin inhibits COX-1 and COX-2 (same mechanism as ibuprofen), activates AMPK (same pathway as metformin), and is hepatoprotective in multiple mechanisms. A 2-tablespoon serving is a meaningful dose. They are not optional decoration.
  • Kalamata olives: The curing process concentrates hydroxytyrosol — an olive polyphenol with stronger antioxidant activity than vitamin C and vitamin E in some assays. Hepatoprotective, anti-atherogenic (relevant for APOE4), and anti-inflammatory. Kalamata olives have a higher polyphenol content than most other cured olive varieties.
  • Onion rings on the bottom of the topping stack: Onions release moisture during baking. Placing them directly on the batter (not on top of the tomatoes) means they steam from below while the tomatoes caramelise on top. Onions are also a good quercetin source — layering capers and onions gives you double quercetin delivery.
  • Kefir in the batter: The lactic acid in homemade kefir partially ferments the flours during mixing and resting, lowering the glycaemic index of the finished bread compared to an identical recipe made with water. This is the same mechanism as sourdough fermentation — kefir is a shortcut to the same outcome without a days-long ferment. It also adds a mild tang that complements the oregano and olives.
  • Millet + sorghum base (no rice flour): Rice flour is the most common GF flour base, but it has a high glycaemic index (~72–98 depending on variety and processing). Millet and sorghum have lower GI values and provide a denser protein matrix that slows glucose absorption. The combination also holds together better in a flat sheet pan application than rice flour would — rice-heavy batters tend to crack when spread thin.
  • Feta on top (not traditional, but right for this version): Traditional Kimolos Ladenia is vegan — no cheese. But many Greek home cooks add feta, and from a health design perspective it solves the tomato oxalate issue at the dish level rather than requiring a separate calcium source alongside. The feta also contributes texture and flavour that works well against the sweet caramelised tomatoes.
