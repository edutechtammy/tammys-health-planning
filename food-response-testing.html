🍽️ Personal Food Response Testing

End-of-fast glucose response tests — log what you ate, when you read, and how your body responded. Build your own personal glycemic map over time.

Δ G = ( C ⋅ C F ) × M order × M time × M recipe × M bio × M activity

∆G (Target Value): The predicted peak blood sugar rise (change from baseline) in mg/dL

C (Carbs): Total net carbohydrates in the meal (grams).

CF (Carb Factor): Your personalized baseline mg/dL rise per gram of carbohydrate under perfect conditions. To calculate this, you need a single ingredient food with precise grams and the carbohydrate count without fiber.

Morder (Food Order Multiplier): A fractional discount based on sequencing.

Mtime (Time of Day / Dawn Phenomenon Multiplier): Adjusts for your diurnal insulin sensitivity.

Mrecipe (Recipe Structure Multiplier): Adjusts for the state of foods in the meal (liquid, highly processed, raw, etc.).

Mbio (Biological Multiplier): Adjusts for lifestyle factors (1 is a perfect day while a higher number is a high stress low sleep day).

Mactivity (Activity Multiplier): Adjusts for physical activity levels where a 1 is a sedentary day and lower numbers are active days such as 0.8 for a casual 15-minute walk immediately after eating.


The equation predicts the magnitude of the highest peak, not the exact minute the peak occurs.

The prediction will be more accurate with fasting blood sugar because the amount of circulating insulin is more stable.

⚠️ Dawn Phenomenon note Your fasting baseline will run higher than population norms due to DP — that's expected and doesn't invalidate the data. What matters is the spike above your own baseline (not the absolute number) and how quickly it returns. Compare test days to each other, not to a textbook fasting target.

💡 What the spike shape tells you Fast spike + fast return → decent insulin response  ·  Slow climb + slow return → insulin resistance pattern  ·  High peak → food is problematic regardless of health reputation  ·  Flat line → safe food for you

📐 Your Standardization Protocol — keeping variables consistent across tests
💧 Hydration
Target ~1 bottle (12 oz) before testing, ~1 bottle during the 2-hr window. Water only during the window. Consistent across all test days.
🌤️ Movement / Outdoor Time
Test food is eaten immediately before outdoor time. Start the walk ~15 minutes after your last bite — sourdough absorption begins at ~15–20 min on a fasted stomach, so walking at this point intercepts glucose while it's actively entering circulation and before the T+30 peak. Earlier than 15 min = burning liver glycogen instead; later reduces the blunting effect on your T+30 reading. A ~20-min walk consistently finishes before the +30 reading. This is intentional — the walk is a controlled constant across all tests, not a confounding variable. Readings reflect real-life behavior (eat → walk → continue day), which is the right map to build. On rainy days: match treadmill time + intensity to preserve the constant.
⏱️ Fasting window & morning baseline timing

The baseline reading is taken ~1 hour after waking, once glucose has stabilised post-Dawn-Phenomenon — then eaten immediately after logging it.

Why 1 hour, not on-waking: In the first 30–60 minutes after waking, cortisol and growth hormone signal the liver to release stored glucose (hepatic glucose output) — glucose is actively rising during this window regardless of food intake. This is the Dawn Phenomenon rise curve. At ~60 minutes post-waking the hormonal pulse settles, the liver stops the release, and glucose stabilises at the true fasting baseline. This stabilised number is the meaningful one: it reflects hepatic fasting glucose set-point, not the DP peak.

CGM confirmation method: On a CGM, wait until the trend arrow is flat (→) with no active rise or fall for at least 10–15 minutes — that is the stable baseline. On a finger-stick meter, the 1-hour post-waking timing replicates this reliably.

Consistency note: If a clinic fasting draw is done early morning (within 30 min of waking), it may read higher than your home 1-hour baseline — this is the same DP mechanism, not a real difference in metabolic status. Note the time and duration-awake on any lab draw for accurate comparison to home data. Compare spike above baseline across food tests, not the absolute number.
