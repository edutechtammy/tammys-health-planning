Guacamole — Classic with Garlic & Lemon

Two ripe avocados, lemon juice, 5-minute activated garlic, fresh coriander, and sea salt. No cooking. Five minutes. This is not a filler recipe — avocado's fat profile (~75% monounsaturated oleic acid) is the same therapeutic fat class as EVOO, and it is one of the few whole foods with a meaningful potassium-to-sodium ratio that directly supports insulin sensitivity. For APOE4 carriers, MUFAs are the preferred dietary fat because they reduce LDL oxidation — and APOE4 makes LDL significantly more oxidation-prone than in non-carriers. The lemon juice is not just flavour — it prevents browning and delivers urinary citrate for kidney stone management at the same time.

⏱ Prep: 5 min 🔥 Cook: none ⏳ Total: ~5–10 min 🍽 Yield: 2 servings

🥑 How to know when your avocado is ready

⚠️ Too firm — not ready Feels hard when squeezed. Flesh will be pale and starchy inside. Leave at room temperature (not fridge) for 1–3 more days. Do not refrigerate until ripe.
✅ Ready to use Yields gently to thumb pressure but springs back slightly. Dark green-black skin (Hass variety). This is the window — use today or refrigerate to pause ripening for up to 2 days.
❌ Overripe — past it Feels very soft or mushy, with no resistance. Brown or stringy flesh inside, possible off smell. Discard. Using overripe avocado produces a bitter, oxidised flavour in guacamole.
📅 Batch planning tip Buy 2–3 avocados at different stages of ripeness. One firm (ready in 3 days), one medium (ready tomorrow), one ripe (today). Rotate continuously so you always have one ready.

Avocado has near-zero glucose impact. Log a food test the first time you pair it with a new carb component (e.g. millet crackers or GF bread) to track that combination's response — not the avocado itself.

🧾 Ingredients — 2 servings
  • Essential
  • 2Ripe avocados⬜ zero ox
  • juice of 1Lemon (or lime)⬜ zero
  • 1–2 clovesGarlic, crushed (rest 5 min)🟢 low ox
  • ¼ tspSea salt⬜ zero
  • Recommended additions
  • handfulFresh coriander, chopped⬜ zero
  • ¼ smallRed onion, very finely diced🟢 low ox
  • Optional
  • ¼ tspGround cumin🟢 low ox
  • pinchCayenne or smoked paprika⬜ trace
Oxalate: Near-zero. Avocado is zero oxalate. Lemon juice is zero oxalate and adds urinary citrate. One of the safest whole foods in the library for kidney stone management.
👩‍🍳 Instructions
  1. 1
    Activate the garlic first
    Crush 1–2 garlic cloves and rest for 5 minutes. The lemon juice you'll add in step 4 can slow the alliinase reaction if garlic contacts it too early — let the allicin form in air first. Finely chop the coriander and dice the red onion during this window.
  2. 2
    Open and scoop the avocados
    Halve each avocado lengthwise and twist the halves apart. Tap the stone with the heel of a knife (it will grip the stone), twist gently, and lift it out. Scoop the flesh into a bowl using a large spoon — run it along the skin to get all of it. Discard the skin.
  3. 3
    Mash to your texture preference
    Use a fork. Chunky: mash loosely, leaving visible pieces — better texture for eating alongside chicken or fish. Smooth: mash thoroughly with the back of a spoon. Do not use a blender — it aerates the mixture and makes it brown faster.
  4. 4
    Add lemon, garlic, and seasonings
    Squeeze in the lemon juice immediately — this slows browning right away. Add the rested garlic, salt, coriander, red onion, cumin, and cayenne if using. Stir to combine. Taste: needs more salt? More lemon? Adjust now.
  5. 5
    Serve immediately or store correctly
    Best served right away. To store: press wrap directly onto the surface with no air gap — this is what slows browning, not the stone. Refrigerate up to 24 hours. Surface browning when you open it is harmless oxidation — scrape off the top layer, the bright green guacamole underneath is perfectly fine.
Mechanism summary: Avocado oleic acid (MUFA) → reduces LDL oxidation (directly relevant for APOE4) + hepatocyte insulin signalling (EVOO-equivalent fat for fatty liver / DP) · Potassium (~975mg per avocado) + magnesium → insulin sensitivity improvement, DP amplitude reduction · Soluble fibre (beta-glucan-type) → slows gastric emptying → blunts post-meal glucose · Garlic allicin (5-min pre-rest) → AMPK, glutathione induction (MTHFR) · Lemon juice → urinary citrate (kidney stone prevention) + prevents oxidative browning. Zero oxalate. No cooking required.

🍽 How to use guacamole in your meal plan

🍗 Chicken + zucchini lunch (menu default) The primary use on your meal plan. Guacamole alongside chicken breast strips and roasted zucchini — the MUFA fat from avocado slows gastric emptying and replaces any temptation to use a higher-saturated-fat sauce or dressing.
🐟 With golden paste salmon A spoonful of guacamole alongside the salmon adds a second MUFA source to the same meal and provides potassium to balance the sodium from the seasoning. The cool, creamy texture also contrasts the warm golden crust.
🥚 With shakshuka Serve a small dish of guacamole on the side. The fat slows absorption of the tomato lycopene (fat-soluble carotenoid), improves curcumin carry-through from the spice blend, and adds a third source of monounsaturated fat to the meal alongside the EVOO already in the sauce.
🍳 With eggs at any meal Eggs + avocado is a natural combination — egg yolk phosphatidylcholine (liver fat export) + avocado oleic acid (hepatocyte insulin signalling) address the same fatty liver pathway from different angles. Works for any egg preparation.
🥒 As a dip with vegetables Cucumber slices, celery sticks, or blanched broccoli florets as dippers — all low-oxalate, all zero glucose impact. This replaces any chip or cracker component with a snack that is entirely condition-compatible.
🍞 On GF bread or millet toast Spread on a slice of the Olive & Feta Cheese Bread or toasted millet slices. The fat from avocado slows the grain GI further. Monitor with a food test if combining with a bread component for the first time.

🔬 Why this is a targeted recipe, not a filler

  • APOE4 and monounsaturated fat: APOE4 carriers have a specific lipoprotein metabolism difference — their LDL particles are smaller, denser, and more prone to oxidation than in non-carriers. Oxidised LDL is the form that crosses the blood-brain barrier and contributes to neuroinflammation. Dietary monounsaturated fats (oleic acid, the primary fat in both EVOO and avocado) reduce LDL oxidation directly. This is why the meal plan specifies avocado and EVOO as fat sources rather than butter, coconut oil, or vegetable oils — it is a specific APOE4 dietary strategy, not a general health recommendation.
  • Potassium and Dawn Phenomenon: Potassium is a cofactor in the sodium-potassium ATPase pump, which regulates cellular glucose uptake. Low potassium is associated with impaired insulin secretion and higher fasting glucose — both of which worsen the Dawn Phenomenon. One whole avocado provides ~975mg potassium (roughly the same as two bananas, but with zero glucose impact). This makes avocado one of the most efficient potassium delivery mechanisms in the meal plan.
  • Soluble fibre and post-meal glucose: Avocado contains approximately 10g fibre per fruit, of which a significant portion is soluble (beta-glucan-type). Soluble fibre forms a gel in the digestive tract that physically slows the passage of food and blunts the absorption rate of any carbohydrates eaten alongside it. This is the same mechanism exploited by the overnight kefir fermentation in the porridge recipe — here, it happens passively from the avocado's natural composition.
  • Why coriander is recommended (not just optional): Fresh coriander (cilantro) contains quercetin and flavonols with documented anti-inflammatory activity. It is also a traditional heavy metal chelator in folk medicine — while the chelation evidence is modest, it contains chlorophyll and active compounds that support phase II liver detoxification, directly relevant for fatty liver and MTHFR. It is low-oxalate, flavour-complementary, and costs nothing to include. If you dislike coriander strongly (there is a well-documented genetic taste sensitivity), substitute fresh parsley — similar flavonol profile.
  • The browning myth — avocado stones do not work: The browning is enzymatic oxidation (polyphenol oxidase reacting with air). The stone only prevents browning in the tiny area it physically touches. The correct technique is removing all air contact — press wrap directly onto every part of the surface with no bubbles. Lemon juice (citric acid) also slows the reaction by lowering pH and chelating the copper ion that the enzyme requires. Both methods together (lemon juice + direct-contact wrap) are the correct storage approach.
