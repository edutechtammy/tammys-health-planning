📅 Week 4 Tasting Set

Bold & Savory EVOO Dressings

Three dressings with distinctly different flavour identities: an Asian-style ginger-lime (short shelf life due to fresh ginger), a Middle Eastern za'atar-lemon (standard shelf life, earthy and nutty), and a pomegranate-mint (standard shelf life, rich and slightly sweet). These work best on heartier greens and stronger-flavoured salads — they are not subtle. The za'atar and pomegranate dressings in particular are in a different flavour zone than the previous three weeks.

📏 Scale note: Ginger-Lime (#12) — 6 oz batch (fresh ginger, short shelf life). Za'atar-Lemon (#13) and Pomegranate-Mint (#14) — 12 oz batches (dried herbs only, standard shelf life).
Bottle label guide — this set
🟡 Orange label — Recipe 12 (Asian Ginger-Lime) · 3–5 days · 6 oz batch
🟢 Standard label — Recipes 13 & 14 · 2–3 weeks · 12 oz batch

All three are low GI at a 1-oz serving. Recipe 14 (Pomegranate-Mint) contains pomegranate molasses — negligible glucose impact at 1-oz serving size but noted here for transparency.

12 Asian Ginger-Lime Dressing
🟡 3–5 days · orange label · 6 oz batch
Ingredients — 6 oz batch
    Oil
  • 7½ tbspExtra-virgin olive oil
    • Acid & umami
  • 2½ tbspRice vinegar (unseasoned)
  • 1 tbspGF tamari (or coconut aminos)
  • 1 tbspFresh lime juice (about ½ lime)
    • Fresh aromatic
  • 1 tspFresh ginger, very finely grated
    • Seasoning
  • ¼ tspGarlic powder
  • pinchFine sea salt (taste first — tamari adds salt)
7.5 + 2.5 + 1 + 1 = 12 tbsp = 6 fl oz ✓ · Fresh ginger causes shelf-life limit — orange label, use within 3–5 days
Method
  1. 1
    Grate ginger
    Grate fresh ginger on the fine side of a box grater or use a microplane. 1 tsp is a small amount — measure it. More ginger is pungent and can overpower the dressing.
  2. 2
    Combine & shake
    Add all ingredients to jar. Taste the tamari before adding — if it's very salty, start with ¾ tbsp and adjust after shaking. Seal and shake 30 seconds.
  3. 3
    Fill & label
    Fill 6 × 1-oz silicone bottles. Orange/yellow label with batch date. Refrigerate. Use within 3–5 days. Shake each bottle before use — this one separates quickly.
What's working: Fresh ginger gingerols activate AMPK (same pathway as berberine — complementary, not redundant), provide prokinetic benefit for gastric emptying (smooths glucose absorption curve), and contribute anti-inflammatory shogaols. GF tamari provides glutamic acid (umami) without wheat. Rice vinegar is the mildest acid in the set — light, clean, with minimal assertiveness. The lime adds limonene and citrate. This dressing is completely different in flavor identity from all other 15 — it belongs on shredded cabbage salads, Asian-inspired slaws, over cold soba noodles (if using GF), over salmon. Celiac note: verify GF tamari label — most major brands are GF (San-J, Kikkoman GF) but check. Coconut aminos is a safe alternative with slightly less sodium. Oxalate: low per serving.
13 Za'atar-Lemon Dressing
✓ 2–3 weeks refrigerated
Ingredients — 12 oz batch
    Oil
  • 18 tbspExtra-virgin olive oil (= 1 cup + 2 tbsp)
    • Acid
  • 5 tbspFresh-squeezed lemon juice (about 2 lemons)
  • 1 tbspWater
    • Spice blend
  • 1½ tbspZa'atar blend (thyme, sumac, sesame, dried herbs)
  • ½ tspGarlic powder
  • ½ tspFine sea salt
  • ¼ tspBlack pepper
18 + 5 + 1 = 24 tbsp = 12 fl oz ✓ · Za'atar settles — shake each bottle firmly before every use · Standard green label
Method
  1. 1
    Combine
    Add all ingredients to jar. Za'atar is a dry blend — it won't fully dissolve but will suspend when shaken. Check your za'atar brand for sesame content if sesame allergy is a concern.
  2. 2
    Shake vigorously
    Seal and shake hard 45 seconds — dry herb blends need extra agitation. Shake each bottle firmly before squeezing, every use.
  3. 3
    Fill & label
    Fill 12 × 1-oz silicone bottles. Green standard label. Refrigerate.
What's working: Za'atar is typically: dried thyme (thymol, carvacrol — anti-inflammatory, antimicrobial), sumac (anthocyanins, tartaric acid — anti-inflammatory, astringent), sesame seeds (lignans, calcium, vitamin E), and dried oregano or marjoram. Together they create a distinctly Middle Eastern flavour profile that is completely different from the Italian and Greek dressings of the first two weeks. The lemon + za'atar combination is traditional in Lebanese and Israeli cooking — it's used as a dipping condiment for bread and as a vegetable marinade. Best on: romaine + cucumber + olives, roasted vegetable salads (especially cauliflower, eggplant), alongside hummus and pita. Oxalate: sesame is moderate-oxalate; at 1½ tbsp za'atar per batch, each 1-oz serving has a very small contribution — not a concern at normal serving sizes.
14 Pomegranate-Mint Dressing
✓ 2–3 weeks refrigerated
Ingredients — 12 oz batch
    Oil
  • 16 tbspExtra-virgin olive oil (= 1 cup)
    • Acid & sweet
  • 3 tbspPomegranate molasses
  • 3 tbspFresh-squeezed lemon juice
  • 2 tbspWater (to thin — pomegranate molasses is thick)
    • Herb & seasoning
  • 1 tspDried mint
  • ½ tspFine sea salt
  • ¼ tspBlack pepper
16 + 3 + 3 + 2 = 24 tbsp = 12 fl oz ✓ · Pomegranate molasses is thick — the 2 tbsp water prevents clogging the silicone bottle tip · Standard green label
3 tbsp pomegranate molasses in a 12-oz batch = ~1.5g natural sugar per 1-oz serving. Not a blood sugar concern at normal use. Would be worth monitoring if using more than 2 oz at a sitting on a day with significant carbohydrate load.
Method
  1. 1
    Warm the molasses
    Pomegranate molasses is very thick and will clump in cold oil. Briefly warm it in the measuring spoon by holding it under warm water for 10 seconds, or microwave 5 seconds in a small cup. This helps it incorporate.
  2. 2
    Combine & shake
    Add all ingredients to jar, including the water. Seal and shake vigorously 45 seconds — the molasses needs extended agitation. Taste and adjust: more lemon if too sweet, more salt if flat.
  3. 3
    Fill & label
    Fill 12 × 1-oz silicone bottles. Green standard label. Refrigerate. Shake each bottle firmly — the molasses settles more than a typical dressing.
What's working: Pomegranate molasses is concentrated pomegranate juice — very high in punicalagins and ellagic acid (powerful anti-inflammatory polyphenols with specific relevance to APOE4 cardiovascular protection and NAFLD). It is the most polyphenol-dense ingredient in the entire 16-recipe set, but also the sweetest. The balance here is: enough molasses for flavour and polyphenol dose, diluted enough that the sugar per serving is negligible at 1 oz. Dried mint provides rosmarinic acid and is significantly gentler on GI than raw garlic-based dressings. Best on: bitter greens (arugula, radicchio), roasted beet salads, grain bowls (if testing rice or quinoa), warm lentil salads. This is the richest and most complex-tasting dressing in the whole five-week set — it divides opinion but people who like it tend to love it. Oxalate: low per 1-oz serving.
